## Worklist execution model

A live route seeds an ordered list of `Change`/`Assertion` instructions (one per grounded candidate, per BD-32's scope --- multi-candidate fan-out and selection stay in the caller), then `runExecutor` drives them to fixpoint by priority: ground the first `ungrounded` instruction; else fire command-expansion on the frontmost `grounded` instruction, which either retires it into the output list or retires it having minted zero or more child instructions pushed to the front (a generator). A `transferMembership` retires as itself; a relational `Change` retires as its whole chain (below). No command-expansion is a generator today; the mechanism stays for the plans that expand into further instructions (containment's, slice 3c of [`AGENT.commandAttemptPipeline.planning.md`](../../../../../../../taskPlanning/lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/AGENT.commandAttemptPipeline.planning.md)). A move's boundary dissolves are not the executor's to find: Expansion adds them to the command attempt as facilitating actions, and the membership dry run seeds them grounded, ahead of the move (BD-28's order).

Two invariants worth knowing before touching this, both enforced by `runExecutor`'s priority order rather than any per-instruction bookkeeping: every seeded instruction is grounded before any command-expansion fires, so a generator never sees a half-grounded sibling; and a command-expansion's minted children are causally downstream (they didn't exist when their parent fired) and already grounded, so pushing them to the front is always sound. No phase widens an operand set: an object's hosted contents live in its own shard and travel with it. The per-primitive dispatch table is documented where it's implemented, not restated here.

## Relational edges: edge, chain, legs

A relation exists at three levels, and only the last is kernel steps:

- **The edge** is the plan's relational `Change` grounded by substitution (`PlanStep<GroundedReferent>`): subject, target, kind and label. **It has no `host`.** Hosts belong to legs, and Expansion picks them when it builds the chain. A hosting kind (`On`, `In`, `PartOf`) puts its edge in the target's own graph by definition, so the kind implies the host. A host the player names would constrain the route rather than name one host; it comes back as an optional constraint when something produces one. With no placeholder `host`, substitution grounds every referent of a relational `Change`, so it seeds through `seedFromGroundedSteps` like any grounded step.
- **The chain** is one route realizing the edge: every leg with its host, and every port it crosses, as `RelationalChainStep[]` (`findRelationalChain.ts`). Establish and dissolve share the type. Establish builds a chain (`findShardBoundary`, then `buildCrossingLegs`); dissolve finds the existing one (`findRelationalChain`). A relation inside one graph is a chain with one leg, not a separate case. No `chainId` or `edgeId` is minted yet.
- **Legs** are the chain lowered to kernel steps by `lowerRelationalChain` (`buildCrossingLegs.ts`): a port becomes `addCrossingPort` or `removeCrossingPort`, and an edge becomes `establishRelation` or `dissolveRelation`, in the chain's own order. A port step and a leg that references it commute, because every check of a port-address endpoint resolves it to its owner.

**The worklist's vocabulary is the grounded `Change`.** A grounded relational `Change` is itself the instruction (`GroundedRelationalChange`, `executorTypes.ts`). Command-expansion dispatches on its `primitive` and retires the chain as one output, an `ExecutorRelationalChain`. That output stays a value through selection; the route lowers only the chosen candidate. The relational route has no construction-time Validation: a candidate whose Expansion found a chain is legal, and the kernel rechecks every leg at commit. The producer drops a candidate whose subject and target are the same object. There is no assertion standing in for the edge, and no side channel for ports. `containedBy` stays a grounded assertion: it is a real check, with `negate`, so the assertion slot remains for real checks (its command-expansion is still an error stub). A hosting-kind expansion branch is slice 3c's; `expandSameHost` errors on hosting kinds today (CD2h).

**Grounding is substitution against a complete assignment (AP-10).** `groundChange(change, assignment)` takes a `Change` to a `Change<GroundedReferent>`: pure, total substitution, no live KR access of its own. `ReferentAssignment` (`plan/planStep.ts`, beside `Referent`) has two namespaces with different lifetimes --- `spans`, keyed by `stableRefKey`, decided once when identities are selected and carried with the candidate; `derived`, keyed structurally (`derivedReferentKey`: `'actingCharacter'`, `` `currentHost(actingCharacter)` ``), built fresh by whoever holds a live snapshot (membership's dry run against its sandbox; positions, once AP-9 lands). A referent already carrying a `groundedId` passes through either namespace; a referent with no entry in the namespace it needs is a construction bug and throws, not a runtime outcome a caller branches on --- the product over identity candidates is the producer's (`enumerateIdentityAssignments`), formed before Grounding, so by the time `groundChange` runs every key an assignment is asked for should already be there. Typing each id for its slot is lowering's job (`groundedInstructionStep`, `executor.ts`), shared by seeding and Grounding. The executor's own grounding phase (an `'ungrounded'` worklist tag, `groundInstruction` firing mid-loop) retired with this: every seed is grounded before `runExecutor` ever sees it, through `seedFromGroundedSteps`, the only seeder.

| [`groundAssertion.ts`](groundAssertion.ts) | Grounding for an `Assertion` (`containedBy`), the same substitution `groundChange.ts` applies |
| [`expandSameHost.ts`](expandSameHost.ts) | BD-16's placement Expansion for a relational `Change` (named for the `sameHost` check it grew out of): finds the edge's chain --- an already-shared host gives a one-leg chain, a shard boundary gives crossing legs plus ports --- or declines; it never relocates an endpoint. `establishRelation` and `dissolveRelation` call different discovery primitives, matching the different question each asks --- establish calls `findShardBoundary.ts`/`buildCrossingLegs.ts` (containment ancestry); dissolve calls `findRelationalChain.ts` (existing relational edges/ports), which also subsumes the old portless case correctly instead of via ancestry coincidence. Returns the chain as a value; it does not lower it. Takes an `env` slice (`getMembershipContainers`/`getGraph`/`getCurrentHost`, all optional) rather than a bare `getMembershipContainers` callback, to carry what `findRelationalChain` needs. Both establish and dissolve now land live end to end, through the shared `executeEstablishEdgeChain` commit path. |
| [`findRelationalChain.ts`](findRelationalChain.ts) | The dissolve-side discovery primitive, `findShardBoundary.ts`'s mirror image: walks existing relational edges/ports outward from a subject (not containment ancestry) to find the chain a prior `establishRelation` already built, so a `dissolveRelation` can remove it. `found` / `notFound` (decline) / `ambiguous` (decline, more than one qualifying chain). Wired into `expandSameHost.ts`'s `dissolveRelation` branch. |
| [`buildCrossingLegs.ts`](buildCrossingLegs.ts) | The establish-side chain builder (`buildCrossingLegs`: `findShardBoundary.ts`'s `'crossed'` verdict into a chain, minting a port at each crossed host) and the one lowerer for both operations (`lowerRelationalChain`). The lowerer has no hop cap; a dissolve chain only names already-stored ids. Also used outside this folder for chain-aware removal (`repairAdministrativeChainDissolve.ts`, `clearCoyoteGameImprovisationObjects.ts`). |

## Follow-up: one grounded step vocabulary (no owning plan)

Grounded ids are needed in only two places: Expansion, which reads a real graph, and the kernel's step vocabulary. Everywhere upstream holds `plan/planStep.ts`'s `PlanStep`, whose referents are grounded or not by what is known (`groundedId`). The relational half is done: a relational edge stays a `PlanStep<GroundedReferent>` until Expansion, and its kernel steps are lowered from its chain. Two grounded shapes still sit beside `PlanStep<GroundedReferent>` for the rest: `transferMembership`'s lowered `TransferMembershipStep` (inside `ExecutorParsePlanStep`), and `GroundedAssertion`. They could collapse too. Nothing is blocked on it, and no plan owns it; pick it up when a change next touches both.
