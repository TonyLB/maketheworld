# Design-variant tuning

**Durability: Keep** --- an instrument, not a task plan. Disposes together with [`AGENT.designVariant.performance.md`](AGENT.designVariant.performance.md).

Candidate and recently-implemented changes to [`AGENT.designVariant.md`](AGENT.designVariant.md), motivated by the patterns accumulating in the [performance tally](AGENT.designVariant.performance.md).

**What this file is for, beyond holding proposals:** a live proposal biases attention while observations are being recorded. *Would this improvement have made a difference here? Would it have been the wrong instruction?* That question turns each observation into a test case run against the proposal set --- **which is the only way an implemented rule can lose.** Without it a rule in force accumulates nothing but confirming evidence, which is how [`AGENT.designVariant.md`](AGENT.designVariant.md)'s confidence table filled with clauses nobody has re-examined.

## Rules

**Cap: 10 rows, shared** between candidates and implemented-under-evaluation. Implementing a proposal does **not** free its slot. That is deliberate: shared pressure forces an evaluation to conclude rather than linger, and adding an eleventh row means evicting something. **The cost is real and is being watched** --- several long evaluations could squeeze the candidate pipeline, which is a signal to revisit the cap, not to quietly exceed it.

**Restate, never link --- at row grain.** A proposal names its motivating pattern **in its own text**, in a clause. Neither file may cite a **row** in the other: no `TP-n` in the tally, no tally line or pattern anchor here. File-level pointers between the two are fine and expected; it is **row-level** coupling that would make eviction expensive, and cheap eviction is the entire mechanism.

**Every proposal carries a counterfactual test, written before implementation:** what observation would show it working, and what observation would show it was the wrong instruction. **A proposal that cannot state its own falsifier is not ready to implement.**

**Three statuses, and eviction costs something different for each.** *Candidate* --- proposed, nothing built; evicting it is free. *[Prototype](AGENT.designVariant.md#graduation-tiers-when-a-decision-licenses-code)* --- built somewhere bounded to get evidence, with **no clause yet in `AGENT.designVariant.md`**; carries a dependency tag naming its rollback set, per the variant's own tier rules. *Implemented* --- a clause is live in `AGENT.designVariant.md`.

**Evicting an *implemented* row obliges one edit** to [that file's confidence table](AGENT.designVariant.md#where-each-clause-came-from), recording what the evaluation actually showed. Without it a rule ages out of evaluation while staying live in the instructions with no record of how it did --- the state that table is in today. **Evicting a *prototype* row obliges a disposition instead:** promote it to a clause, or unwind it and say so. A prototype that simply ages out leaves a mechanism running that nobody ever graded.

**Review both caps whenever a proposal is evicted as settled.** That is the moment the system has demonstrably improved, so it is the honest moment to act on the expected trajectory: **tally cap down** as noise falls, **tuning cap up** as quick wins deplete and proposals get more numerous and more lightly held.

**IDs are for conversation only.** Nothing outside these two files may cite a `TP-n`. Monotonic, not reused.
