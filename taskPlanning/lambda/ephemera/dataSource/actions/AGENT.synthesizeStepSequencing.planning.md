# Object manipulation: generalized Synthesize step sequencing (iteration 8)

**Status:** Named, not started, deliberately. Split out 2026-07-21 while investigating BD-16's `sameHost` rebuild --- caught before it became scope creep on that work: shipping `sameHost` didn't require this generalization (an ad hoc, route-specific Expansion check was enough, matching the existing precedent `expandTransferMembership.ts` already set on the membership route), but it surfaced a real, distinct gap worth naming on its own. See [`AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md`](AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md) for the full iteration ladder.

Task-planning conventions: [`taskPlanning/AGENT.md`](../../../../AGENT.md).

## Purpose

Design and (eventually) build one real, generic Synthesize-side executor that walks an ordered sequence of ungrounded steps --- `Change`s and `Assertion`s alternating as needed --- performing Grounding, Expansion (inserting repair steps when an `Assertion` is violated), and Validation per step, so that a repair (a `TransferMembershipStep`, say) becomes a first-class step in a real grounded sequence rather than a bolted-on field on some other step's result. The executor's own shape is decided (see "Expansion execution model" below, BD-29/BD-30): Expansion splits into operand-expansion (set-closure fixpoint) and command-expansion (step emission over the closed set), and the whole ground -> operand-expand -> command-expand cycle runs as a phase-stratified worklist over progress-tagged instructions, not as recursion.

This is **not** new design from nothing: the shape already exists, unwired, in two places:

- `plan/compileUngroundedPlan.ts`'s `compileRelationalUngroundedPlan` already returns a real `UngroundedPlanStep[]` of exactly `[sameHostAssertion, change]` --- written, tested in isolation, never called by any live route.
- `sandboxPlan.ts`'s `evaluateSandboxPlan` already folds a step array through state generically, and is the one place (`selectIdentityPlanTuple.ts`, membership dry-run only) where an Expansion-inserted repair (`expandTransferMembership.ts`'s `dissolveSteps`) gets appended into one real array before evaluation --- but it never reaches the live commit path.

Two shipped live examples now exist to design the generalization against, where previously there were none: BD-13 (`computeCarryClosure`, cascading fixpoint entirely within Expansion, no Plan involvement needed) and BD-16 (`expandSameHost`, a genuine Assertion-shaped precondition with a real repair step, currently bolted on as `transferFromHostId` rather than appended to a sequence). Per this project's own "expand as concrete cases demand" discipline (the same principle that kept `Assertion`'s union to one member until BD-16 needed a second), this is the point at which designing the general shape stops being premature.

**A third worked example, found 2026-07-21 (BD-28): relation dissolution during carry is itself a live case of exactly this incoherence, not just a motivating analogy.** Verified concretely: `classifyInteractionUnderTransfer`/`boundaryEdgeOutcomes` (`positions/positionGraph/expandValidate/interactionUnderTransfer.ts`) gets independently re-derived in **two** places against what should be the same graph state --- once in `enrich/objectManipulation/synthesize/expandTransferMembership.ts` (live-wired via `selectIdentityPlanTuple.ts`'s dry-run, producing real grounded `DissolveRelationStep[]`), and again inside `positions/positionGraph/expandValidate/applyTransferSet.ts` at actual commit time. The explicit `DissolveRelationStep[]` from the first computation never reaches the live apply --- it only feeds the dry-run's own legality re-check (`evaluateSandboxPlan`) and is then discarded; only the closure's `objectIds` set crosses into `applyObjectSetTransfer.ts`, where dissolution actually happens purely as an implicit side effect of `EphemeraPositionGraph.removeObject`'s automatic edge-stripping. Net effect: no player-facing fact is ever streamed when a carried object's relation to something left behind is severed (unlike BD-16's compound apply, which does stream `Object Relation Changed`). See BD-28 below.

## Expansion execution model (BD-29 / BD-30 rationale)

The two-kinds-of-expansion decomposition (BD-29) and the worklist driver (BD-30) are the same design seen at two altitudes: BD-29 is *what the phases are*, BD-30 is *how they run*. Recording the reasoning here rather than in the table cells, since it's the load-bearing part.

**Why two phases, ordered.** Operand-expansion (the carry fixpoint) and command-expansion (step emission) have a real data dependency, not just a stylistic split: you cannot know which edges cross the operand set's boundary until the set is *closed*, because closure itself manufactures boundary edges. `cup On tray` + `cup Under chandelier`, player takes the tray: cup-chandelier is not a boundary edge at rest --- it only becomes one once operand-expansion absorbs the cup and leaves the chandelier behind. So command-expansion (which severs crossing edges) must wait for operand-expansion's fixpoint. This is exactly the cut today's `boundaryEdgeOutcomes` fails to make --- it classifies every edge as absorb-or-dissolve in one pass and then re-sweeps, which is why the classification ends up computed twice (BD-28).

**Why a worklist, not recursion.** A command-expansion can spawn a step that itself needs the full ground -> operand-expand -> command-expand cycle (e.g. `sameHost` inserts a `transferMembership`, and if that object carries a saucer, the inserted transfer needs its own carry-closure). That looks like recursion, but modelling it as a phase-stratified worklist over progress-tagged instructions (BD-30) is strictly better here, for two reasons that are not cosmetic:

- **The shared expansion environment is first-class.** The "compute the closure once and share it between the `transferMembership` and the `IsolatedFromRelations` over the same group" requirement (the whole point of killing BD-28's duplication) wants a *global* environment the loop reads and writes, not a closure-map threaded down and back up recursive frames. Draining all operand-expansion before any command-expansion means the shared set is saturated once, then every consumer reads the identical settled set.
- **The reified state is suspendable.** An explicit worklist + per-item phase tags can be paused at a phase boundary and resumed --- an addressable resume point, which is precisely the shape iteration 4 (BD-18, backtrack channel) will want. Not designed in here, but the formulation doesn't fight it, where recursion (state buried in the call stack) would.

**Soundness invariants the eventual implementation must preserve:**

- **Monotone per-item progress.** Every instruction only advances forward through its tag states (`ungrounded -> grounded -> operandExpanded -> retired`); nothing regresses. The frontier grows (command-expansion pushes new items), but each individual item is on a one-way ratchet.
- **Command-expansion is the sole producer.** Grounding and operand-expansion transform in place; only command-expansion mints new instructions. That is *why* draining the two in-place phases before firing a producer is the correct scheduling policy --- every consumer sees fully-resolved operands and a fully-populated shared set.
- **New items are causally downstream.** A child instruction did not exist when its parent command-expanded, so it can never retroactively invalidate anything already emitted; "push to front, drop back to operand-expansion" is consistent, not just convenient. (Within a single intent, sets never retroactively grow --- command-expansion emits steps *over* a final set, it does not enlarge an existing one.)
- **Termination rides on a settled-groups ledger.** The worklist analog of carry-closure's existing visited-set: before operand-expanding a grounded item, check whether its base object-group is already settled in the shared environment and reuse if so. That single check does double duty --- it is both the dedup (BD-28) and the guard that stops a pathological graph (isolating A forces moving C which carries A) from looping. "operand-expand as necessary" is carrying exactly this lookup.

## Recommended order

Use `[ ]` for pending and `[X]` for complete.

- [ ] **Do not begin implementation until someone actually schedules this iteration** --- both live routes work correctly today; direction is decided (BD-27a/b/c) but scoping/building is not yet underway. This row exists to hold that discipline visibly.
- [ ] Read `compileRelationalUngroundedPlan`/`compileMembershipUngroundedPlan` and `evaluateSandboxPlan` in full and write down concretely what reshaping each needs (BD-27b) to become the live step vocabulary and executor, rather than treating them as drop-in reusable.
- [ ] Design the general executor against BD-13, BD-16, and BD-28 as its three required worked examples --- BD-13/BD-16 must be expressible without changing their own Expansion logic; BD-28 is the one case expected to *change* (its current dry-run/commit-time duplication is what the design should eliminate). The executor shape (two-phase Expansion, phase-stratified worklist) is decided per BD-29/BD-30 and the "Expansion execution model" section --- implementation realizes it, does not re-litigate it, unless a fourth worked example breaks an invariant listed there.
- [ ] Design the progress-tagged instruction representation (BD-30): the `ungrounded | grounded | operandExpanded` tag states, the shared expansion environment ("common referent" / settled-groups ledger), and the worklist driver's priority ordering, with the four soundness invariants preserved.
- [ ] Design `IsolatedFromRelations` (BD-28) concretely: the ungrounded primitive shape, the closed-set `Referent` output shape (distinct from `groundReferent`'s disjunctive candidates), and its lowering into per-edge `DissolveRelationStep`s --- as a command-expansion over an operand-expansion output (BD-29).
- [ ] Resolve BD-28's sequencing wrinkle: decide and document the order of explicit `DissolveRelationStep` application vs. `removeObject`'s implicit edge-stripping within one transact, so the two never conflict (a throw on an already-removed edge) or double-apply.
- [ ] Design the general kernel-layer apply over `EphemeraPositionGraph` primitives (BD-27c) that a grounded step sequence bottoms out in, superseding the route-specific compound kernels' bespoke transact-building (their shared MultiKeyUpdate/legality-reuse pattern is the starting point, not the target shape) --- this is also where BD-28's `DissolveRelationStep`s actually get applied and their facts streamed.
