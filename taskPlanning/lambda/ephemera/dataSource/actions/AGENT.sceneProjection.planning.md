## Goal

Every LLM rung still to be built (the orchestration loop in the [iteration ladder's proposal](AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md#proposal-deterministic-first-then-an-orchestration-loop-2026-10-25), the deferred adjudication tier's judge, the synthesis fallback) has to reason about what the actor can reach. The `ludicCache` holds that, but in a shape that is very bad for semantic reasoning. Membership is node-list position, not an edge. A hosting kind sits on a cover entry, not on a relation. Where a thing sits is a walk over `fromHostPresences`. One tie is three edges (`Custom`, the `SecuredTo` it justifies, and the `Justifies` between them). Handing the model JSON makes it reconstruct those facts before it can reason, and switches register between ids and meaning on every line.

**The reader the scene is designed for is the orchestration loop** (user, 2026-11-02). There, nothing is identified yet: the loop identifies things itself (Identify is one of its tools), reasoning over the scene and naming things by handle. The adjudication judge (CA-1's prose) is a secondary consumer: it reads an attempt the deterministic pipeline already grounded, so it adds a referents section the loop doesn't have. Design choices here answer to the loop's needs first.

This plan builds an **output-only projection** from the actor's cache into text an LLM reads as a scene:

```text
Contents of the Lab:
- [pole1] Pole (a tarred mooring post)
- [rope1] Rope (a coil of thick hemp rope), tied to [pole1]
```

It has two layers:

- **A scene model:** structured data built from the actor's cache. It holds the described things, their handles, where each is placed, and the relations between them as phrases. Beside it sits a **handle table** mapping each handle to an id, which is never shown to the model.
- **A text renderer** over the scene model. Several candidate renderers are built first and compared by a hands-on Bedrock probe (Slice 3); the losers are deleted and one renderer ships.

"Output-only" means the model is never parsed back into the cache. The handle table is how a later reader's output names things again (see SP-1).

**Payoff, at the observable output:** in dev, a player types `/test scene` and receives, as an OOC message, the rendered scene of their own actor's cache. That covers the room's contents, nested, with relations in words, and their held inventory. The integration test ends at that published message, not at the scene model. A second, smaller payoff is that `CommandAttempt.renderProse`'s room-context section is filled by the renderer instead of its string-list stub (Slice 6).

## Candidate formats

**Decided (SP-7, user, 2026-11-03): D ships. Reopened the same day (user) against `fans`, below.** The other candidates and their goldens are deleted; they stay described below as the record of what was compared and why. Code for the two live candidates: [`renderSceneTree.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/sceneProse/renderSceneTree.ts) (D) and [`renderSceneFans.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/sceneProse/renderSceneFans.ts).

**Why compare (user, 2026-11-02).** English is much closer than JSON to the register the reader reasons in, but it is bad at nesting. More precisely, it is bad at branching. A right-branching chain reads easily ("the cup on the saucer on the table in the tavern"). A fan of siblings inside another fan does not: after a subtree, prose has no clean way to say which level it has returned to. Indentation is the usual patch. Whether an LLM reads indentation reliably is a hypothesis, not a fact. Finding a line's parent from indentation means comparing leading whitespace, which is a counting task over tokens that split inconsistently. Matching an identical handle string earlier in the context is what attention does best. So the candidates differ in which cue carries the structure: whitespace, a lexical parent clause, or neither.

The worked scene used below: a tavern (with gloss) as the room; a table holding a saucer with a cup on it, and a candle; the actor holding a hammer. The candle is there so every format has to climb back out of a subtree.

**A. Indented tree.** Hosting kinds are carried by indentation alone.

```text
The Tavern: A low-beamed common room, smoky from the hearth.
Contents of the Tavern:
- [table1] Table
  - [saucer1] Saucer
    - [cup1] Cup
  - [candle1] Candle
You are holding:
- [hammer1] Hammer
```

**D. Indented tree with a parent clause.** Every nested line names its parent by handle, with the hosting kind in words. The indentation is a visual aid; the clause carries the meaning.

```text
- [table1] Table, in the Tavern
  - [saucer1] Saucer, on [table1]
    - [cup1] Cup, on [saucer1]
  - [candle1] Candle, on [table1]
```

**Blocks.** Shape-aware, with no indentation. The renderer analyses the physical forest and uses English's two native shapes: a host with children is a block ("On [table1]: ..."); a child whose contents form a chain (one thing per level) stays inline as a phrase read downward from the child; a child that branches (two or more things) is promoted to its own block and marked "(see below)" in its parent's list. The deepest structure is one list plus inline phrases, and every jump between blocks is a handle match. The number of blocks grows with how much the scene branches, not with its depth.

```text
In the Tavern: [table1] Table.
On [table1]: [saucer1] Saucer, with [cup1] Cup on it; [candle1] Candle.
You are holding: [hammer1] Hammer.

(with a tray holding a teapot and a jug also on the table)
On [table1]: [saucer1] Saucer, with [cup1] Cup on it; [candle1] Candle; [tray1] Tray (see below).
On [tray1]: [teapot1] Teapot; [jug1] Jug.
```

Known costs of Blocks: the render's shape flips when the scene changes (a spoon put on the saucer promotes it to a block), so across a loop's turns one object can be rendered two ways, though its handle is stable; and each hosting kind needs chain wording ("with X on it", "with X in it", "with X as a part").

**blocksD: Blocks' grouping with D's lines** (user, 2026-11-02, after the first probe batches; see SP-7). The reading: Blocks' strength is *associating* the right things (a block gathers a host's members), and its weakness is *disassociating* them on structural questions (an inline chain read as the outer host's member; a line with no local placement cue). So every item is a D-style row naming its parent, with no inline chains. A member with exactly one thing below it, and nothing below that (a one-deep chain), has that thing hoisted into the same block as its own row, its clause naming its real parent; anything more (a fan, or a chain two or more deep) is marked `(see below)` and gets its own block. Members of different hosting kinds get a block each, as in Blocks; a relation never promotes, since no row is inline. With tea in the cup:

```text
In the Tavern:
- [table1] Table, in the Tavern (see below)
On [table1]:
- [saucer1] Saucer, on [table1] (see below)
- [candle1] Candle, on [table1]
On [saucer1]:
- [cup1] Cup, on [saucer1]
- [tea1] Tea, in [cup1]
```

Its known risk: a hoisted row sits under a header that is not its host (`On [saucer1]:` heads the tea), so the clause must beat the header; fixture `sideboard` asks exactly that (`directContents` of the saucer and the mug). The room is a list block like the others, not Blocks' one-line room. Code: [`renderSceneBlocksD.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/sceneProse/renderSceneBlocksD.ts).

**blocksDLabelled: every header true of every row under it** (user, 2026-11-02, after the `sideboard` batches; see SP-7). The `sideboard` batches showed blocksD's known risk come true: the model reads a header as membership over a hoisted row's own clause. So nothing is hoisted, and each header says what kind of block it is (the user's proposal: label the shapes; the wording states the reading rule rather than naming the shape). A **fan block** holds a host's direct members, `Directly on [x]:` or `Directly in [x]:` (other hosting kinds keep their headers, which are already direct). A member with anything below it is marked `(see below)` and gets its own block, in pre-order. A **single-file run** at least two deep (one thing, one thing on or in that, and so on) is a **layers block** of D-style rows, `[x] and what is on or in it, in layers:`; where the run ends in a fan, that thing is marked `(see below)` and gets a fan block. A one-deep chain is a fan block of one row. On `sideboard`:

```text
Directly on [sideboard1]:
- [candle1] Candle, on [sideboard1]
- [saucer1] Saucer, on [sideboard1] (see below)
- [mug1] Mug, on [sideboard1] (see below)
- [tray1] Tray, on [sideboard1] (see below)
- [box1] Box, on [sideboard1] (see below)
- [lamp1] Lamp, on [sideboard1]
[saucer1] and what is on or in it, in layers:
- [cup1] Cup, on [saucer1]
- [tea1] Tea, in [cup1]
Directly in [mug1]:
- [coffee1] Coffee, in [mug1]
```

Known gap: a join (fixture `join`) sits in the block of its last host's binding, so `Directly on [table-b1]:` heads a fruit bowl that is on the tablecloth; the scene model places a join there for every format, and no probe batch has asked about it yet. Code (deleted with SP-7's first decision): `renderSceneBlocksDLabelled.ts`, at commit `30ed8e4cc`.

**`fans`: grouping with no shape analysis** (user, 2026-11-03, reopening SP-7). blocksDLabelled without its layers block: every host with members gets one block per hosting kind (`Directly on [x]:`, `Directly in [x]:`, `Parts of [x]:`), each row a D-style row naming its parent, and a member with anything below it marked `(see below)`, its block following in pre-order. A chain is a block per link, so nothing depends on a thing's shape, depth is stated by every row's clause, and every header is true of every row under it. This is the "fan-blocks-only variant" SP-7 named after the blocksDLabelled batch and held; it was never built before the decision. Members of another hosting kind get their block after the first kind's blocks and all their sub-blocks (shape test), which may put `Parts of [x]:` far from `Directly on [x]:`. On `sideboard`:

```text
Directly on [sideboard1]:
- [candle1] Candle, on [sideboard1]
- [saucer1] Saucer, on [sideboard1] (see below)
- [mug1] Mug, on [sideboard1] (see below)
...
Directly on [saucer1]:
- [cup1] Cup, on [saucer1] (see below)
Directly in [cup1]:
- [tea1] Tea, in [cup1]
Directly in [mug1]:
- [coffee1] Coffee, in [mug1]
```

**`flatD`: D with no indentation** (user, 2026-11-03). D's lines in D's pre-order, every line flush left. The user's worry: an indented tree may bring a YAML or tree-manipulation register with it, so a model reasons about nodes and children instead of the things in the room. D's clauses already state every parent, so the indent carries no fact of its own; dropping it removes the cue and changes nothing else. If flatD beats D on the questions where answers drift from the physical facts, the indent costs something; if they tie, it is decoration. Unlike the rejected flat-chain form below, each line names only its parent. Goldens are D's with each line's leading whitespace stripped (`sceneFlatDGoldens`, derived, since that is flatD's whole spec). Free check alongside it: scan earlier batches' reasoning for tree vocabulary ("child", "nested", "node"). On `sideboard`:

```text
- [saucer1] Saucer, on [sideboard1]
- [cup1] Cup, on [saucer1]
- [tea1] Tea, in [cup1]
- [mug1] Mug, on [sideboard1]
```

## Frame and answer-format findings (Slice 5, 2026-11-03)

The evidence behind the frame and answer-format changes (the renders' first comparison, the direction audit, each frame edit, the answer-format table, the renders under the current frame, the blocker ladder) is in [the discussion record](AGENT.sceneProjection.discussion.planning.md#frame-and-answer-format-findings-moved-from-the-plan-2026-11-03). **Where it stands (2026-11-03):** the frame now has a grounding with a blocker ladder (simplest steps, then freeing one blocker, then puzzle-solver paths) and a precedence line by register (the scene wins on what is here and how it is placed, the world on what follows from it), a `MOVING` legend in two registers (results, then the records' edits), and an `answerFormats` axis with `abstain` (default) and `twoRegisters`. The ladder cut over-moves to 0 of 6 (from 2 of 4). The renders, compared before those changes, showed no separation inside the spread of about 2 points. Direction errors came from symmetric or reused wording, not from a model belief. The latest findings follow.

**Two registers: `MOVING` rewrite and the `twoRegisters` answer format (2026-11-03, user's design).** Grades are the assistant's, `teaTable`, `registers`, Nova Micro, temperature 0.1, 2 to 3 runs per cell.
- **`MOVING` as two registers.** A first rewrite that added a part-out sentence and a fish "tip out" example made results worse (3 of 3 pours fixated on the teapot; one pick-up lifted the saucer and table); both came out. The rewrite kept: the world paragraph speaks of results, not actions ("different actions with the same result are the same change"), with no licence that anything is possible; the records paragraph defines the move and "one move for each thing whose own place changed, not for the things carried along". Which of the example, "what it holds" or naming "pouring" caused the fixation was not separated.

## Questions this iteration should surface

- **Scope.** Is the whole actor's cache the right context, or a part scoped to the command (the things Identify's pools touched, plus their neighbours)? This iteration renders everything. - **Size.** Token count of a rendered scene for the Coyote rooms at their placement cap.
