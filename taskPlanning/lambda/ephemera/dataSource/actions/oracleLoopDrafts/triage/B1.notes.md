**The bar is no added latency** (the user's prior, at the checkpoint): a split passes if the slower of its two calls is no slower than the single call, and is then judged on accuracy. I drifted to how much a split saves, and first wrote that it saves nothing, so it fails; that was the wrong test as well as an overreach. **What it says (mine; corrected 2026-11-26, the user's questions):** I first wrote that a split's wall time is its grounding call's, and so no faster. That outran the evidence: no grounding call has been run, and my 4 ms a token came from comparing the two prompts' medians, which differ by about 900 input tokens as well. Fitted within each prompt instead, where only the output varies:
- B1: about 2.7 ms an output token, and 376 ms at none.
- Draft 5: about 3.1 ms a token, and 473 ms at none, leaving out T-1's 1,338 ms at 48 tokens. With it, the fit is meaningless (0.4 ms a token).

So, roughly: a floor of about 210 ms, about 0.1 ms an input token, and about 3 ms an output token. **A split's wall time is the slower of its two calls**, and what a grounding call costs depends on a prompt not yet written. With its block alone (`says`, `improvise`, perhaps `match`: 25 to 40 tokens) and draft 5's examples, that model puts it near 0.55 s; with draft 5's `Needs` line kept, near 0.75 s.
