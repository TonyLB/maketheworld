## Why

Batch 1's task named four kinds: the things a step touches, what they are like, where they are, and what the earlier steps left. That asked for an inventory, and got one ([1.notes.md](1.notes.md#results-2026-11-13-batch-1)). The size never appeared on the size cells, and h5 never said what the tip does to the loose saucer and cup. "A few words, naming things by their handles" turned the JSON into a list of handles.

## The change

One change to the Task's first sentences (mine). Everything else is word for word draft 1.
- **Was:** "list what someone judging whether it can happen as written should be considering: the things it touches, what they are like, where they are, and what the steps before it have left. Each item is a few words, naming things by their handles."
- **Now:** "list what should be considered in judging whether it can happen as written: facts about the things it involves, and how they relate to each other and to what the step asks of them, in the state the steps before it leave. Each item is a short statement, using handles for things. These items are for someone else to weigh, so state each one as it is."
- **Relations, unnamed:** "how they relate to each other and to what the step asks of them" points at the size against the cup and the loose things on a tipped table, without naming either, since naming them would teach to these cells.
- **Statements:** the item form is aimed at SC-P1.
- **"For someone else to weigh"** is a positive phrasing against verdicts (SC-P3). It is part of the same change, to be watched, not counted on.

**Against the rubric:**
- **SC-P2:**
  - the size is present on 2 of 3 size cells explicitly, and on 1 implied (batch 1: 0 of 3);
  - h7's carry-forward is present;
  - OC-20 and OC-6 have what is on the table and where the cup is;
  - **still missing:** what is on [table1] on OC-1, the tip's effect on the loose saucer and cup on h5, and the carried cup on h10.

  The relational register took, but it reaches stated relations, not knock-ons.
- **SC-P1: fixed,** statements in 11 of 11.
- **SC-P3: worse, now inside the items.** Verdict words ("cannot", "impossible", "not feasible", "difficult", "immovable") are in 10 of 11 replies, mostly in the block. "For someone else to weigh, so state each one as it is" did not hold: a statement can carry a conclusion, and a bare handle couldn't.
- **The securing:** written as an effect that stops movement every time ("immovable", "cannot be moved independently", "constraint"), except in h5's prose.
- **Invented:** "[table1] glued to the floor" on OC-11, OC-18 and OC-3, one more than batch 1's two. The render has only "[teapot1] is glued to [table1]". The empty hands are used as a reason in 7 replies, twice backwards ("holding nothing, so they cannot … tip").
- **Headings:** a few bold labels (OC-3, OC-1, OC-9), with no fill pattern.
- **Kept from batch 1:** the player's ability stayed out (only "physical strength" on OC-18 and "capability" on OC-1, mildly); h7's line read rightly; OC-9's weight.

**What it shows:**
- **The relational wording works for what is stated:** the size, where the cup is, the slide line carried forward. It doesn't reach what a step does to things the step doesn't state. That is the consequences gap, which in the decomposition is the imagination's to fill, and the judge's to notice as missing.
- **Statements let verdicts in.** Each item tends to end with what the fact means for the step ("…, which makes it immovable"). For a list meant to be judged later, that is the main defect now.
- **The glue's misreading is the most stable error across both pieces:** the securing stops movement, and spreads to the floor.
