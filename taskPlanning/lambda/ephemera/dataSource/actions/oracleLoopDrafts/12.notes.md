## Why

Since the generic `Rules` step (L-27), the pry reads as moving the teapot apart while it's glued. Nothing on the page says the pry is an attack on the glue, so the oracle has to infer it, and mostly doesn't. Going the other way, OC-6's misses since draft 7 claimed "the teapot is first unglued" with nothing on the page to support it (7 of 7).

The design (the user's, 2026-11-09, a synthesis of declaring intent and judging it):
- **The candidate declares intent.** A step that sets out to void a rule says so, by the rule's handle. The engine knows its intent, since it was asked to find a way past the rule. For a player's method, it's what the request says.
- **The oracle judges success, and when.** The timing is across steps, not within one (the user's correction, 2026-11-09):
  - **Within the voiding step,** its motion can be how the fact ends. To take the teapot by brute force (a later case, not yet in the harness) is to tear it free, and the glue failing is the same event.
  - **Across steps,** a later voiding doesn't cover an earlier motion. Pouring and then prying the glue loose with a screwdriver is blocked: the pour happened while the glue held.

  Whether the method works at all is OC-19's skill question (JP-14), which this leaves to the oracle.
- **With no declaration, there's no claim to judge.** The rule is in force for every step, as in draft 11, and an unstated "changes first" has nothing to stand on.

This is the inherited premise JP-14 (a candidate whose step acts on the fact behind a rule says so, the shape v45's `Revises` had), with the oracle judging the claim. It is general over kinds of rule: every rule has facts behind it, and a lock or a lit candle can be voided the same way.

### Two judgements, in order

The user's shape (2026-11-10):
1. **Attempts.** For each step marked as setting out to void a rule, does the play-by-play show it voiding the rule? This is judged once per attempt, and it's where the skill question lives.
2. **Rules.** Each rule is checked against only the steps it is in force for: every step before the one that voids it, or every step if none does. Within that window, the check is draft 11's generic one.

The voiding step is outside its rule's window, so its own motion isn't checked against the rule it voids: the brute-force case. A failed attempt voids nothing, so its rule stays in force for every step, including the attempt.

### The vocabulary

The user's (2026-11-10), from a list I offered. Each term has one sense, and none is used in any other way in the instructions:
- **An action voids a rule** when it makes the fact behind the rule no longer true. Ungluing the teapot voids `teapot-secured-to-table`.
- **A rule is in force** until an action voids it.
- **A rule in force blocks an action** that moves its things in a way the fact doesn't allow.

The words they replace, and why:
- **"breaks":** the user's first proposal for the voiding side. In plain English, breaking a rule means violating it, which is the opposite sense. The generated rule text used it in that opposite sense ("A method breaks it if it moves…").
- **"ends":** mine, then withdrawn. Steps end and the plan ends, which collides with the window's own wording.
- **"holds", "stands":** both are physical in this prompt (`held by`, a teapot standing on a table).
- **"keeps":** no longer needed. The verdict is "blocks" or "allows".

"Voids" is also a word a player could use in a command. The pairing is chosen so that Micro can tell voiding a rule apart from violating it there too (the user's point).

## The harness change

A Prototype change. The built judge is untouched.

1. **`calibration/oracleLoop/oracleInputs.ts`:** `CandidateStep` gains `voids?: readonly string[]`, the handles of the rules the step sets out to void. Its comment says that it is the generator's declared intent, judged by the oracle, and a guess about what the engine will emit.
2. **`oracleCases.ts`:** OC-19's pry step carries `voids: ['teapot-secured-to-table']`. No other case in scope sets out to void a rule. OC-30's tip moves the two together; OC-7's wipe and OC-20's chain don't attack a rule.
3. **`renderOracleInputs.ts`:** a step with `voids` adds an indented line after its action and any record line:
   ```
   - pry [teapot1] off [table1] with [hammer1].
     sets out to void: teapot-secured-to-table
   ```
   The label is a new framing key, `voids`, defaulting to `sets out to void`.
4. **A third rule wording, `inForce`,** in `dataSource/actions/sceneProse/sceneRules.ts` (`JudgeRulesWording` and `JUDGE_RULES_WORDINGS`), worded in the new vocabulary. `violation` stays as it is, so earlier batches can be reproduced. Both are probe-only: the built judge uses `v45`. The wording is mine:
   > [teapot1] is glued to [table1]. While this rule is in force, it blocks an action that moves [teapot1] and [table1] relative to each other more than "glued to" allows, and it allows an action that moves them together. An action that makes it no longer true that [teapot1] is glued to [table1] voids this rule.

   For a thing secured to something not shown, the same sentence names "something not shown here" in place of the other thing, with no clause about moving together.
5. **Tests:**

6. **Docs:**

   - **The `JudgeRulesWording` doc comment:** the third wording.

   > 4. Rules: Consult only the rules that name the things under the candidate's "Plan:" and under Changes. **What it shows:**
- **`Attempts` doesn't key on the line.** It writes a verdict for the glue in all 12 replies: "voids" 11 times and "doesn't void" once. It never writes "none". On the three cases with no "sets out to void:" line, it says "voids" 8 of 9 times, including OC-1, where its own play-by-play says the teapot stays glued. The step was read as "for each rule", the shape `Rules` has, and the "For each action … with a line" filter was skipped. The fallback, "Write "none" if…", comes last. My reading, a guess: Micro filled the step's template once per rule, and the condition never acted as a gate.
- **`Rules` ignores the window, 12 of 12.** It never writes "allows" because a rule is in force for no action, even in the 11 replies where `Attempts` said "voids". On OC-19, it writes "blocks" for the voiding step 3 of 3, which is the misreading the rubric named. Its lines are draft 11's motion check, run over the whole plan. Since the window is put as a condition over actions, it asks for a computation that Micro doesn't do.
- **`Tests` doesn't read `Rules` on OC-19.** OC-19/1 and /3 answer "conflict: no" against `Rules`' "blocks", and get the right end result. They seem to answer from `Attempts`' "voids" instead. The pry gets credited, but by a route the draft didn't lay out.
- **The vocabulary leaks once.** OC-30/2/1 writes "voids" for a violation ("would move the teapot and table relative to each other more than "glued to" allows"). OC-30/2/3 has the glue give way, which makes the tip into a voiding. Both of the "voids" sentences in the rule's own text are there to copy from.
- **OC-6 found no new support for "first unglued".** None of the three replies claims it. Two of them write "voids" with no line, though, and `Rules` then blocks anyway, so the unanchored voiding didn't change the verdict here. On OC-1, the "voids" with no line came with a "blocks", which is the false refusal.
