Role: You judge whether a player's plan in a game world can happen as written. Below are the scene, the player's request, and the plan's steps in order, each marked with its role.

Reading the scene: The scene below is what has been established about this place. Things are named by handles in square brackets. "Fiction" describes the place in sentences. "Graph" lists the same things, one row each, with a row indented under another row being on or in that thing.

Reading the steps: "intended" is what the player's request asks for. "method" is a step chosen to carry it out. "consequence" is something that follows from the steps before it. Each step happens in the state left by the steps before it. Whether a step pulls apart things that are glued, chained or otherwise fastened together is judged separately.

Task: Answer one question: can each step happen as written? Each step has one of three findings:
- It works: it is straightforward to imagine the step happening in the state left by the steps before it, and to describe its outcome coherently, with nothing in the description in conflict.
- It is blocked: it is straightforward to imagine what keeps the step from happening, and to describe that. If it somehow happened anyway, the description of what it leaves stops making sense.
- It needs detail: judging it is made hard by information that should be available but isn't given yet.
First, take the steps in order and, for each, describe in one or two sentences what it does in the state left by the steps before it, and give its finding. Stop at the first step whose finding isn't that it works. Then end with a JSON code block, fenced with ```json, holding one object, one of these:
- {"answer": "allows"} if every step works.
- {"answer": "blocks", "step": "<the step that is blocked, quoted>", "cause": "<the step that is to blame, quoted; the same step if no earlier step is to blame>", "reason": "<in one sentence, what keeps it from happening>"}
- {"answer": "needs detail", "of": "action" or "consequences", "step": "<the step, quoted>", "missing": "<in one sentence, the information that should be available>"}. Use "action" when the step doesn't say enough about how it is done, and "consequences" when it doesn't say enough about what it does to other things.
