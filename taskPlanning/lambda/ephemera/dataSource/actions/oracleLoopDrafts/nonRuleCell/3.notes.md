# Non-rule cell draft 3: notes and reading rubric

For [the non-rule cell plan](../../AGENT.nonRuleCell.planning.md#recommended-order)'s step 3, drafts in isolation: library entries **NR-L5** (the non-branching walk) and **NR-L6** (consequences on their step) in [`iterations.md`](iterations.md), for **NR-P6**. The design is the plan's [Draft 3's proposal](../../AGENT.nonRuleCell.planning.md#draft-3s-proposal-the-non-branching-walk-with-consequences-on-their-step), accepted by the user 2026-11-13.

## Why

Batch 2 showed that framing the outcomes positively doesn't move the answer while the stop is the only structured endpoint: every "works" finding was written as an absence ("nothing prevents …"), never as an outcome ([2.notes.md](2.notes.md#results-2026-11-13-batch-2)). Draft 3 removes the branch, so every step ends at a structured slot, and success has one of its own (`establishes`).

## The change

A contract change as well as a prompt change, so it departs from "one change per draft" as a new baseline for the walk (as the imagination's draft 11 did).
- **The walk (NR-L5):** every step, through to the last, with no stop. Each step's finding, and the state it leaves: a working step its outcome, a blocked step what it would establish if it happened anyway, a needs-detail step what is certain from it as written.
- **The block (NR-L5):** `steps`, one entry per step in order, each quoting its step, with `finding` (`works`, `blocked`, `needs detail`) and `establishes` on all three; `cause` and `reason` on `blocked`, `of` and `missing` on `needs detail`. Code applies the first-non-works rule, so the canonical answer is as before.
- **Consequences on their step (NR-L6):** the render nests consequence lines under their step; "Reading the steps" says a consequence line is part of its step. `cause` can quote a line (`blocks 2/1c1`).
- **Carried from draft 2:** the outcome framing, word for word, and the removal of draft 1's hunt checklist, which was part of the same change (NR-L4) and cost nothing on the size cells. Mine: draft 2 wasn't kept because it moved nothing, not because it did harm, and the framing is what `establishes` gives a slot to.
- **Carried from draft 1:** Role, Reading the scene, the roles' words, the securing sentence.
- **The cap:** `maxTokens: 1024` on the piece (the probe's answered pieces gained the field), as the imagination asks use.

**Unchanged:** every cell's inputs but h7's, so their inputs hashes match batches 1 and 2. h7 is now two steps, the tip carrying "[saucer1] and [cup1] slide off [table1] onto the floor", expected `blocks 2/1c1`.

## Rubric (written before the batch)

- **NR-P6, the main measure:** `allows` on the guards (OC-1, OC-2, OC-20, OC-6) and h10, against 2 of 15 in batches 1 and 2. Across all replies, the count of `works` findings in the blocks.
- **`establishes` on "works":** does it describe an outcome ("the table is in hand, the teapot and cup with it"), or still an absence ("nothing prevents it")? This is what the slot is for.
- **`establishes` on "blocked":** does it state the impossible outcome ("the table is inside the cup"), as the user's framing has it, or restate the reason?
- **The size cells must hold:** OC-11, OC-18 and OC-3 `blocks 1/1` with the size. Losing them would mean the walk without a stop has tipped into allowing everything.
- **Invented reasons:** the glue and chain (NR-P1), preconditions (NR-P2), "not feasible" (NR-P3), over-asked action detail (NR-P4), each counted. Also the glue's physics (batch 2's second strand): does a "works" `establishes` on OC-1 have the teapot moving with the table?
- **The walk:**
  - does every reply give an entry per step, and does the prose go on past a non-works step?
  - h10: the state carried forward (NR-P5): after picking up the saucer, is the cup with it?
  - h5: `needs detail of consequences 1`, or a block on the tip, or `works` (assuming the cup stays)? What does step 2 get after it?
  - h7: does the pour's `cause` quote the consequence line? Does any reply ask for consequences of the tip although the line gives them (the evidence for NR-L6)? Does any block the slide line itself, which isn't an entry?
- **Form:** each malformed reason, especially entry counts that don't match (a consequence line given its own entry, or steps merged), quotes out of order, and a cause after its step. Replies cut off at 1024 tokens.
- **OC-9:** what the trestle gets, and what `establishes` says, as background for the skill-or-chance outcome.

**Against the rubric:**
- **NR-P6:** moved on the guards, 6 of 12 `allows` against 2 of 12, all on OC-2 and OC-6. Where the securing is in the scene (OC-1, OC-20, h10), it still blocks. `works` appears in 12 entries against none in batch 2's blocks (batch 2 had no slot for it).
- **`establishes` on "works":** describes an outcome every time ("The player now holds the saucer"). The slot did what it was for: no "works" is written as an absence.
- **`establishes` on "blocked":** mostly not the impossible outcome. It is "would defy the physical constraints" or the unchanged state ("The table remains on the floor"), in 16 of 21 blocked entries. The exceptions are OC-20's "the chain dangling" and h7's copy of the consequence line. And in the prose, the replies add a "State left" of their own for a blocked step, which is the unchanged state, against the draft's "a blocked step leaves what it would establish": Micro won't carry a counterfactual forward as the state.
- **The size cells:** held as blocks, 9 of 9 in substance; the size as the reason in 5, the glue in 4 (OC-18 all three, OC-11 r1).
- **Invented reasons:** the glue or chain as a blocker in 13 replies (OC-1, OC-20, OC-18, OC-11 r1, h10 r1 and r2, h5 r3 on the pour); the tip "not feasible" in 7 (NR-P3); action detail asked of the pour in 6 (NR-P4).
- **The walk:** every reply gives an entry per step and goes on past the first non-works step, 33 of 33. The non-branching walk holds in form.
- **h7:** no reply blames the consequence line, and no reply asks for the tip's consequences. The tip is blocked before the line matters.
- **Form:** every quote of a step matched. **All 12 malformed are `cause` on a blocked single-step cell,** quoting the reason or a scene fact in place of the step: where the step blames itself, the reply fills `cause` with what it sees as the cause. Draft 1's "the step that is to blame" became draft 3's "the step or consequence line that is to blame", and no batch 1 or 2 reply did this. No reply was cut off.

**What it shows:**
- **The structure works where the scene gives no excuse:** OC-6 went from 0 of 3 to 3 of 3, and every "works" now states an outcome. That fits the user's diagnosis: with a structured endpoint for success, success is reported.
- **`cause` on a self-blamed step is a form fault, not a reasoning one.** It hides 12 answers, 9 of them right in substance. The next draft's cheapest change.
- **Two problems remain and are content, not structure:** the securing used as a blocker (NR-P1, with the glue's physics misread), and the tip judged unwise or infeasible (NR-P3), which now blocks all three hops before their later steps matter.
- **A counterfactual isn't carried forward as state.** The walk goes on, but from the unchanged state, so a blocked step's "anyway" has no effect on the steps after it.
- **Kept** (mine, agreed by the user at the batch 3 checkpoint, 2026-11-13): draft 3 is the base, and draft 4 follows from it. Its graded tally is lower only through the form fault, and in substance it is the first draft to move the guards.
