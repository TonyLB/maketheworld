# Non-rule cell draft 2: notes and reading rubric

For [the non-rule cell plan](../../AGENT.nonRuleCell.planning.md#recommended-order)'s step 3, drafts in isolation: library entry **NR-L4** in [`iterations.md`](iterations.md), for **NR-P6**. Batch 1 showed a reluctance to allow ([1.notes.md](1.notes.md#batch-1-overall)), and NR-P1 to NR-P5 are its symptoms.

## Why

The diagnosis is the user's, 2026-11-13, refined in discussion. In draft 1:
- the stop was the only structured endpoint;
- the walk was defined by its halting condition;
- `allows` was a leftover ("if every step can happen").

So the model read the task as a search for the stop, and when nothing real was there it found something: glue misattributed to the table, an invented precondition, a tip called "not feasible".

It isn't token economy. The single-step cells have no early exit and were blocked 9 of 9, and the multi-step replies walked every step anyway.

## The change

One change: the three outcomes are framed by what they indicate, not by what they exclude (the user's framing, 2026-11-13; my wording).
- **It works:** it is straightforward to imagine the step happening in the state, and to describe its outcome coherently, with nothing in conflict.
- **It is blocked:** it is straightforward to imagine what keeps it from happening, and to describe that. If it happened anyway, the description of what it leaves stops making sense.
- **It needs detail:** judging it is made hard by information that should be available but isn't given yet.

What follows from the framing:
- **The walk:** each step's finding is given in the walk, with "describe … what it does … and give its finding" in place of "say in a sentence each what the step does". It allows one or two sentences, since an outcome is now described.
- **The block's wording:**
  - `allows` is now "if every step works";
  - `reason` is "what keeps it from happening";
  - `missing` is "the information that should be available".
- **Removed (mine, flagged):** draft 1's closing list in "Reading the steps", "so here judge everything else that could stop a step: size, weight, reach, position, and what the earlier steps have done". It is a checklist of what to hunt for, the negative framing this change replaces. The securing sentence stays without it.

**Unchanged:**
- the stop at the first step that isn't a success. Removing it is the parked non-branching walk, NR-L5;
- the block's shapes and fields, so the grade is the same;
- the cells and their inputs, so the inputs hashes match batch 1's.

## Rubric (written before the batch)

- **NR-P6, the main measure:** how many replies answer `allows`, against batch 1's 2 of 33. On the guards (OC-1, OC-2, OC-20, OC-6) and h10, count `allows`. For each reply that still blocks or asks, note whether its walk gives a "works" finding to any step before the stop.
- **Invented reasons:** count the blocks and requests whose reason the scene doesn't support: the glue misattributed, preconditions, "not feasible in this context", detail nothing in the cell needs. Each is classed as NR-P1 to NR-P5.
- **The size cells must hold:** OC-11, OC-18 and OC-3 `blocks 1/1`, with the size as the reason. Losing them would mean the positive framing has tipped into allowing everything. Note whether dropping the checklist changed how size is reasoned.
- **The walk:** do passing steps now get an outcome described ("now the saucer and cup are in hand")? Is the state carried forward on h10 (NR-P5)?
- **h5:** a request of consequences on step 1, or still a block on the tip?
- **h7:** does any reply still ask about step 1's consequences although step 2 states them? That is evidence for NR-L6. Also, does any reach step 3 and blame step 2?
- **OC-9:** the trestle under the "works" framing: heavy but imaginable, or blocked?
- **Form:** malformed replies and their reasons; replies cut off at 512 tokens, now that each step's outcome is described.

**Against the rubric:**
- **NR-P6:** unchanged. The framing was adopted in form: every step gets a "Finding:" line, in the draft's words. But **no "works" finding describes an outcome.** Each says "there is nothing in the scene that prevents …", success stated as an absence, so the reply never establishes a state. The walk now goes on past the stop and then says "we stop here".
- **Invented reasons:** as many as batch 1. The glue appears in 20 of 33 replies, misplaced onto the floor or the table's surface in 11.
- **The size cells:** held, 9 of 9, so dropping the checklist cost nothing.
- **NR-P5 (state carried forward):** h10's replies all pass step 1, and none says the cup now leaves with the saucer.
- **h7:** no reply asks for consequences; all invent a blocker at step 1.
- **Form:** 0 malformed, every quote exact, and no reply cut off.

**What it shows:**
- **The framing alone doesn't move the answer,** as the user expected. The stop is still the only structured endpoint, and "works" comes out as a bare absence.
- **The securing has a second strand, apart from the bias:** what glue means physically. OC-1's correct readings conclude that glue keeps the table from being picked up, and h10 r3 that a glued teapot "would fall off or break" when tipped. With no Rules section, the piece has the fact ("glued to") and not its consequence ("moves with what it is secured to"), which the imagination's `rules: 'consequence'` wording gives.
