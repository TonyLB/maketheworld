The instructions are [`1.txt`](1.txt), hash `c42dbd09` (833 characters) when sent without the trailing newline. **Status: written 2026-11-10; batch 1 run 2026-11-10 ([results](#results-2026-11-10-batch-1)), the baseline; batch 2, the same draft with `OC-30/2/slides` added ([results](#batch-2-2026-11-10-with-oc-302slides)).** Draft 1 is the baseline that later drafts are compared against, so it has no keep-or-drop bar.

## What the draft is

Mine, all of it, and short, as step 3 asks.
- **`Role`:** one sentence on the job, and one on what follows the instructions: the rule, its two things, and the actions with their lines.
- **`Reading the scene`:** a cut-down version of v45's module. It keeps the handles, `Fiction` as sentences, and `Graph` as rows with indenting. It drops the open-world and fiction-not-simulation sentences, since this cell judges given motions and not what is established.
- **`Task`:**
  - The one question from the plan. "Move [x] and [y]" becomes "the rule's two things", since the instructions hold nothing variable and come before the cell.
  - The reasoning is one or two sentences, and the verdict comes last (Micro decides, then justifies).
  - The answer is `Answer: blocks` or `Answer: allows`, read by `pieceAnswer`.
- **Left out on purpose:**
  - there are no examples, since words get echoed;
  - there is no "moving together is not moving relative to each other". That is the enforcing rubric's own clause, and saying it again in the `Task` would be L-15's territory, a later draft.

## Rubric (written before the batch)

The tally grades each answer. I read only the wrong and malformed replies. For each one, record:
- **(a) The lines or the rule:** does the reasoning read the action's motion lines, or does it copy the rule's own clause ("more than "glued to" allows") as its reason (L-29)?
- **(b) Both move, read as apart:** on `OC-1` and `OC-30/2/*`, does it take each thing moving as moving relative to each other (PR-9, PR-11)? `OC-30/2/together` ("tilts", "tilts") is the bare form of PR-11.
- **(c) The scene over the lines:** does it draw on sub-scene text that the lines don't say, such as the Fiction's "glued to" sentence (the sub-scene's risk)? If (c) recurs, it triggers `RC-L1` (`show: 'none'`).
- **(d) The case over the input:** on `OC-6/stays`, does it answer against what pouring would need and not what the lines say (the premise that a piece is graded given its inputs)?
- **(e) Form:** there is no `Answer:` line, or its word is outside the set.

Also record any reply that writes "voids", "in force" or "breaks". None of these words is on the page.

**The wrong reply, OC-1/2 (blocks), against the rubric:**

**The malformed replies, read for form only:** each is one paragraph ending "… allows. Answer: allows". Five of the six are on the OC-30/2 cells. Draft 1 asks for "a last line", and Micro joined it onto the line before.

**The vocabulary:** no reply wrote "voids", "in force" or "breaks". "Violates" appears 4 times, in its plain sense.

**What it shows:**
- **Relative motion reads on the cell.** OC-30/2 is `allows` in substance 6 of 6, including the bare "tilts"/"tilts" form of PR-11. In the one prompt, OC-30/2's rule was 1 of 3. OC-6/tilted, OC-19/glued and OC-20 block 9 of 9. Taking the judgment out of the one prompt carried PR-11 (pending the form), and most of PR-9.
- **PR-9 is left, OC-1 wrong 1 of 3.** The OC-1 replies reason from the action's words, not its lines, and the one wrong reply turns on the rule's own clause.
- **A piece is graded given its inputs:** OC-6/stays answers `allows` from its lines in substance 3 of 3. Two replies say "both remain in their original positions", taken from the lines. One argues that pouring needs no movement, which is wrong about the case, though the answer is right given the lines.
- **The verdict-line form is the largest fault,** 6 of 21, and it is a matter of form, not of judgment.
- **An open question, not a fault:** OC-30/2/tipped's replies explain the together-motion by the glue ("since the teapot is glued to the table, they move together as a single unit"), which is a sound account of the fiction and agrees with the lines (user, 2026-11-10). What it doesn't show is that the lines were read. The cells whose lines separate the two (OC-6/tilted, OC-19/glued) were read and blocked 6 of 6, so the glue-as-reason has shown up only where the lines agree with it. The untested case is an action that reads as together-motion with lines that say apart, so a new cell, `OC-30/2/slides` (expected `blocks`), runs in the next batch.
