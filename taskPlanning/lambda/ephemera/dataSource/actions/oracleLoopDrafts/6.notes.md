## Why

"Move" means three things to Micro in draft 4, with nothing to tell them apart (the user's question, 2026-11-09):
- **(A) the candidate's listed steps:** the `Moves:` heading, step 1, "the things under Moves";
- **(B) a recorded change of what contains a thing:** the dry run's line, "[tea2] moves from [teapot1] into [cup1]";
- **(C) physical motion relative to a frame:** `Relations and frames`, and the rule's "moves [teapot1] and [table1] together".

Draft 5's "for each thing that moves" was answered in sense (A), with the things on `Moves:` lines. That was a fair reading, which I had graded as an error.

The steps are also two kinds under one heading:
- **A change step** is a recorded outcome with no action behind it (OC-1, OC-6).
- **A words step** is an action with no recorded outcome (OC-19, OC-30/2).

The user's correction (2026-11-09): a recorded change always has a counterpart in the fiction, something that puts the saucer in your hands, while an action ("wave the flag") need not change the record. So every step is an action, and some actions carry the record change they make. That is Slice 2's sketch (`says` required, `change?` optional), which my Slice 3 departure 2 had turned into an either-or.
