> Each entry maps each of the things the action moves, or that moves as a result of it, by its handle, to a line saying what is done to that thing or what follows for it.

- **What it replaces:**
  - **The narration** had "what each of the things … has to do physically for the actions to happen", which was read as what the plan needs (PB-L8): preconditions, things held still for the pour, rules cleared.
  - **The block** had "whose relations change, or that move relative to the thing they are on or in". That settled each thing's motion from its relations, and the glue is a relation: PR-1 came back on OC-6 (PB-L11's reading).
- **Why consequences** (user, 2026-11-10):
  - **Blocking stays out.** Whether a rule blocks is the rule cell's judgment. The play-by-play uses a rule only for what it causes. On OC-30/2, the glue is why the teapot tips with the table while the saucer slides.
  - **Bad consequences are wanted.** An unwanted consequence (the saucer sliding out from under the cup) is for a later stage to judge against the request, and to send to Improve for preparatory or mitigating actions. So the play-by-play reports consequences neutrally, the bad ones included: "whether or not the plan needs it".
- **Kept from draft 2:**
  - the silence contract: a line only for things the action moves or that move as a result;
  - its check;
  - the rest of the prompt, `sceneRules` off. The Fiction still says "glued to" and "chained to".
- **Risk (mine):** "slides or falls" are examples, and words get echoed. Watch for slides or falls on cells where nothing should slide (OC-1, OC-6, OC-19, OC-20).
