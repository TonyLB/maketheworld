## What the draft is

**A test of the iterative engine's expander, not a whole-scene draft** (PB-L18). The hypothesis (user, 2026-11-11):
- The whole-scene play-by-play fails because it is asked every question at once. With "provide everything" and no direction, the reply goes looking for its own direction and makes spurious connections, like a saucer held still because the teapot is glued.
- An iterative engine asks something narrower: the current action, expanded "with a particular eye for how [teapot1] moves relative to [table1]". That is much easier to answer. More loops of a process we can build beat fewer loops of one that eludes us.
- So the order may reverse: the iterative engine first, and a general play-by-play later, if at all.

**Built reduced** (user, 2026-11-11). A plain focus sentence added to draft 5 would leave the kitchen-sink asks competing with it. Then a failure would be only weak evidence against the hypothesis. The reduced draft makes a failure tell us something. It changes two things at once, the focus and the cuts, so a success can't be credited to one of them. That split changes no decision: the expander would be reduced anyway.

**Against draft 5:**
- **Role:** the plan is worked out one action at a time; fill in how the action would really happen, with a particular eye for how [A] moves relative to [B].
- **Reading the scene, Relations and frames, and the two examples:** unchanged. Cutting the frames section too would make three changes (mine).
- **Task:** describe the action happening, briefly, with the same eye. The block keeps its shape. Its entry maps [A] and [B] to a line each.
- **Cut:** "what each of the things … has to do physically for the actions to happen" and "what else follows … whether or not the plan needs it".
- **Unchanged:** `rules: 'none'`, the cells, the check. The check passes silent things, and other listed things may still get lines.
- **The focus is the pair, not the rule** (mine). The Fiction still says "glued to" and "chained to". The draft says "how [A] moves", not "whether", and keeps "describe the action happening", draft 5's premise.
