## Why

The examples' prose isn't reasoning. It is the answer written twice, as bullet lines and then as JSON. Across batches 44 to 49 the two copies agree on steps and consequences in 43 of the 92 replies that have both, though some of that is my strict parser. The prose also comes from history rather than a choice. In the narration era (up to draft 16) it was the thinking and the JSON its extraction. Draft 17 (PB-L27) shrank it to a summary list of the sub-steps, and draft 24 (PB-L34) added the step-record line to that list. Straight-to-JSON has never been run as a draft.

Replies that wrote the JSON with nothing before it did well: OC-1b in batches 44, 46 and 47, 9 of 9 at the bar.

The experiment (the user, 2026-11-20): run without the prose, and let the gaps it shows decide what preparatory reasoning comes back, as thinking rather than recap.

## What changed from draft 24b (the wording mine)

- **The examples:** every reasoning line is removed ("- the step, as written/revised: …", the sub-step lines, the "- consequence:" lines). Each example is its `Step: …` line, then its JSON block. The JSON is unchanged.
  - Two pieces of explanation went with the prose: the rope's "it already says how [rope1] is held", and the coat's line showing its revision.
- **The Task:** "Reply with one JSON code block, fenced with ```json", followed by the fields in order:
  - `says` (as written or revised to name its things in brackets) and `intent`;
  - the carry sentence;
  - `steps` when the step needs breaking down, with each field;
  - the step alone when it already states the detail.
  - The sentences about the describing (first give the step, list the smaller steps, "say so") go.
- **Unchanged:** the Role, the examples' inputs and JSON, the render.

**Gone with the prose:** the path written as the step (3 to 0), the OC-19 intent leak (2 to 0), OC-1b's parent intent (1 to 0), and the echo (already 0). Each of these had been made in the prose and copied into the JSON.

**The gaps, by the decision each got wrong:**
1. **How many motions the detail needs** (OC-20 3 of 3, OC-1 r3). The detail asked for names two parts, "how [table1] is held and lifted", and the reply gives the grip only, with the lift folded into its consequence. Batch 49's prose listed the grip and the lift as separate lines first, 3 of 3.
2. **What the step's items claim** (the concession cell 3 of 3). The unchecked "cannot be lifted" is answered as the step's own consequence, so it is taken as settled. The prose didn't hold this either (batch 49 1 of 3), but it sometimes reasoned to a method.
3. **Which handles the step uses** (OC-6/command 0 of 3). With the prose and without, the command's words aren't matched to the scene. That has been 0 of 12 on (c) since batch 44, apart from batch 48.
4. **How the thing is ordinarily worked** (OC-19 r1 and r2: the hammer gripped by the part that goes under). This is new. Batch 49's prose placed the hammer first, then worked it.
- **Whether the step is already the answer** isn't a gap: OC-1b is 3 of 3.
- **Which step is asked about** isn't a gap either: no reply restates another step, and no path is copied.

**Verdict (mine, for review):**
- **The prose did two things.** It copied its own errors into the JSON, and that's gone. It also laid out the motions before the JSON, which held OC-20 and OC-19's sequence, and that went with it.
- **So the thinking worth bringing back is not a recap of the record.** It's decisions 1 to 4:
  - the parts the detail asks for;
  - the standing of the step's items (an unchecked claim weighed, not taken);
  - the command's words matched to the scene's handles;
  - each part of a tool and where it goes.
- **A draft for each is the next step.** The order is the user's call.
