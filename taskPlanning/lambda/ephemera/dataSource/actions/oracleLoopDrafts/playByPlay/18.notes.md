## What changed from draft 17

**The form only.** Batch 32 failed 11 of 12 on one cause: `changes` was written as an object (PB-P8). Every block merged once it was rewrapped, and the content was at parity. The user's direction (2026-11-19) is to ask the model for what only it can provide, not to have it reproduce syntactic sugar.

**The code** (the contract; deploy needed):
- **The reply is one object:** `{"steps": [...]}` or `{"atomic": true}`.
- **`replyChange` in `playByPlay.ts` reads it as one change.** Code supplies the `add`/`mark` verb, the one-entry list, and `to`.
- **The list is gone.** Every valid reply was one entry, since the contract refuses an empty list, an expansion with a mark, and a second expansion.
- **The verb is gone.** The presence of `"steps"` already says which shape the reply is. Batch 32's nested shape, `{"add": {"steps": [...]}}`, came from the `add`/`steps` pairing.
- **The refusals:**
  - a key other than `steps` and `atomic` (so `changes`, `to` and `thing` are refused);
  - both keys, or neither;
  - `atomic` other than `true`.

  The merge's own refusals (one sub-step, an entry key, a blank intent) arrive without their `change 1:` prefix.
- **`consequences` is unchanged.** It keeps `{"changes": [...]}`, since its replies hold several entries.
- **The render is unchanged,** so the inputs hashes are as in batch 32.

**The ask** (the fenced block is the user's; the wording is mine):
- **The examples' blocks are fenced with ```json and written in the new form,** in place of the unfenced `Changes:` line. The atomic example's block is `{"atomic": true}`.
  - That line was my mistake. I had borrowed `consequences`' draft 3 reason ("so the reply's one fenced block is its own"). That worry never showed up: all 12 replies in batch 32 fenced their block.
  - The line also broke step 3's own rule that the examples match the Task.
- **The Task's form sentence:** "end with a JSON code block, fenced with ```json, as in the examples: an object whose "steps" lists the sub-steps in order, each with "says" … and "intent" …. When the step is one motion, the object is {"atomic": true}."
