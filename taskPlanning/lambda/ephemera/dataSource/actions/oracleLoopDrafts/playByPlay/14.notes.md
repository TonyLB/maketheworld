## What the draft is

**Draft 13 with the help-or-hinder examples** (PB-L25, the user, 2026-11-11; the lamp's wording mine). Two examples' problem lines change together, as one idea:
- **The rug's problem:** "The crate is on the rug, and may be too heavy to let the rug be pulled out" becomes "The crate is on the rug, and its weight may hinder rather than help pulling the rug out from under it".
- **The lamp's problem line:** "Problems: none. The lamp is bolted to the crate, and goes with it" becomes "Problems: none. The lamp is bolted to the crate, which helps rather than hinders: it goes with the crate".

Everything else is draft 13's, "each fact established" included.

**Why** (the user): the examples give Micro a question it can answer of each fact: does it help or hinder the method?
- On OC-1, the glue helps the teapot come up with the table, so it is not a problem.
- On OC-6, it hinders lifting the teapot.
- On OC-20, the chain hinders lifting the table.

Mishaps aren't established facts, so the question has nothing to work on for them. The rug's old "may be too heavy" was itself a mishap, shaped like the weight flags.

**The cost (mine):** the lamp is an analogy for OC-1, a secured thing on the thing lifted. A gain on OC-1 is partly the analogy's, not only the general test's.
