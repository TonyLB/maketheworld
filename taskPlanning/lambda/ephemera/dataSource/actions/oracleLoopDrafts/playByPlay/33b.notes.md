**Status:** written 2026-11-21; batches 59 and 59b run the same day ([results](#results-2026-11-21-batches-59-and-59b)).

## Why

- **The user's design for the arm:** the instructions say how to reason, and the examples start from reasoning and show how it informs the JSON, rather than from an abstraction of the input (33a's one-line inputs).
- **The plan-entry copy (mine):** in batch 58, 21 of 48 replies opened with a line in the examples' input form ("Step: …, with the intent pour-tea-cup.intent: …"), and in 10 the path reached the JSON's `intent`. With no input lines in the examples, that form has no source in the prompt.
- **Two cases batch 58 never produced** ([33a's verdict](33a.notes.md#results-2026-11-21-batches-58-and-58b)): a step line that revises (OC-6/command 0 of 6), and a sub-step answer that is not the sub-step's own intent (19 consequences restating it).

## What changed from draft 33a (the wording mine)

- **The examples' input lines are gone** ("Step: put [book1] on [shelf1], with the intent …. The detail asked for: …"). Their JSON is verbatim, and the Role is unchanged.
- **Every step line decides whether the step names its things** (mine): "It names its things." in five, and the coat's "The step, as written: hang up the coat. It doesn't name its things, so it is revised: hang [coat1] on [hook2]. It doesn't state how [coat1] is hung, so it is broken down." With the input gone, the coat's original wording has to be in its line.
- **Every sub-step line gives the intent first, then the answer after "beyond that,"** (mine): "pull [rug1] toward you: [rug1] slides out from under [crate2]; beyond that, [crate2] settles onto the floor. No rule." and "grip the edge of [rug1]: [rug1] is held at its edge; beyond that, nothing." Each intent is its JSON's `intent`, verbatim.
- **The Task's questions,** to match: "the step, as written; whether it names its things, and if not, the step revised to name them; and whether it states the detail asked for, …; then, for each sub-step, its intent, and then what it brings about beyond that, …". The stance question and the JSON description are unchanged.
- **Left alone, flagged:** the stance question's wording ("makes it happen where it ordinarily wouldn't", pasted beside `violation` in batch 58). Changing it would be a third change to the lines.
- **A confound, flagged:** dropping the inputs and the two line changes are read together. If the result is unclear, they can be split.
- **Checked before the batch:** the census reads all six examples as agreeing (step lines 6, sub-step lines 10, stances 2), with no consequence restating its intent.

**Verdict (mine, for review):**
- **Neither change can be read on its own, because of the confound.**
  - The two new kinds of line did what they were for: the filler is gone (19 to 1) and the command is revised (0 to 5).
  - Dropping the inputs cost the form of the answers (27 questionnaires), OC-1b, and OC-19.
  - It also let the scene's carry sentence through: with nothing in the examples standing where the scene stands, the model's content comes from the scene's words.
- **The examples' input lines were anchoring more than their form.** They gave the answer lines a shape to follow, and they held the reply to the Task's decisions, not its question text. 33a's plan-entry copy was their cost, and that has gone too.
- **The diagnosis we wanted on OC-1 has now come out:** with the carries back, the reasoning quotes the render's carry sentence word for word. It is the strongest evidence yet for the render booking on the imagination plan.
- **Next (mine):** split the confound. Draft 33c would be 33a's ask (inputs kept) with only 33b's two kinds of line. That tests whether "beyond that," and the "names its things" decision hold their gains with the anchor in place. If they do, needless revision is the bound to watch.
