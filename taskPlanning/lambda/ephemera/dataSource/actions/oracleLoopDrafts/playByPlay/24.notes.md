## What changed from draft 21 (the wording mine)

- **The Role:** "…break the step into the smaller steps that state it, or, when the step already states it as one motion, mark the step atomic." becomes "…give the step first, as written or revised so that its words name the things it uses, then break it into the smaller steps that state the detail, or, when the step already states it, give the step itself as the answer."
- **Every example's reply is the record:** each opens with a reasoning line "- the step, as written: <says>, with the intent <intent>", and its JSON with `"says"` and `"intent"` before `"steps"`. The book, crate, rug and cart examples restate their step as written.
- **The rope example** is the step itself as the answer: "- the step, as written: …; it already says how [rope1] is held", and `{"says": "grip [rope1] with both hands", "intent": "grip [rope1] with both hands"}`.
- **A new coat example** (the design's "an example of a revision that carries a child forward"): "hang up the coat", its intent its own text, the plan holding an unchecked "[hat1] falls off [hook2]". The reply revises it to "hang [coat1] on [hook2]", with the intent "[coat1] hangs from [hook2]", expands it, and carries the consequence forward onto the sub-step that brings it about. It is an analog of the tea cell, not the tea case, which is `OC-6/command`.
- **The Task:** "First give the step: as written, or revised so that it names in brackets the things in the scene it uses, with what it is for. When you revise the step, give again each of its items you keep, as a consequence of the step or of the sub-step that brings it about." The JSON sentence names `"says"` and `"intent"` first. The atomic sentence becomes "When the step already states the detail, say so, and the object is the step alone, its "says" and "intent", with "consequences" beside them when the step brings any about."

**Unchanged:** the input label "The step to expand:" (the render's), the other example content, and the rest of the Role and Task.

**The risks (mine):**
- **Revising everywhere.** Four examples restate as written and one revises. A reply that rephrases an intent it didn't need to revises the step, which on the concession cell drops its proposal. That is at the bar (the look-for), but it is worth counting.
- **The coat example's pull.** It is the only one that carries an item forward, and the concession cell is the only cell with an item to carry. A reply there that carries "[table1] cannot be lifted" forward onto the lift is allowed by the form, and is a concession in the method's place if the method bends to it.
- **The label.** "The step to expand:" still presupposes an expansion (batch 43's "To expand the step" 3/3). This draft doesn't test it.
