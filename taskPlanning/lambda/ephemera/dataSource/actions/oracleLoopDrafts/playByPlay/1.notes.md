## Rubric (written before the batch)

Read the form tallies first, then every reply.

**Each cell's look-for** (mine, from the plan's table; reviewed and accepted by the user for a start, 2026-11-10):

| Cell | Look for |
| --- | --- |
| OC-1 | everything on the table rises with it; nothing moves relative to the table unless the reply says why |
| OC-6 | the teapot lifted or tilted off the table, as pouring needs, and not written to fit the glue (PR-1) |
| OC-30/2 | the teapot tips with the table; the saucer and cup slide (the corpus's end result) |
| OC-19 | the teapot levered up and off the table, the table staying |
| OC-20 | the table lifted and pulled from the wall, the wall staying |

**For every reply, record:**
- **(a) Physics or the record:** does it say what has to happen physically, or restate the action (draft 10's register)?
- **(b) Fit to the rule:** is a motion written to keep a rule true, such as the teapot staying put because it is glued (PR-1)? The Fiction says "glued to" even with `sceneRules` off.
- **(c) Knock-on motion:** do things the action doesn't name move as they would, such as OC-30/2's saucer and cup, or OC-1's cup on its saucer?
- **(d) The lines read relationally:** would the rule cell read each pair's lines as together or apart without guessing? A pair of bare "tilts" is PR-11's form. Note which lines name another thing by handle.
- **(e) The narration and the lines agree.**
- **(f) Invented things or handles,** in the prose or the block.

**The rubric:**
- **(a) Physics or the record:** mostly physics. Lifts, tilts and pivots are named, and OC-1 and OC-6 read as motions. OC-19's lines restate the action in 2 of 3 replies.
- **(b) Fit to the rule:** **none on OC-6**, the PR-1 case. But the glue and the chain are read as something the plan must get past. Four replies add or suggest a step that clears them: OC-6 repeat 1 ("will need to be unglued"), OC-30/2 repeat 3 ("released from its glued position"), OC-20 repeats 1 and 3 (the chain detached). That is the reverse of PR-1. The rule is not kept by the motion; the action is rewritten so that the rule doesn't stand in its way. This is the forbidden-action reasoning the user left for a later generator (PB-P2).
- **(c) Knock-on motion:** right on OC-1, where everything is carried. **Absent on OC-30/2**, where the saucer and cup are written as staying put because the pour needs them to (PB-P3).
- **(d) Lines read relationally:** good where there are lines. OC-6's "lifted from the table" against "remains stationary", OC-19's "pried off the table" against "remains stationary", OC-30/2 repeat 2's "pivots with the table", and OC-1's "remains glued to the table as it is lifted" and "stays on the saucer" would each read as together or apart without guessing. OC-30/2 repeat 1's "remains stable on the table until the tilt begins" would not.
- **(e) Narration and lines agree:** yes, wherever both are there. The failures lose lines; they don't contradict the prose.
- **(f) Invented things or steps:** no invented handles. Invented steps: OC-6's teapot returned, and the clearing steps under (b). OC-30/2 repeat 2 pours the tea "onto the saucer" on its way to the cup.

**What the batch says.**
- **The carried context works for the motion.** OC-6's tilt, the case Slice 4 fought for twelve drafts (PR-1), is 3 of 3 with the table still, and OC-1's carry is 3 of 3 with the frame model's reason.
- **Two problems remain:**
  - the block's shape, half the replies (PB-P1);
  - a goal-directed register: "what each of the things has to do physically **for the actions to happen**" is read as what each thing must do so that the plan succeeds. That gives preconditions ("must be placed on the saucer"), things kept still because the pour needs them (OC-30/2's saucer and cup, PB-P3), and rules cleared so that the plan can go through (PB-P2).
- That phrase is drafts 6, 11 and 12's. There it sat in a prompt whose later steps judged the plan, so it may have been read the same way and not been visible.
