## Diagnosis with the user (2026-11-19): why a `consistent` rule at all

**The user's ruling:** proposing a violation isn't `playByPlay`'s job, so a missing one is no miss. What needs looking at is why the replies propose a rule's consistency at all. On OC-6 the glue's rule was attached to the tea flowing and to the pot emptying.

**What the replies say they mean by it** (their prose, mine to read): **`consistent` is read as "the rule is still in force", a status of the rule, not a judgment that the consequence agrees with it.**
- OC-6 r1: "The teapot remains secured to the table due to the rule [teapot-secured-to-table], which continues to be consistent."
- OC-1 r3: "the rule [teapot-secured-to-table] remains consistent since the teapot is glued to the table", attached to the saucer's carry.
- OC-20 r1: "maintaining the rule that the table is secured to the wall."
- OC-20 r2: "The rule `[table-secured-to-wall]` remains consistent because the table is not detached from the wall, even though it is lifted."

Under that reading, any consequence in a scene with a rule can carry the rule as `consistent`, because the rule is still there. That accounts for the tea and the saucer. It also accounts for OC-20's three `consistent` chains: the replies report that the chain still holds, and the method is shaped so that it does.

**What in draft 20 invites it** (mine; candidates, not tested):
1. **The Role asks what happens "to the scene's rules"**, as if a rule were a thing with a fate to report.
2. **The crate example's reasoning line reads "keeping rule [lamp-secured-to-crate]".** "Keeping" reads naturally as "the rule is maintained".
3. **The ask never says what a stance judges.** It only names the two values.
4. **Every proposing example carries a rule** (19 of 19 proposals did), so a consequence seems to need one. With one rule in the scene, the nearest handle gets attached; with none fitting, one gets invented (6 form failures).
