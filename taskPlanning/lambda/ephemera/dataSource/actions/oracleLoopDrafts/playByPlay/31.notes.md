  - consequences on the thing being handled: 69 of 93, against 1 of 6 in the examples;
  - what fills the grip: the intent restated ("is ready to be used") 16, the next step's lift (the folded lift) 14.
- **The examples' inputs looked nothing like the real ones** (the user's intuition). They were one line of prose, with no scene, no plan listing and no path. The real input carries a carry sentence on every thing and a violation sentence on every secured thing, and no example showed the right answer to either. Output-shaped lessons transferred (the record's keys, the step as the answer, the stance values). Conditional ones didn't (an empty grip, an untagged carry, no invented handle).
- **Fewer examples, fully described** (the user): the cost is a fixed prefix, which caching can make cheap in production (the house precedent is `invokeBedrockHypothesis.ts`'s cache point). What caching doesn't change is Micro's attention, so the held cells are the check on length.
- **This takes up a parked item:** draft 25's input-shaped examples, parked on the imagination plan's step 2 after batch 48 changed three things at once in the prose era. Draft 25's `Scene:` was one line; this draft writes the scene in full.

**Verdict (mine, for review):**
- **Draft 31 isn't kept.** It cost OC-1b (6 to 1 of 6) and doubled the rule tags, with invented handles at 10.
- **The user's intuition holds, the other way round from the hope.** Examples in the input's form transfer far more strongly than one-line ones: the tray's sentence shape, the cart's violation, the per-thing consequences. So input form was suppressing transfer, and what now transfers is the content the examples say, not what they leave out.
