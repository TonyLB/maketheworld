## What the draft is

**The first step of the iterative loop: "I need this action imagined in more detail"** (PB-L19). The loop, as the user sketched it (2026-11-11):
1. The oracle asks for the action imagined in more detail.
2. The imagination gives the ordinary method (pick up the teapot, tilt it, put it down), and a possible problem: the teapot is glued to the table.
3. The oracle agrees it's a problem and asks for an alternate method.
4. The imagination gives a new method (tilt the table, and the teapot tilts with it).
5. The oracle asks what happens to the other things.
6. The imagination gives the knock-on (the saucer and cup slide off), and a possible problem: the cup is falling.
7. The oracle asks for a mitigating method, and so on, until it judges the plan possible.

The oracle's asks are a small set (mine, from the sketch): more detail, an alternate method, what happens to the other things, a mitigating method, a verdict. This draft is the first ask only.

**The scene is shown in full, and the problems get their own list** (user, 2026-11-11). In batch 8 the glue had nowhere to go but the motion ("remains firmly glued … doesn't move relative to the table"). The `problems` list gives it a place, so the method can be written the ordinary way. Hiding the glue at first was considered and kept as the fallback. The later steps use the glue as a resource (the teapot tips with the table), and hiding means code decides in advance which facts matter (mine).

**How this differs from draft 6's complications** (draft 6's question was "what does a separate place buy?", user, 2026-11-10). Draft 6's complications were consequences of the motion: the same register, with a look-back over the reply's own text. These problems are a different kind of thing: what in the scene might stop the method. They are the hand-off to the oracle.

**Against draft 7:**
- **Role:** "the imagination for a game world"; the action "needs to be imagined in more detail"; describe how it "would ordinarily be done", and point out anything in the scene that might get in the way.
- **No focus pair.** One text for every cell. Naming the secured pair was half of batch 8's trouble.
- **The premise is back** (batch 8's other half): "what each of the things after "The things:" has to do physically for the action to happen", draft 5's sentence in the singular.
- **Step by step:** "describe step by step how it would ordinarily be done". Batch 8 split actions into steps 3 times. Here the steps are asked for in the prose, and the block keeps one entry per action.
- **Possible problems:** "anything in the scene that might get in the way of doing it that way". It flags, it doesn't judge.
- **The block:** draft 5's `actions`, plus a top-level `"problems"` list, "empty if there are none".
- **Unchanged:** Reading the scene, Relations and frames, the examples, `rules: 'none'`.
- **No code change.** The check ignores top-level keys other than `actions`, so the problems list is read by hand, not checked.
