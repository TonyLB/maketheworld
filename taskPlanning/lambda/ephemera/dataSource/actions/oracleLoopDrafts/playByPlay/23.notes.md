## Why

Across batches 41 and 42, OC-1b's six replies included five one-entry expansions. Each paraphrased the target ("place your hands on [table1]'s edges"), and batch 42 r1 said in prose "This step is atomic in the sense that it describes the action of holding the table by its edges". The replies say the step already answers the question, then write a breakdown, because `atomic` is a word they have to translate into ([21.notes.md](21.notes.md#batch-42-the-conditions-renders-confirm-2026-11-19)).

## What changed from draft 21

Three places, all of the atomic case, the wording mine:
- **The Role:** "…or, when the step already states it as one motion, mark the step atomic." becomes "…or, when the step already answers it, say that it already answers the detail asked for."
- **The rope example:** "- one motion: the step already says how [rope1] is held" and `{"atomic": true}` become "- already answered: the step already says how [rope1] is held" and `{"alreadyAnswers": ["how [rope1] is held"]}`.
- **The Task:** "When the step already states the detail as one motion, say so, and the object is {"atomic": true}…" becomes "When the step already answers the detail asked for, say so, and the object is {"alreadyAnswers": [the detail asked for, as written]}…".

**Unchanged:** the input label "The step to expand:", which is the render's, so the hashes stay. Also unchanged: every other example, and the rest of the Role and Task.

**The risks (mine):**
- **False answers on the cells that should expand.** OC-1 and OC-20 ask "how [table1] is held and lifted" of "pick up [table1]". A reply that says the step already answers it is a regression, and the more intuitive the form, the likelier it is.
- **The label.** "The step to expand:" still presupposes an expansion. If OC-1b doesn't move, this is the next suspect, and it is untested here.
- **Copying the question.** "as written" may give back `missing` word for word, or a paraphrase of it. Either is fine for the probe. Exact-match refusal is a design question, booked.
