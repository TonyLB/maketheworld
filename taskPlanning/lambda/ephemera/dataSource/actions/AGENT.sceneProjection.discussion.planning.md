## SP-10 contenders as axes

**Contenders as axes (user, 2026-11-03).** The contenders were first listed under one framing: who does each kind of reasoning, the reader by reading or code by computing (every SP-7 candidate left all of it to reading). They turned out to be settings of independent axes, not packages: an affordance render with structure behind tools, and the same render with structure up front in other registers, differ on one axis only. So each test varies one axis, where SP-7's formats differed on several at once. **Renders and framing are tested apart (user, 2026-11-03):** new renders first, compared under one fixed frame; then, independently, framing variants (general worked examples, output registers) over the chosen render. The order is Slice 5's.<br>- **The principle (user, 2026-11-03): highlight the exceptions to the norm, so the reader fills the rest from what it expects.** Widened in discussion to: state what the reader's prior would get wrong, and otherwise get out of its way. The misses run two ways. The prior overrides the scene: Nova knows teapots pour, so it noted the glue and poured anyway (the exception was recorded but not salient). The representation overrides the prior: Micro lifted [cup1] and left [tea1] on [saucer1], which no prior would do (the tree layout called up reparenting). Foregrounding exceptions answers the first; a register that lets the prior work answers the second. The prior cannot be read, so what it would get wrong is approximated by deviations from the game's own defaults (secured, glued, fixed features).<br>- **Foreground:** nothing marked; exceptions only; or exceptions and consequences, computed from the game's rules ("[teapot1] cannot be lifted: glued to [table1]"; "lifting [saucer1] also lifts [cup1] and [tea1]"). A consequence line states the norm, so by the principle it earns its place only while the representation suppresses the prior; whether a good world register makes it unnecessary is testable. Limit: only rules the game encodes; creative uses (prying with the saucer) stay with the reader. Its queries are graph-side, but not the deferred operation vocabulary.<br>- **Structure delivery:** up front, or by tools that answer exact structure and consequences from code (a thing's ancestry, its relations; a semantic search over embeddings once the attention-focused cache trims the scene). World content (glosses, ties, "glued") stays up front either way: the first tool sketch withheld it, but a reader asks only about what it already suspects matters. A tool turn is one more call in a loop that has tools anyway (Identify), so a query or two per command is affordable. Testing tools needs a multi-turn probe, which SP-8's is not.<br>- **Registers:** one view, or several views of one scene model tied by handles (a room-physics description, a graph listing; see "One render is not a constraint" above).<br>- **Teaching:** none, or worked examples, from "can this happen" to "what changes", in two kinds (user, 2026-11-03). **Generated:** built deterministically by code from the scene being rendered, about its own things, so they are part of a render. **General:** one or two fixed worked scenes in a domain far from the fixtures, part of the frame. An axis of the prompt, not the representation; examples can leak into answers, as the grounding's lid example did (SP-7).<br>- **Output registers (user, 2026-11-03):** one, or two: what happens in the world, then what changes in the scene's records. A slot for graph thinking should keep it from bleeding into the fiction, and two registers can be checked against each other: "pick up the cup, and pour its tea onto the saucer" over `(move [cup1] to held), (move [tea1] to [saucer1])` is a correct answer (with explicit operations, contents go with their container and any reparenting is stated), while the same operations under "pick up the cup" alone show a mismatch no single register would. The world register comes first, so the graph translates the fiction rather than the fiction rationalising the graph. Free notation for the graph register until the operation vocabulary exists. Testable now through the frame: `OPEN_ANSWER_FORMAT` already asks for an explanation and then the changes, but labels neither as a register, and its Constraints and Blockers headings mix the two.<br>**Future functionality, not a contender (user, 2026-11-03): dry-run self-correction.** The orchestration loop proposes operations, and a dry run says whether they are legal and returns what the scene would be after (as a diff in world terms, easier to compare than a full render). It catches illegal operations, and corrects a wrong expectation about what a legal one does (a reader expecting the tea left behind sees it still in [cup1]); operations that do not match the fiction are the output registers' check. It needs the operation vocabulary, so it comes after the world side, and the loop is its owner (the ladder plan). **It is not built or simulated while renders are graded against each other**; this plan's share is the wording of the after-diff, when the loop calls for it.<br>**Considered, not a contender (user, 2026-11-03):** a render centred on the command's grounded referents. Most of the time the loop's referents are not grounded yet ("flip the table"); it fits an impossibility check on a grounded attempt ("get teapot" against the teapot's securing), not the loop's reading.<br>

**Renders (first batch, pick up `[cup1]`, old frame, 3 runs each, score out of 9: tea comes, saucer stays, teapot and table stay).** D 5, `registers` 4, `affordance` 6, `examples` 4--5: no separation inside the spread of about 2 points. The shared failure was the saucer coming with the cup (10 of 12 runs, every format). `affordance` carried the tea in 3 of 3 runs ("Moving [cup1] also moves [tea1]") and misread its own chain of lines as symmetric, taking the saucer along in 3 of 3. `examples` copied the examples' notation and invented operations (`static`, `clear`, `update`), once contradicting the scene. These batches predate the frame changes below, so the renders have **not** yet been compared under the current frame.

**Direction audit (the saucer and `[cup1] is no longer in [tea1]` errors).** Language in the frame or renders that was symmetric where the error needs direction: the grounding's "how things are connected" (models reasoned "because they are directly connected"), the open-answer headings (Constraints restated the scene; a placement then became a Blocker, and "must be solved" pushed the model to lift the saucer or detach the cup), "placed to move together" (my own first draft of the `move` paragraph), `affordance`'s "also moves" chain, "comes with" in the question, and one phrase ("on or in") used for two relations in one sentence. Untested suspects: the foreground stating what *stays* (`Moving [cup1] also moves [tea1]; [saucer1] and [table1] stay.`), and "comes with" in the question wording. Not an error after all: "table fixed in place", which is the game's rule (a secured relation blocks either end).

**Frame changes made (each a single edit, user-reviewed wording):**
- Grounding: "how things are connected" became "what other relations it has to another thing, such as being tied or glued to it", with placement given as examples (on, in, held).
- A `Moving things` paragraph (`MOVING`) defines `(move X to On Y)` / `(move X to In Y)` with the subtree going down, never up, and a physical reason (a fish in a bucket; a far domain, since examples leak). Under its first wording ("move together", which the audit later found mutual) the saucer stayed behind in 3 of 4 pick-up runs (before: 1 of 6 in D and `registers`); it was reworded afterwards, and the reworded form has not been run on the pick-up question except under `abstain` and `plain` (3 and 2.5 of 3).
- The open-answer line asks for moves in that notation (the first batch under `MOVING` wrote every change in prose). Under it all pour answers used the notation; "to In hand" recurs where the definition says `held`.
- An `answerFormats` axis on the probe (`headings`, `plain`, `candidates`, `abstain`; a `brief` option returns answers without the repeated scene). Default `abstain`, set by the user.

Every `affordance` run stayed clear of the glue as a blocker for the cup; the glue-as-blocker misread was `registers`' (it moved the teapot "with some effort"). **Suggested next, not run:** the `untie` hypothesis (an `examples` variant whose blocker example is a plain `move`, or none); a `held` example in `MOVING` (already parked) as a cheaper way to the `examples` gain; run-to-run spread of `registers` on the pour is wide (2 of 4), so any later comparison needs 3 or more runs per cell on the question it turns on.

**Blocker ladder (2026-11-03, user's design).** The grounding's blocked-way sentences assumed a blocker and never made "nothing blocks it" a result, so the over-moves (clearing the saucer and the glued teapot) were suspected to come from our wording, not a belief of the model's; a negative added to that would not have worked. Replaced by an all-positive ladder (simplest steps, checked step by step: a blocker belongs to a step, from the scene or from the world, such as a motorcycle that cannot fit a shoebox; then freeing what stops a step; then the puzzle-solver's inventive paths), a precedence line by register (the scene is authoritative about what is here and how it is placed, the world about what follows, the records' layout is notation), and `abstain` reworded to check referents first. Pour on `registers`, 3 runs each, then B: **over-moves 0 of 6 (was 2 of 4)**; the stop rule held (candle 2 of 2 "No candidate", about 90 output tokens); pick-up facts right 2 of 2. New problems: in 3 of 6 pours the tea never moves by an operation, two `abstain` runs set the cup `On table1`, one repeated "nothing is stopping it" under every step, and both pick-ups added a redundant tea move after the cup. Clean pours 2 of 6, no better than before. The `MOVING` ending was suspected for the redundant moves and was reworded to say what a move stands for (any action ending in a new place), that one move carries the contents, and how to get only part out. **Parked question (user): how to move a thing and not its contents** (move the contents to the thing's host first, then the thing). Left out until a question needs it; the first time someone tries to take the saucer from under the cup it will matter, and it may be the tablecloth-pull case. It also describes the end state the model reaches by mistake (the tea left on the saucer), so check it against that when it is added.
