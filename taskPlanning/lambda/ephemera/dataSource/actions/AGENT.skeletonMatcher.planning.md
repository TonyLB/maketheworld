# Skeleton matcher: split finest, merge as needed

**Status: opened 2026-10-25; Slice 0 (characterization corpus) done 2026-10-26; SM-11 and SM-12 open.** An implementation plan, per [`taskPlanning/AGENT.md`](../../../../AGENT.md), with an unusually long Open decisions section: every slice after Slice 0 is gated on at least one row. It is rung 15 of the iteration ladder in [`AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md`](AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md). It rebuilds what rungs 12 (skeleton patterns) and 13 (phrase coverage) built. It goes before rung 14 (match tuning) without superseding it: structure first, then Identify tuning (SM-10).

## Goal

Replace the template matcher and the stages around it with one structure built to be solid, rather than repaired one bug at a time. Rungs 12 and 13 got the matcher to "not actively broken" by a run of local fixes, each correct where it was made, each putting a decision in whichever stage saw the symptom (see [What exists](#what-exists-read-from-code-2026-10-25)).

**The principle (user, 2026-10-25): split at maximum resolution, then let the matcher reconstitute larger aggregates.**

- The command is cut at every dividing word (`on`, `in`, `at`, ...), so no tokenization ever decides grouping. Splitting loses nothing, because a merge rebuilds any run, while a run never split cannot be cut later.
- Grouping is the grammar's job. A span slot is a run of one or more tokens, re-joined as text. So a slot handed `B C D` tries both the object phrase `(ObjectN) C (ObjectN+1)` and `B C D` reconcatenated as one name.
- An action template `A (Object1) C (Object2)` makes one candidate per place its fixed word can cut (`A B C (D C E)` and `A (B C D) C E`), multiplied by the candidates each object phrase returns recursively.

**Payoff, stated at the observable output.** `put pig in a blanket on the plate on the table` (tokens `put | pig | in | a blanket | on | the plate | on | the table`) produces every reading below, and each world commits the one it allows:

| Destination cut | Object1 | Object2 | Readings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2nd `on` | `pig in a blanket on the plate` | `the table` | the meal, already on the plate, placed on the table; a pig in (the blanket on the plate) placed on the table |
| 1st `on` | `pig in a blanket` | `the plate on the table` | the meal placed on the plate on the table; a pig in a blanket placed on the plate on the table |
| `in` | `pig` | `a blanket on the plate on the table` | a pig put into the blanket on the plate on the table |

Each row multiplies by Object2's own readings (`plate on table` as a qualifier chain, or one merged name).

- In a world with a "pig in a blanket" object and a plate on the table, the meal ends on the plate (asserted on the player's line and the graphs).
- In a world with a pig and a blanket, the qualifier readings are judged by the dry run like any other.
- Kept as they are: `take cup from table` with the cup on the floor still answers "The table has no cup."; `take man in the moon from table` takes it; `put cup on saucer on table` commits the reading the world allows (`qualifiedTakePayoff.integration.test.ts`, `phraseCoveragePayoff.integration.test.ts`).

## Why now

The two consequences of the principle (more candidates, which pressures Identify, and a divider set that spans templates) need design work. They are the right design work: both questions arise in any parser of this language, so answering them once gives them one home. Most of today's fixes answer questions that exist only because of this pipeline's shape (user and assistant, 2026-10-25).

It also serves step 2 of the ladder's [orchestration proposal](AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md#proposal-deterministic-first-then-an-orchestration-loop-2026-10-25) ("templates match against the text"): one engine over the finest tokenization makes the fast path and the LLM route the same matcher by construction.

## What exists (read from code, 2026-10-25)

The pipeline:
1. **Fast path** ([`discriminateIntent/deterministicChecks.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/discriminateIntent/deterministicChecks.ts) `maybeDeterministicManipulationIntent`). The membership pattern and the containment template's pattern are matched against the text (`matchPatternAgainstString`), behind routing gates; each fill is flattened back to a draft (`draftFromPattern`) and handed up as `ObjectManipulationIntent.skeletons`.
2. **LLM route** ([`parseCommand.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/parseCommand.ts)). Classify, then the intent rows (`matchNonObjectManipulationTemplate`), the Navigation regex (`plan/matchNavigationParaphrase.ts`), Parse (one flat skeleton), Plan, then AcmeOrder (`plan/matchAcmeOrderFamily.ts`).
3. **Plan** ([`plan/planSkeleton.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/plan/planSkeleton.ts)). Each stamped skeleton is matched against every `AttemptTemplate` (`matchPatternAgainstTokens`); each fill is a reading; `build(captures)` makes an attempt, and `lowerQualifiers` adds `containedBy` assertions keyed by `stableRefKey`.
4. **Producer** ([`compileAttemptsFromSkeleton.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/compileAttemptsFromSkeleton.ts)). Recovers each attempt's kind from its content (`kindOf`), then filters (entry checks, exact-fill precedence, the membership unread-span gate) before Identify, the dry run and selection.

The epicycles, which the slices retire:
- **Two engines, one element vocabulary, different meanings** ([`deterministicTemplate/patternTemplate.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/deterministicTemplate/patternTemplate.ts)). A text wildcard takes one or more words, a token wildcard exactly one token; `objectPhrase` gives flat cuts on text and nested readings on tokens; a fixed word may sit anywhere in the text but must equal a whole token (so `hurriedly into` fails); `rest` is checked last-only in the token engine alone. The fast path therefore matches twice: text, flat draft, stamp, tokens.
- **Positional captures; roles unread.** Builders read `optionAt(captures, 0)` and `spanAt(captures, 3)` ([`plan/templateCaptures.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/plan/templateCaptures.ts)); the look builder reads `captures.length - 1` so two patterns can share it. `role` is declared on elements and read only by `assembleIntent`.
- **Template kind discarded, then inferred.** `planSkeleton` drops the template name; `kindOf` infers it (last action narrates: look; transfer with no `containment`: membership). "The primary action is the last" is relied on by `kindOf`, `transferOf` and `commandAttempt/lookedAtObjectId.ts`.
- **Over-generation corrected downstream.** Membership's `rest` lets the pattern match anything; the producer's BD-20 gate drops fills whose tail holds a span. Exact-fill precedence chooses a segmentation inside compile. `stableRefKey` is derived from span text so keys agree across fills, which is what lets one Identify serve them all.
- **Match policy outside templates.** `claimsMembership` destructures captures by position, holds a `take hold` regex and a room-label check for `get`. Containment and spread share `placingPattern` and avoid each other only by disjoint verb lists.
- **Several template and matcher kinds.** `DeterministicTemplate` (one fill or `noMatch`; `makeDeferPatternTemplate` has no users), `AttemptTemplate` (one attempt per fill), a Navigation regex, a hand-written AcmeOrder matcher (no repetition element), bare-word regexes in `deterministicChecks.ts` duplicating the bare-word rows. `deterministicTemplateRegistry`, the bare-word `lookTemplate` and `predictTemplate` have no live caller.
- **Lexicons scattered.** `MEMBERSHIP_VERBS`, `PLACING_VERB_FORMS`, `CONTAINMENT_PHRASES`, `SPREAD_PHRASES`, `QUALIFIER_PREPOSITIONS`/`QUALIFIER_OPTIONS` (with `out` read as `out of`), two inline look-verb lists that differ, `drop` in two verb lexicons with different meanings, two exports named `lookTemplate`.
- **Bookkeeping.** Fills deduplicated by `JSON.stringify` in three places; an intent-classification result type (`ObjectManipulationIntent`) carries parse output.

Not epicycles, only misplaced (they need a home, not deletion): `out` means `out of`, containment and spread must not both claim one command (homes: the elements' typed lexicons, SM-6). The `take hold` and `get` gates were read the same way when this plan opened; SM-7 retires them instead.

| SM-1 | **Tokens are granules of the fixed words (user, 2026-10-26).** Granules: two adjacent words of a fixed-word option stay joined only if they are adjacent in every option where either appears. One pass over adjacent pairs, no iteration: `on top of` with `of both` gives `on top` \| `of` \| `both`; `a b c` with `b c d` gives `a` \| `b c` \| `d`. So each fixed word belongs to exactly one granule, and a boundary exists only where some option begins or ends. Tokenizing the command is a scan: a run of words is a granule token where that granule's whole word sequence appears, and every other run of words is one span-material token. With only `on top of` and `of both`, `get the book on top of both` is `get \| the book \| on top \| of \| both`; `on top` is not split, since no fill could cut there. With the real qualifier lexicon (`on` and `out` stand alone) the prepositions come down to single-word granules, while span material stays one token per run (`super laser death ray gun`), which is what keeps adjacent-span readings (SM-12's repetition) from multiplying. Word-level tokens were rejected for that reason (user, 2026-10-26). Overlap is the matcher's job, not the tokenizer's: a fixed-word element consumes a run of granule tokens equal to one of its options, so `on top` + `of` is `on top of` in one fill and `of` + `both` is `of both` in another, and no fill uses a token twice. Longest match needs no rule: `put cup on top of box` gives fills at `on` and at `on top of`, and SM-4's malus charges `top of box`. A template's words cannot match inside a longer word (`on` in `onto`). Tokens depend on the template set, harmlessly: readings come from complete fills, span text is re-joined, `stableRefKey` comes from span text, and SM-4 reads complete readings, so changing granules changes nothing outside the matcher (pinned in Slice 2: adding a template changes no reading of a command it does not read completely). Sub-questions: (1) punctuation (`order anvil, rocket`; `take cup, then ...`): a token, attached to a word, or dropped; SM-12 depends on it; (2) a word appearing twice in one option (none does today). | 1 | Decided |
| SM-2 | **The Parse LLM is retired (user, 2026-10-25).** The finest tokenizer is the only segmenter, on every route: deterministic templates run first against the command text, and what they leave falls through to the Plan LLM, which (once designed) reads with the world in view and can segment better anyway. Why it costs the templates nothing: under SM-1 every fixed word divides and a span is a run between them, so Parse could add only a fixed word inside a span (merging covers it), open words in a text run (`hurriedly into`, which no template reads either), or adjacent spans with no divider between (AcmeOrder's list, SM-12). When it is retired is SM-11. | 1, 5 | Decided |
| SM-3 | **Right-nested, one qualifier per span; a merge may cross any token (user, 2026-10-26).** Right-nesting is not equivalent to any bracketing, because a qualifier relates the **head** (leftmost span) of its left operand to the head of its right one: `cup on (saucer on table)` asserts cup-saucer and saucer-table, while `(cup on saucer) on table` asserts cup-saucer and cup-table, two hosts for one head, which a direct-host judgment can never satisfy (the shelf's "A phrase with two hosts"). Merges supply the left-bracketed readings that are legitimate: `[cup on saucer] on table` reads an object *named* "cup on saucer" hosted by the table, one edge. Each qualifier preposition inside an object phrase is either a link or inside a span, so an object phrase with k of them has exactly 2^k readings, one per choice; any bracketing would multiply that by the Catalan numbers. A merge may cross any token, a template's own fixed words included (`look at cup` read by the plain look template merges `at cup`, which SM-4's malus charges because the look-at template reads `cup` alone). A closed list of relation words that block a merge was rejected: deterministically a relation word is only a word on a list, and relational phrasing is open ("the cup sitting by the stove"), so a list would block some commands and absorb others by membership alone. Consequences: SM-1 divides only at words templates read; `get cup under table` absorbs ` under table` as surplus no reading explains, judged by Identify at the ordinary rate (today the LLM route refuses it via `rest` and the BD-20 gate, a change Slice 0's corpus records); recognizing that such a command should defer to the LLM rung is rung 14's (item (6) of its ladder row). `cup on saucer under chandelier` (a peer relation, perhaps two qualifiers on one head) is a conceptual need of the Plan LLM's output vocabulary, not a fast-path requirement. Flagged for graduation (assistant, unanswered): whether the contract's "A qualifier is never dropped to rescue a take" needs a sentence saying it governs the qualifiers the matcher reads, not surplus text. | 2 | Decided |
| SM-4 | **The Identify evidence that judges a merge (structure only; SM-10). Decided 2026-10-25; what remains is work in Slices 1 and 4.** A merged run competes on every command, and the lexical rank forgives words around a name (`cup from table` fuzzily matches the cup). The cause, read from code: `lexicalRelevance` (`embeddingMatch/lexicalRelevance.ts`) embeds the shorter string in the longer and penalizes the flanks, never asking which side the surplus came from. **Proposal (user, 2026-10-25): surplus text that could be explained structurally takes a heavier lexical malus than ordinary surplus.** Against "pig", `pig in a blanket` is a worse match than `piggy pig piggy`, though the surplus is about as long, because a cut at `in` would explain the former's surplus and nothing explains the latter's. Every candidate stays in the pool with its score; nothing is pruned before selection. It extends the flank split `deriveFlankLengthMetrics` already makes (adjoined vs remote) with a third class: flank outside a shorter run that another reading reads as a span of its own. **Span side only (user, 2026-10-25):** the heavier malus applies to the span's own surplus, never to a catalog name's extra words. `pig` against "pig in a blanket" takes no special penalty, though the omitted `in a blanket` could be structural: the player shortened a name, and nothing in the command was left for the structure to explain. **What counts as explainable: only a cut some complete reading makes (user, 2026-10-25).** While expanding the templates, the matcher tags each token with the groupings it is matched over; Identify then knows, for each span, the shorter runs inside it that another reading of the command reads as spans of their own (for `pig in a blanket`: `pig`, `a blanket`). Surplus outside such a run takes the structural malus. An alternative counts only if it comes from a **complete** reading of the command: a template that would cut at a word but fails elsewhere contributes nothing. That is what makes the malus a judgment that the text could legitimately be read differently in the context of the whole, not a penalty for a token being present. Example: with `onto` a divider (some `spread (X) onto (Y)`), `get pig onto jewel` against "pig" takes **no** structural malus, since no template reads `get (X) onto (Y)` and the object phrase does not link at `onto`; the only reading is the merged span, charged at the ordinary rate. Consequences: SM-1's divider set governs tokenization only, never scoring, and adding a template changes scores only for commands it reads completely. Viability of the alternative (whether its other spans ground) is deliberately not consulted: the malus is a score, not a prune, so a merged reading whose alternative fails (`take pig in xyzzy`) stays in the pool and can win. Settled points: (1) the boundary type (work, Slice 4): each span carries its token sequence and the set of its alternative sub-runs as token ranges within the span (for `pig in a blanket`: `pig`, `a blanket`), derived from the token tags, the union across every reading the span text appears in, rather than Identify receiving the raw tags; (2) structure is carried in token positions only, never character offsets into the command (user, 2026-10-26): Identify derives character ranges itself, inside the string it scores (the span's tokens re-joined, normalized to single spaces), with article peeling (`spanMatchForms`) a fixed shift by the article's length; with Parse retired (SM-2) a span's text can never differ from its tokens; (3) **decided: a match crossing a sub-run's edge takes the ordinary rate (user, 2026-10-25).** The structural malus applies only when the match lies inside an alternative sub-run. A name whose match runs across a cut (`pig in a blanket` against "pig iron", which Sellers fits to about `pig in a`) is reading across the structure, the fuzzy form of an exact name holding a divider ("man in the moon"), so it is evidence for the merged reading, not against it. Containment is tested on the match trimmed to its aligned characters (edit fringe and whitespace dropped), not on Sellers' raw region, so a region stretched by a trailing space or an edge substitution (`pig ` against "pigs") does not count as crossing; no tolerance constant; (4) **decided: lexical channel only (user, 2026-10-25).** The lexical alignment is the only place the cost of the surplus words themselves can be told apart: Sellers locates the match, so the flank characters are known, while an embedding is one vector for the whole string with no attribution to its words, and charging the joint relevance would penalize embedding evidence for something it did not measure. Consequence, read from code: the joint relevance is a weighted RMS soft-OR of the two channels (`weightedRmsJointRelevance`, `relevanceCombine.ts`, `JOINT_RELEVANCE_W_L` = `JOINT_RELEVANCE_W_E` = 1.0), so a strong embedding score attenuates the malus's effect on selection without masking it; how much survives is rung 14's calibration; **Handed to rung 14, not open here (user, 2026-10-25; recorded as item (5) of its ladder row):** the malus's size (this plan builds the class, a placeholder cost and the ordering tests), and retiring exact-fill precedence (`actions/AGENT.contract.md` "Qualifier"), which this plan carries over to the new candidates and which the malus is expected to make redundant. | 4 | Decided |
