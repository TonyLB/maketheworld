# ConnectedTo: topology within one whole, and a rope across rooms

**Status: opened 2026-10-15; Slice 0 shipped 2026-10-21; instruction set revised 2026-10-20 (see [The instruction set](#the-instruction-set-extend-contract-and-a-move-of-a-view)). Next step: [Slice 1](#recommended-order).** An implementation plan, per [`taskPlanning/AGENT.md`](../../../../AGENT.md). The design question it builds on is [EA-12](AGENT.edgeAbstractions.planning.md#ea-12) in the edge-abstractions plan, answered *yes, a kind* by the user on 2026-10-15. The code it licenses is a **Prototype** (see [Licensing](#licensing-a-prototype-with-its-rollback-trigger)).

## Goal

Add `ConnectedTo` as a relation kind that states **topology within one whole**: parts (or bindings) of one conceptual whole, and how they join. Use it first to make a **rope across two rooms** creatable: carrying one end of a rope into the next room **extends** the rope in place (a second presence binding beside the first, the two connected by `ConnectedTo`) and **moves** the new binding into the other room, in one committed step sequence.

**Why a kind and not a `Custom` label** (from EA-12): the motivating consumer is a deterministic retrieval read --- tugging a rope extends the `ludicCache` to the rooms the rope reaches. Retrieval builds the context the adjudicator reasons over, so it cannot ask the adjudicator which edges to follow; it has to select edges by kind. That read is **not** in this plan (see [Not in scope](#not-in-scope)); this plan makes the thing it reads exist.

**The distinction this plan must keep, the user's call:** `ConnectedTo` joins parts of **one** whole. A join between **separate** wholes (`SolderedTo`, plugged into, tied to) is a peer relation, `Custom` with a label, and stays that way. Slice 3 enforces this.

**Payoff, stated at the observable output:** a player carries one end of a rope from room A into room B. A `look` in room B shows the rope, a `look` in room A still shows it, and the rope's own graph holds `{Rope, pA} -ConnectedTo-> {Rope, pB}`. **A second payoff, the same instructions:** a player drapes a tablecloth over a table. The cloth has one binding under the room and one under the table, connected; a plate put on the cloth lands in the cover of the part on the table.

## The instruction set: extend, contract, and a move of a view

Decided with the user 2026-10-20, after reviewing the spread shipped under ISS8306 (a `keepFrom` flag on `transferMembership` that writes one binding with parents on two hosts, plus a take that the commit widens to every host, `alsoLeaving`). These are Plan-level instructions (the `PlanStep` union, [`planStep.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/plan/planStep.ts)), not kernel steps; each compiles to the kernel steps beneath it.

- **Extend** takes an object that has extent (a rope, a string, slime, a cloth) and adds a second presence binding **in place**: under the same parents as the binding it extends, connected to it by `ConnectedTo`. It changes nothing anyone sees; it is the step a move of the new binding follows. Compiles to `splitPresenceBinding` plus `establishRelation` with `kind: 'ConnectedTo'`. Its precondition, *this thing has extent*, is where [OD-3](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only)'s judgment lives; no other instruction asks it. For now it assumes extent for anything it is applied to.
- **Contract** takes two bindings of one object under the same parent and combines them: the `ConnectedTo` edge between them is dissolved, and any other `ConnectedTo` edge on the absorbed binding is rewritten onto the survivor. Compiles to the edge dissolves and rewrites plus `joinPresenceBindings`. It is a thing a player does ("coil the rope back in"), narrated, with its own preconditions; see [OD-6](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) for what that does to the automatic joins.
- **Move** names a **view**: its moved referent is one binding of the object (`GroundedPresenceBinding`, `{ host, presence }`), and `from` is the parent that binding leaves, not the object's host. A take of a rope that spans two rooms takes the binding in the actor's room, and the rope stays extended with the actor holding one end. A whole move of a one-binding object is the degenerate case.
- **Drape and spread are extend plus move.** A tablecloth over a table is two parts, one hanging in the room and one lying on the table, not one view seen from two parents: what a binding is seen from follows containment (the table is in the room), so the room sees both parts either way, and a plate on the cloth belongs to the part on the table.
- **A shared binding (one binding, several parents) is for overlapping views of one container**, not for parts: [PR-C5](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/manipulation/membership/presenceParentSets.payoff.test.ts)'s backgammon set sits under two bindings of the one steamer chest. The spread was the only writer of a parent set spanning hosts. **Candidate invariant:** a binding's parents are all bindings of one host. Checked and enforced, or recorded as unholdable, in [Slice 5](#recommended-order).

**Extend and contract rhyme with split and join but differ** (the user's expectation, recorded so the slices test it): split partitions a cover and knows nothing of topology, while extend must say where the new binding sits in the whole's `ConnectedTo` topology (extending the middle of a rope that already runs A to B must say which piece keeps the edge to A); join is bookkeeping the planner writes unasked, while contract is a player act that must rewrite the absorbed binding's edges. Split and join check only that the bindings they name exist; extend checks extent, contract checks that the two bindings are connected.

## Licensing: a Prototype, with its rollback trigger

Per [Graduation tiers](../../../../AGENT.designVariant.md#graduation-tiers-when-a-decision-licenses-code), stated before the code exists:

- **Classified:** Prototype. EA-12 is answered but not corpus-grounded; building this is how its evidence arrives.
- **Dependency tag:** the [Progress](#progress) table lists every file each slice touches, so the rollback set stays known. No plan-ID markers in code.
- **Rollback trigger (an invariant, not a cost):** *every `ConnectedTo` edge lives in its whole's own graph, with both endpoints that whole's parts or bindings.* If a fiction forces a `ConnectedTo` edge that cannot satisfy this, the topology/peer distinction is unholdable and the kind rolls back to a `Custom` label. Cost or size surprises are measurements, not triggers.

## Not in scope

- **The tug read** (tug-driven `ludicCache` extension along `ConnectedTo`). The motivating consumer, and the next plan once this one ships. It also needs a cache read rooted at several rooms.
- **Wholes with named parts:** the carve-out walk that reads `ConnectedTo` to decide which parts go to which binding's cover, including edges that join a part to a binding ([OD-5](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only)). This plan's wholes are partless, so extend *writes* the edge and nothing yet *reads* it to place parts.
- **PR-12's cascade** (re-partitioning a split whole's own contents to depth). Partless wholes have no contents to re-partition.
- **Extending the middle of a whole that is already extended** (which piece keeps which `ConnectedTo` edge). Slice 1 extends a one-binding whole only; the first fiction that extends a middle names the rule.
- **Manner** (spliced versus continuous) and **rigid versus slack**: no label slot on `ConnectedTo` in this plan.
