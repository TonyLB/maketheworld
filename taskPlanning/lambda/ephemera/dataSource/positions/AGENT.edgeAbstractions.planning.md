## Why this is a separate plan, and why it is opened now

**The trigger, from conversation 2026-08-24: [AB-57](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#open-decisions-design--plan-only) has been languishing because it is a large implementational ask with no scratchpad for working out what is being *asked*.**

That is the parent's own methodological failure mode, named in its own words:

> *What must the model be able to express?* is answerable from the fiction with confidence. *What is the representation?* is not, and depends on the first. **A row asking both makes the answerable half unreachable.**

**AB-57's title asks both halves in one sentence** --- *"What does a boundary crossing have to deliver, **and** what structure carries it?"* --- and it has stayed open since 2026-08-20 with a four-instance drift documented against it. The [size limit rule](../../../../AGENT.designVariant.md#open-decisions-design--plan-only) says a row that resists should be checked for exactly this conflation and split. **This plan is that split, taken one step further than a second row**, because the requirement half turns out to have at least six questions in it and the representation half at least three.

**The second reason is that the subject is wider than a crossing.** The conversation that opened this plan reframed AB-57's crossing as one instance of a general question --- *representing edges at different levels of abstraction* --- which also covers the exit that licenses a room-to-room span ([AB-26](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#open-decisions-design--plan-only)), the interior edge whose label points outward ([PQ-8](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused)), and the unspecified *arrangement* that two parent rows already reason about ([AB-58](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#open-decisions-design--plan-only) finding 5). **A row is the wrong container for a subject that four other rows are each seeing one face of.**

## Two scales of one edge

**Stated first because every row below is phrased in it, and because it is the formulation whose failure would fail them together.**

**This section is an *exhibit*, not evidence, and the distinction is load-bearing.** It exists to establish vocabulary, so a case read under it will look like it confirms the formulation whatever it actually shows. **The gradeable version of this same fiction is [EA-C1](AGENT.edgeAbstractions.corpus.planning.md#ea-c1-the-power-cord-and-the-flashlight)**, in the corpus, with its falsification targets named --- **including its own admission that it does not discriminate on [EA-1](#settled-register).** Argue rows against the case, never against this section.

The worked case, from conversation 2026-08-24. A `PowerCord` has parts `Cord` and `StrippedEnd`. `Cord` is in the Room; `StrippedEnd` is in the Flashlight; the cord is therefore **multi-hosted**. At part scale the edges are unproblematic:

```
Cord         -[ConnectedTo]-> StrippedEnd     (interior to PowerCord)
StrippedEnd  -[SolderedTo]->  BatteryCase     (crosses into Flashlight)
BatteryCase  -[PartOf]->      Flashlight
```

**The question is what relationship `PowerCord` itself has with `Flashlight` itself, and how that reflects the connections of their parts.**

**The working formulation:** the whole-scale relation and the part-scale relations are **two abstraction levels observing the same underlying truth**, in exactly the way a whole and its parts already are under the locked frame. Not one part-level truth plus a whole-level cache. Not a coarse label licensed upward and never read back down. **Two-way.**

**Three things follow, and each is a row rather than a consequence.**

- **The parent's stated error shape is wrong under this formulation, and that is the sharpest early consequence.** AB-57 part (3) reads the desire path as *"a **peer** edge (`ThreadsInto`, a `Custom`) to express what a **hosting** edge already says"* --- i.e. `In` minus a leftover attribute. Under this formulation `ThreadsInto` is **not** `In` with manner attached. `In` is the whole-scale view of *hosting*; `ThreadsInto` is the whole-scale view of the part-scale **peer** edges. **Two different edges at the same scale, not one edge missing a field.** That reframes *decide manner first* into [EA-2](#settled-register) and [EA-3](#ea-3), which are different questions.
- **This is the edge-side statement of [AB-58](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#open-decisions-design--plan-only)'s finding (5)** --- *"this entire plan family exists to take several **nodes** and abstract them into a containing pattern ... there is no construct that takes several peer edges and names the pattern they form."* That was recorded as a gap. This plan treats it as the subject.
- **The same relation has a second, already-argued end.** Ariadne's thread spans two rooms; the exit from `ROOM#A` to `ROOM#B` is complicit in whatever whole-thread-scale representation exists in either room's `ludicGraph`. AB-57 part (4)(i) already isolated the axis --- *"same coupling, opposite polarity"* --- and filed it as an axis to note. **Under this formulation it is one relation with two ends**, which is a unification rather than a note. **It is also the thing most likely to be wrong**, and [EA-1](#settled-register) is where it is graded.

**Do not resolve this in prose.** It is [EA-1](#settled-register), it is a requirement question before it is a representation one, and every other row leans on it --- so a fault here fails them together and no amount of case-writing in this vocabulary would catch it.

### The one inherited clause that most changes how this plan should be read

**AB-26 raises the burden, and it is the clause a fresh reader is most likely to argue past.** The tempting disposal of every row here is *the part-scale edges already say it, so do not represent the whole-scale one.* **AB-26 rejected that argument one layer down** --- two ropes in adjacent rooms are not connected, so an explicit edge is legitimate *even where the structure could imply the connection*. A row here that closes on derivability must either say why a **scale** relation differs from an **adjacency** relation, or inherit AB-26's answer.
