## Discussion record

Reasoning trail behind the Open-decisions rows, kept for provenance. Not needed to know what to do next --- only *why* the questions are shaped the way they are, and what a future reader would otherwise have to re-derive. Following the pattern of [`AGENT.backtrackChannel.planning.md`](../actions/AGENT.backtrackChannel.planning.md)'s History section.

**Seeded 2026-08-05 from conversation.** The initiative began from a scalability observation: the current treatment of objects is a fine starting point, but complex manipulation on top of it would become unworkably burdensome to reason about --- for an LLM in particular. The proposed answer was aggregations reasoned about in summary, at levels both above and below the human-convenience scale of objects. Four seed examples were given (place setting, dismantled flashlight, powered door, rope through several rooms) and are recorded above.

**The examples were immediately suspected of not being one structure.** Three axes were separated on first pass --- composition, functional aggregate carrying state, multi-host extent --- and AB-1 exists because collapsing them prematurely, or keeping them separate unnecessarily, are equally live failure modes. Notably, the place setting and the flashlight are **mirror images of one relation**, differing only in which level pre-existed and which was improvised: the place setting's members are primary and the whole is minted, the flashlight's whole is primary and the parts are minted.

**A factual correction, 2026-08-05.** An earlier draft asserted that multi-host presence "breaks the at-most-one-host invariant" and that `repairObjectPlacementDrift` "exists to eliminate it." Both were overstated; the verified position is now in [Multi-host presence](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#multi-host-presence-what-is-actually-true-today). The correction materially changed AB-8: framing (a) is far cheaper than it first appeared, so extent's cost is dominated by the modelling choice rather than by any storage obstacle.

**The granularity insight, 2026-08-05 --- the pivot the rest of the design turns on.** A rope coiled in a backpack is a plain object: no sub-part is separately addressable, and pulling it from the pack yields the whole coil by default. Lowered into an abyss, room-level atomicity breaks down and the ends become distinct loci. **The rope did not change; the useful granularity did.** That yields a candidate criterion --- decompose when parts become independently addressable or independently positioned --- which runs *symmetrically* in both directions: the place setting aggregates because its members stopped being separately interesting, the rope decomposes because its parts started being. Evidence that aggregation and decomposition are one axis rather than two mechanisms.

**Consequences drawn the same day.** (i) The transition must run backwards --- re-coiling the rope must restore one object --- which the flashlight example hides and which makes reabsorption mandatory rather than optional (AB-17). (ii) Rope parts are unlike flashlight parts: homogeneous slices of a continuum versus heterogeneous separable things (AB-16). This weakened an earlier claim in this plan that AB-8's segment framing cheaply collapses into AB-4. **[Corrected later the same day: the "ordered along a path, load-bearing for `pull`" half of this was wrong --- see the AB-26 entry below.]** (iii) Tying the rope to a rock puts a relation on an *end*, not the whole --- which under a single multi-room node leaves the relational step's host ambiguous, and under segments is cleanly same-host. Evidence for segments, arrived at from the relational side rather than the positional one.

**The reframe, 2026-08-05 --- AB-12.** Asking *which* things can be promoted led to the observation that `<Object>`, `<Room>`, and `<Feature>` are **already abstractions**: a battery is an object, so is the flashlight it is part of, so is the casing that is part of that. There is no natural floor or ceiling, so pinning `<Object>` as a privileged origin with `<Part>` below and `<Abstraction>` above is not philosophically sound --- it would build two hierarchies for one relation. **The question may therefore not be "what is this strange `Abstraction` type" but "how do we mint and reabsorb Objects at different levels of abstraction, as they are needed."** Recorded as a candidate, deliberately not adopted: deciding is premature and there is useful discussion left.

Two objections were raised against the reframe and are carried as their own rows rather than resolved. **AB-13:** "levels" invites a scalar, but composition has overlap and multiple parents, so the shape is a partial order, not a ladder. **AB-12's own second half:** Room is a *host*, not a node, so extending the reframe to Room and Feature unifies hosts with nodes --- AB-2's most radical answer --- and contradicts the documented decision that Features are deliberately narrower participants. It would, in exchange, *explain* that narrowing rather than assert it (a part cannot be membership-moved away from its whole without an act of decomposition). Object and Room/Feature are to be weighed as separable bets.

**Two further candidate answers recorded, not adopted.** *Minting triggers (AB-14):* operational necessity and attention are unlike needs, and keeping them separate --- operations mint durably, attention selects a projection level and mints nothing --- would prevent description-time LLM inference from becoming a write path. *Authoring (AB-15):* if no level is ontologically privileged, an authored `<Object>` is plausibly the **default binding level** for unqualified reference, revisable by circumstance, which preserves a real role for authoring without pinning the ontology.

**AB-14 reshaped, 2026-08-05 --- Improvisation and Recognition.** The row's first framing (operational necessity vs. attention) was superseded on the observation that **attention is not a minting trigger at all** --- it was a projection concern in a trigger's clothing, and the row therefore described one minting operation plus a note about description. The replacement pair, motivated by the necessity of narration and the structure of the knowledge-representation system, is two genuine operations, both write-side and both consequences of player action: **Improvisation** invents detail an in-flight operation requires but nobody has authored (the flashlight's interior under a screwdriver), and **Recognition** notices that a player's arrangement has composed into something above the level they were working at (the snare trap). The displaced guardrail --- description selects among existing levels and never creates one --- moved to AB-9, where it belongs and where it is more legible as scope control.

The narration obligations were what distinguished the two most sharply. Improvisation's narration **is** the minting event: the battery and the corroded bulb come into existence for the player by being described. Recognition's narration is a **contract with the player**: it is how they learn the world has accepted their construction as meaningful, and without it a player cannot tell whether a clever arrangement counts for anything and will re-attempt it looking for magic phrasing. This is the argument that Recognition can never be a silent internal summarization pass, and it is why AB-19 (description vs. mechanism) is a real fork rather than a shading.

**Two asymmetries recorded the same day, both loading onto Recognition** (AB-18): Improvisation is *bounded by its operation* while Recognition is an unbounded search over arrangements possibly separated in time; and a wrong Improvisation is cheap (plausible detail for a player who asked for detail) while a wrong Recognition changes what exists and what is subsequently legal. The candidate that would resolve much of both at once --- restricting recognition-for-mechanism to an **authored pattern vocabulary** --- is recorded on AB-19, along with its cost: players could then only construct what someone anticipated.

**AB-14 refined again, 2026-08-05 --- the pair was marking a real distinction but naming it wrongly.** The *action* is improvisation in **both** cases: a sufficiently confident recognition of cups, plates and flatware mints a `Table Setting` component of which the others become parts, which is the same act as minting a north wall. What actually differs is **when the mint must happen, and that is downstream of whether a pending operation is blocked on it.** "Lean the table against the north wall" cannot resolve until the referent grounds --- synchronous, on the command's critical path. A loop of rope laid on a road may be understood as a snare immediately, thirty seconds later, or never, with no material loss of responsiveness. **Same persistence layer, potentially very different decision processes** --- which the previous session's finding independently supports, since `ASSET#IMPROVISATION` is a merge layer and therefore renders provenance invisible downstream.

**Timing is the symptom; criticality is the axis.** Preferring the second framing is not pedantry --- it predicts three things the timing framing does not, each now carried as its own row. **(AB-20, failure semantics):** an on-demand mint is a correctness obligation whose failure fails the command, while recognition is discretionary enrichment whose failure owes nobody anything --- and both disciplines already ship side by side in the objects lane. **(AB-20, authorization):** on-demand minting needs no confidence gate because *the player's reference licenses the existence*, whereas recognition volunteers an unprompted claim --- so AB-18's false-positive cost follows from authorization rather than being an arbitrary policy asymmetry. **(AB-18, convergence):** the mode is a property of the moment, not the thing --- "check the snare" puts recognition on the critical path, so one mint is reachable from both a blocking and a deferred path and the two must converge.

**AB-19 reframed, 2026-08-05 --- the abstraction bounds the LLM step, it does not replace it.** The row's earlier proposal (recognition-for-mechanism made tractable by an **authored pattern vocabulary with behavior attached**) is rejected on the grounds that it compiles fictional physics into a deterministic structural context. That is the wrong tool --- we are not doing physics simulation, and it is not what the fiction is about anyway --- and deriving a snare's result from first principles on every interaction is a fool's errand regardless. **Most player-assembled contraptions will require an LLM step to reason about consequences; the design goal is to give that step the right information so it is bounded and manageable.**

Description and mechanism therefore separate by **stakes, not machinery**: recognizing a place setting is a pleasant first pass over cogitation with no causally complicated consequences, and stays pure projection under AB-9's guardrail; recognizing a snare *functionally*, and reasoning about its function in the abstract, is **essential**. This is a cleaner allocation than the previous framing, and it lets AB-9's no-creation rule stand rather than straining it --- description never mints, mechanism mints because something must reason over the result.

**Coyote named as the existing first-iteration attempt at exactly this** --- flawed and ad hoc by its own author's description, and worth reading before designing. The generalizable core (annotate at mint time; closed structural vocabulary with open narrative content; graded aptness; multi-hop structured parse; internal-only reasoning handoff) and the specific ad-hoc parts (no assembly-level object at all, room-scoped rather than contraption-scoped context, boundedness that comes from a tiny world and a closed genre rather than from structure) are written up in [Coyote as prior art](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#coyote-as-prior-art-bounded-llm-reasoning-that-is-not-bounded-structurally). Two rows came out of it: **AB-19** re-aimed at "what must an abstraction carry to bound an LLM step," and **AB-22** on annotation timing and vocabulary --- including the observation that member affinities are a cheap filter for *whether an arrangement is worth recognizing at all*, which attacks AB-18's unbounded search without needing a pattern library.

**Scheduling substrate and situational meaning, 2026-08-05 (two findings from one exchange).** First, `mtw.ephemera.thinking` was named as the intended home for deferred recognition --- its future possibility of work "queued for consideration as time allows, but foregrounded by necessity if the results suddenly become needed" is exactly what tier 2 requires. Verification found the **persistence and vocabulary shipped and the execution model absent** (no `claimed` producer, no claimer, every job bootstrapped and completed in one synchronous invocation), plus one free win: `Meta::Result`'s `nonCollidingPutItem` first-write-wins already satisfies AB-18's convergence requirement. Detail: [`thinking` as the scheduling substrate](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#thinking-as-the-scheduling-substrate-shipped-ledger-unbuilt-queue).

Second, and more consequential: **AB-22's vocabulary question was posed statically and should not have been.** "Is the vocabulary general, or authored per world?" has no good answer because relevant meaning is **situational and varies within a world** --- ambushed by goblins, meaning is combat-relative; dining at the Faerie Court, the place setting's causal complexity becomes very real. Coyote shortcuts this legitimately, as an early iteration should, because its genre supplies a constant interpretive context. Two consequences: mint-time annotation can only be a partial answer (durable base plus situational overlay), and the member-affinity *filter* is itself context-dependent. Recorded as **AB-23**, with **AB-24** for the proposed low-immediacy scheduled task that watches for frame shifts and parameterizes recognition. Note the second deliberately-deferred axis this conversation has landed on: the render cache's `n` / mark-state axis is **built and unpopulated** (`markState` pinned to `[]`, `SITUATION#DEFAULT` only, per iteration 10), and situational *meaning* is that same axis raised from rendering to interpretation.

**Part connections corrected, 2026-08-05 --- adjacency-mediated, not sequenced (AB-26, AB-27).** This plan had twice claimed extent parts are "ordered along a path." Wrong, and wrong in a way already diagnosed one layer up: it imposes a **scalar where a graph belongs**, the same error AB-13 records for levels. A sequence only looks right because a rope is linear; a net spanning four rooms, wiring from a generator to a powered door, and two ropes knotted together all branch. The correct shape is *"the clifftop rope end connects to the shaft section **by way of** the 'into the abyss' exit"* --- a graph whose edges are labeled by the spatial relation that licenses them. Branching is then free, the edge **explains** the adjacency instead of asserting it, narration takes direction and wording from the exit (as the shipped move compiler already does with `exitName`), and `pull` becomes graph propagation rather than an index walk. Area edge kinds beyond Exit were **already** documented as able to carry non-traversable spatial facts ("north of" without a door), which is exactly what a rope through a floor-hole needs --- a third anticipated axis this design lands on.

Two rules proposed with it: connections are **explicit but constrained** (adjacency *licenses* an edge, never derives one --- two ropes in adjacent rooms are not connected), and an edge must reference **world truth** rather than `projectRoomExits`' perspective-filtered output, or a rope's physical shape would vary by observer. **AB-27** then notes the encouraging corollary: parts sharing a host can use the *existing* intra-host relational vocabulary, so the new edge kind is needed **exactly and only where `applyStepSequenceCore` already throws** --- a sign of a real joint rather than a parallel mechanism.

**Rule (ii) corrected within the hour, and the correction matters more than the fix.** It was first written as "the **authored Exit** edge," which collapsed **three** independent things into one: *world truth* (the real constraint), *Exit* (one kind of spatial relation), and *authored* (one provenance). The licensing relation may be **any kind** --- an Exit edge hard-coding a connection affordance, **or** an adjacency/nesting relation, such as going over the rail of a boat and ending up in the bay --- and **any provenance**, authored **or improvised**, since an improvised relation is world truth the moment it is minted. Only the *projection* is excluded.

Three consequences followed. **(a)** The boat case is the deferred containment operator arriving: `In`/`inside` was explicitly excluded from relational v1 (BD-2) and parked for "a future nested container operator," and a rope over the rail needs exactly that relation to license it. **(b)** Nesting does **double duty** --- it structures the containment tree *and* licenses a boundary-crossing connection --- so AB-25 and AB-26 share a substrate, though not identically, since an Exit gives adjacency with no containment either way. **(c)** Improvised provenance has a concrete gap: an improvised area-level adjacency needs an **ephemera-side** Area graph, which does not exist because `EphemeraAreaId` does not exist --- the same missing piece P1 requires, so one gap with two consumers. Once it exists, mixing provenances is cheap, because participation-stack merge already does exactly that. And **AB-28** captures the failure mode neither framing had named: licensing relations are **mutable**, so a repaired floor-hole strands the rope threaded through it --- probably content rather than drift.

**AB-28 recognized as an instance of a shipped pattern, 2026-08-05 --- and it deflates from "new problem" to "existing discipline, extended."** The mutable-licence question is the same shape as the boundary-edge rule already in production: *a mutation that would sever a relationship is not legal until the severing is made explicit.* Assert-and-throw with no silent-strip fallback, `boundaryEdgeOutcomes` classification, expansion-classifies-compiler-sequences, dissolves ordered ahead of the mutation. The three outcomes guessed for the rope-through-a-sealed-hole case turned out to **be** the existing verdict channels --- `error`, `dissolve`, `defer` --- rather than needing new ones. A convergence worth noting: `defer` already exists as the verdict meaning *needs LLM judgment*, which is exactly the bounded LLM step AB-19 argues contraption consequences require. The classification vocabulary anticipated it.

**AB-29 carries what does not carry over**, and its bifurcation is the substantive finding: **play-driven** licence removal has an operation to block or transform (so the discipline applies and the conflict is content), while an **asset-withdrawn** licence has no operation to make illegal at all --- nobody did anything --- which is the **eviction ladder's** exact situation one layer over, and wants trim-and-repair rather than legality. An earlier guess in this plan that the whole case is "content, not drift" was therefore half right.

**The reverse edge-reference index is now the initiative's most load-bearing parked item.** Three independent lines of reasoning reached it on the same day: cross-host aggregate membership (AB-6/AB-8), part-connections referencing a licensing relation (AB-26), and the backwards discovery a licence-removing mutation requires (AB-29). Recorded in [Parked extension points](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#parked-extension-points-this-initiative-would-unpark) with that annotation, because a slice that unparks it pays for itself three times.

**A pattern in this plan's own errors, worth watching.** Twice now a rule has privileged one *instance* of a category as though it were the category: `<Object>` over parts-and-aggregates (corrected by AB-12), and authored-Exit over spatial relations generally (corrected here). Adjacent to, but distinct from, the scalar-where-a-graph-belongs error recorded at AB-13 and AB-26. Both are worth checking for explicitly the next time a rule is proposed.

**And a complication surfaced by the wall itself (AB-21).** The north wall may not be *invented*: a room has walls whether or not anyone wrote them down, exactly as the flashlight always had a battery. Materializing something always implied is different from conjuring something new, and it carries an **identity obligation** --- two references to "the north wall" in one room must yield the same wall, across Lambda cold starts included, or repeated reference silently multiplies the world. `stableKey` on `Meta::Object` already solves this shape of problem for Coyote correlation and is the obvious hook. Recognition largely escapes this obligation; on-demand minting cannot.

**Naming check resolved, 2026-08-05: it is the objects lane's mechanism, raised from ad-hoc handling of one kind of thing into a general mechanism named for its purpose.** Verified against [`objects/AGENT.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/objects/AGENT.md), and the confirmation was stronger than the question assumed: `ASSET#IMPROVISATION` is an **asset layer in the participation stack**, not a flag on a special object kind, so improvised components already merge and render exactly as authored ones do. The general purpose was therefore already implemented and merely unnamed. Consequences --- what arrives pre-built, the sharpening of AB-12 into a cost question, three bounded gaps, and the naming fork now carried on AB-0 --- are in [Improvisation is the objects lane's mechanism, generalized](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#improvisation-is-the-objects-lanes-mechanism-generalized) rather than here, because that content outlives this record.

~~**Settled, 2026-08-05:** extents spanning hosts of different kinds (two adventurers holding opposite ends of a rope in different rooms) is not a concern.~~ **Reopened later the same day by H1's adoption** --- see AB-8. Under framing (a) it really is a non-issue; under H1 it is a question about whether the containment tree admits characters as interior nodes. **The general lesson is the durable part: adopting a hypothesis silently re-poses questions that were settled against the model it replaced, and nothing marks them as stale.** Sweep the pre-H1 settled notes deliberately rather than trusting them.

**Phase 0's first sub-step run, 2026-08-05 --- mining the ladder's data-blocked parks, and it paid for itself in corrections.** Four cases landed ([C1](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c1-look-at-the-character-standing-here-look-at-the-north-wall)--[C4](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c4-the-place-setting-at-the-faerie-court)), one per park. Three of them falsified something this plan had asserted, which is the argument for having mined before inventing: an invented case cannot correct the plan, because it is built out of the plan's own assumptions.

**(1) The membership compile gate.** `evaluateComplexityPreGates` returns `multiPresent` for `containers.length > 1` and maps it to terminal player-facing error copy. So this plan's "the actual single-host pressure is the end-state planner" was understated --- the gate is harder, earlier, and the only one of the pressures a player can see. Found by reading BD-16(3)'s own code, which is exactly the park that named it.

**(2) The non-uniformity is worse than "not uniform across routes."** The plan recorded that the cross-host move route bypasses the end-state planner. Verification found **three different rules on three routes**: membership compile refuses, relational compile silently leaves the object hostless (`if (containers.length === 1)`), the move route does neither. That inconsistency is a defect on its own terms, independent of how multi-host resolves, and Phase 1's reconciliation sub-step now carries it.

**(3) AB-23's premise was wrong in a load-bearing way.** "The situational axis is built and unpopulated" suggested a switch awaiting a flip. It is populated for Rooms and structurally absent elsewhere: non-Room hosts are pinned to empty mark state **because they have no `Meta::Room` to carry it**. That converts AB-23 from "turn on an existing axis" into "there is no state carrier below room scale," which is AB-7 --- so AB-7 gains a second consumer and is less likely to be out of scope than the row currently guesses.

Two findings that corrected nothing but sharpened a case. **Coyote's limit is not scoping, it is projection:** `CoyoteStagedObject` carries no edges and the snapshot never reads `graph.relationalEdges`, so a rope tied to a branch and the same objects in a heap yield byte-identical prompt context. Widening or narrowing the scope of a set that cannot express relation does not help --- which strengthens the [Coyote as prior art](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#coyote-as-prior-art-bounded-llm-reasoning-that-is-not-bounded-structurally) conclusion by locating the gap precisely. And **CPG-5's dead filter documents itself**: `compileDescribeFromSkeleton`'s `isEphemeraObjectId` guard carries a comment calling itself "a no-op guard," because nothing upstream can produce a candidate for it to reject.

**One finding against Phase 0's own method.** C4 has no description-vs-mechanism value: at an inn the place setting is inert, at the Faerie Court it is causally loaded. **AB-19's tag is a property of *(case, frame)*, not of the case**, so the per-case annotation column cannot hold it and Phase 2 must not read that tally as a partition. Deliberately **not** given a new AB row --- AB-23 already states that the member-affinity filter is context-dependent, and this is the same claim arriving from the corpus side. Noted here because the temptation to allocate AB-30 for it was real, and the discipline that a corpus case should *sharpen* rows rather than *breed* them is worth holding visibly.

**P1 promoted to a working hypothesis, 2026-08-05 --- and the posture change is the part worth recording.** This plan has run on *deciding is premature*, and that has been right: most of these rows still have useful discussion in them. What was noticed is that a question can be under-discussed for the opposite reason too --- with no grounding assumption, downstream rows have nothing to argue against and the discussion stays abstract rather than premature. **The middle move is to adopt a candidate explicitly as a test of how well it grounds further design**, tag everything derived from it, and keep the derived set rollback-able as a unit. If H1 dies, the trail says which answers die with it and where to branch. Recorded in [Working hypotheses](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#working-hypotheses); a reader who finds the lock and reads it as drift should find this paragraph first.

**Why H1 and not framing (a), stated carefully because the obvious argument does not work.** The tempting one --- *the route non-uniformity means any consolidated representation beats this* --- is true about the inconsistency and **symmetric** between the two consolidations, so it selects nothing. What selects H1 is asymmetric **cost**: five sites become correct as written under H1 and all five need widening under (a). Five independent decisions for single-host is weak evidence single-host is natural and strong evidence widening is expensive. Recorded in that shape because the weaker version would equally license the expensive direction, and it is the version that comes to mind first.

**Two things the promotion does not buy, both recorded so the lock is not later defended as more than it was.** It is **not a net simplification**: it retires AB-8 and buys AB-25 --- which needs an ephemera-side `EphemeraAreaId` that does not exist, an upward room -> area index that exists nowhere, and an answer to whether containment is a tree at all --- while making AB-9, the scope-controlling row, acute rather than deferrable. And **surviving is not evidence of being right**, only that nothing has killed it yet. The named rollback triggers exist so that "dead end" is a test rather than a mood.

**The failure mode designed against is sunk cost rather than error**, and the mitigation is ordering: the two falsification cases (born-spanning things, rope state (iv)) move to the **front** of the remaining corpus, so H1 faces its cheapest tests while the rollback set is near-empty. Conditional adoption goes de facto permanent once rollback means redoing fifteen answers, at which point the hypothesis survives on accumulated cost and the section documents a decision nobody will act on.

**And the mechanism was made general rather than a P1 one-off.** AB-12's privileged-level reframe has the same shape --- a candidate that dissolves rows rather than answering them --- and is named as **candidate H2**, deliberately not promoted: two hypotheses in flight would overlap their dependent sets, and a row tagged both cannot be rolled back as a unit, which is the whole mechanism. This is the same promote-a-pattern-rather-than-treat-it-as-coincidence move already applied to AB-15.

**First falsification case run, 2026-08-05 --- and it worked, which is the meta-finding.** [C5, the chain mounted at both ends](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends), was raised in conversation as a substitute for rope state (iv) on the grounds that re-coiling changes co-location and load-bearing **simultaneously** and therefore cannot distinguish them. The chain holds co-location fixed --- everything in one cell --- while the ends stay load-bearing. It falsified P1's speculative "migration-down and reabsorption may be the same event" immediately. **H1 itself is untouched:** nothing in the case bears on single-host objects or on hosting at the containing node, so this is a sub-claim dying, not a hypothesis. That distinction is exactly what the [Working hypotheses](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#working-hypotheses) tagging exists to keep legible, and it held on its first use.

**The plan had already written the right answer and P1 walked it back.** AB-17's criterion has always been a disjunction --- "no part is separately referenced **or** separately positioned" --- and P1 clause 5 tests only the second half. So this is not a new discovery so much as the plan's earlier, more careful statement surviving a collision with its later, sloppier one. Worth recording in that shape: the failure was **collapsing a disjunction**, which is a fourth entry in this plan's own error catalogue alongside scalar-where-a-graph-belongs (AB-13, AB-26) and instance-mistaken-for-category (AB-12, AB-26's rule (ii)).

**The reabsorption guard turned out to be shipped already, and it throws.** Reabsorbing a part removes its node, and `removeObject` asserts and throws `RelationalEdgeStillReferencedError` when a Relational edge still references it (BD-33/35, deliberately no silent-strip variant). So the relational half of "separately referenced" is a **graph query** --- `nodeHasRelationalEdge`, already on the class --- and unsafe reabsorption is already impossible rather than being a hazard this initiative must guard. Third convergence of this kind, after AB-28's and AB-18's; the boundary-edge discipline keeps anticipating cases it was not written for.

**One honest gap, recorded rather than worked around.** C5's more interesting anchor --- chain-end to *wall* --- is not representable at all, because Features are not graph nodes ([C1](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c1-look-at-the-character-standing-here-look-at-the-north-wall)). Chain-end to chest works today; the wall end needs AB-12's Room/Feature half first. The finding rests on the half that can be stated.

**Two consequences drawn the same day.** **(i)** AB-9 escalates from acute to **routine**: load-bearing parts persist while co-located, so anything anchored sits in its host graph as a multi-level structure permanently, and level selection becomes a default read-path concern rather than an edge case. **(ii)** Rollback trigger 3 becomes **more** likely: parts surviving migration means dragging a chained chest out and back cycles the whole up to the Area and down again with structure intact --- a high-frequency cascade on an unchanging structure, where coincident reabsorption would have made cascades rare.

**And a third decomposition trigger, previously unnamed.** The chain's ends exist while everything is co-located, so decomposition was not triggered by spanning --- it was triggered by needing an endpoint **finer than the whole** to bear a relation. That is on-demand Improvisation (AB-14), and it restores the symmetry the granularity insight claimed while moving it off the axis P1 guessed: **parts exist while they are needed as distinct loci, by position or by relation; the whole hosts wherever it must to contain them.** Two independent questions, which P1 had as one.

**"Born-spanning" dissolved under challenge, 2026-08-05 --- and it was this plan's own case, not an inherited one.** The objection: an object can be born spanning without that being a complicated problem. Correct, on two counts. **(i)** [C5](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends) had already absorbed it --- a river's parts are permanently separately-positioned, so the whole stays Area-hosted and migration-down never fires, which is a rule giving a stable answer for a stable thing rather than a rule doing no work. **The same-mechanism requirement, 2026-08-06 --- an argument from requirements that settled more than four constructed scenarios had.** Put as a dichotomy: *you can have a single scale-invariant processing rule or single-hosting, not both*, since a single-hosted spanning whole cannot be a node in each room it passes through, so reaching it needs a second path and a second path is an epicycle. **Substantially right**, and the first pressure on clause 1b to arrive from a requirement rather than a scenario --- which is the case for AB-32 existing, made two exchanges after the row was created.

**The phantom edge, 2026-08-06 --- a conceptual challenge to `positionGraph` itself, and the code confirmed it in a stronger form than it was put.** The claim: hosting creates implicit *contents-of* links that the structure does not record, so they cannot be given nuance --- no `part-of` alongside `contents-of`. **Verified and sharpened three ways.** The link is not merely unlabeled but **untypeable** (`hostId` is a scalar sibling of `nodes`; relational endpoints are both `EphemeraObjectId`, so the host has no node to be one). **The diagnosis that came out of it, and it is the general form: hosting is one construct doing two jobs.** The host union is not arbitrary --- Room and Character are both *things that move while retaining their contents*, which is a real sharding justification and exactly why a pick-up-able box belongs in it. **The roadmap is coherent as storage.** What is incoherent is that the same construct also carries the containment *relation*, which needs a label and a traversal and cannot have either while it is a collection. **Shard by mobility; express containment as edges.** Recorded as [P2](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#proposal-p2-separate-the-storage-shard-from-the-containment-relation), with AB-33 / AB-34 / AB-35 split out.

**A flatten-instead-of-nest alternative was proposed and withdrawn within the same exchange, and the reason is worth more than the proposal.** Letting the room's graph hold `box`, `lantern` and an `In` edge gets uniform traversal with no cross-shard hop --- and **breaks the write path host-binding exists to make cheap**: every pickup migrates N nodes and edges instead of moving one shard. The middle path (nesting constraint + denormalized cache + part-addressed endpoints) keeps shard-mobility *and* uniform traversal by paying for reads in the cache. **The error was optimising the read path in isolation**, which is the same shape as the earlier failure to check whether a cheaper operation leaves a *faithful* state.

**Part-addressed edge endpoints are the load-bearing third clause, and they decouple decomposition from membership.** Four open items clear at once: AB-9's "you see a rope, a rope end, and a rope end," C7's double-binding in room B, C5's permanent three-node cell, C8's flashlight vanishing under inspection. **None of the four was what the mechanism was designed for**, which is the same weak-evidence-of-a-real-joint signal part-mediated reference earned. **Two cautions recorded against over-crediting it:** C5's "permanent multi-level steady state" was an artifact of parts-must-be-nodes rather than a fact about anchored things, so AB-9's escalation rests on a premise P2 removes and must be re-weighed rather than quietly kept; and AB-16's positional disagreement --- the plan's strongest evidence for containment and extent being genuinely distinct --- may be the same artifact, which now makes that evidence **hostage to AB-34**.

**And the clause carries the defect it fixes, one scale down, unless part identity has a home.** An endpoint naming `rope: rope-end` where no such part is declared anywhere is exactly the phantom again. **The reading adopted for the write-up: the address points into the rope's own graph**, the rope hosting its parts as the box hosts its contents --- which makes "objects will be hosts" load-bearing for the part story too, a second independent argument for it. **The unresolved half is cross-host addressing** and is the next topic: ports on the host boundary, connecting outside references to inside addresses (**AB-34**).

**One invariant pinned on the cache, with local precedent that cuts against it.** `mtw-wml`'s schema README records this codebase backing *out* of denormalizing child data upward --- "an enormous source of unnecessary complexity" --- in favour of selectors over the source-of-truth tree. The analogy is limited (authored source, not a runtime cache) but the discipline transfers: **the write path never touches `positionCache`; invalidate-and-rederive only.** Without it the cache becomes a second truth and the phantom returns as a coherence problem one layer up (**AB-35**).

**Ports, 2026-08-06 --- recorded as [P3](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#provisional-p3-ports-as-host-boundary-bindings), provisional, with its questions listed rather than its answers guessed.** The framing that makes it worth designing ahead of H1: **the cross-boundary addressing requirement is common to every hosting model.** A whole at a non-local level needs relations to jump *out* of the room's graph; a whole at a local level needs its parts addressed *in*. Neither direction is removed by any decision about multi-host, single-host, or Area-hosting --- and part relationships in particular either cross a level or need an entirely separate graph system.

**The strongest argument for ports is not the one they were proposed for.** Traversal would work with direct part-addressing too. **What ports add is encapsulation**, and the payoff lands on the two hardest lifecycle rows: under direct addressing, coiling the rope (**AB-17**) or burning it through (**AB-31**) dangles every external reference and forces a cross-host rewrite; under ports the interior repartitions and the ports rebind, so **nothing outside changes.** AB-31 opened by asking what happens to anything holding a reference; the answer is *survives by construction*. **Ports were not designed for either row** --- the third time this design has had a mechanism answer a question it was not aimed at, which is weak evidence of a real joint and has twice been treated as such.

**The root node turned out to be required rather than incidental**, since `part` edges must emanate from the whole. That is **AB-3**'s pre-registered trigger firing a *third* time, independently: composition (where it was written), hosting (AB-33), ports. At three, `CarryClosureFragment = {rootId, members, edges}` is just what a ported object's graph looks like.

**And the "assume multi-host for the moment" framing is not neutral --- it re-prices the three-positions table.** Position 1's charge was C7's where-along objection: segments still required, so room B holds two overlapping nodes with an ambiguous binding. **Ports answer that without single-hosting**, and multi-hosting under ports is cheaper than ever priced, since the rope is *listed* in two rooms but has exactly **one graph** --- closer to a presence index than to real multi-hosting. **Clause 1b takes a new hit: not falsified, but its competitor got materially cheaper.**

**Nine sub-questions recorded as PQ-1..PQ-9, deliberately as questions.** Several have an obvious-looking answer and a leaning is noted, but **the instruction was explicit that first guesses are not to be locked in** --- some have alternatives not yet on the table. The two that are not preferences: **PQ-6**, egress discovery, which nothing indexes, and **PQ-7**, which is the consequence --- `removeObject`'s assert-and-throw is enforceable today only because edges are same-host and locally discoverable, so a cross-host port reference **loses a shipped safety guarantee** rather than merely adding a risk. Both are downstream of the reverse index.

**Which is now AB-36, promoted from parked to prerequisite on its fourth independent appearance.** Stale Exit edges after take-hold (the original), AB-29's backwards discovery from a licensing relation to its dependents, AB-31's fission footprint, and now PQ-6. **Four unrelated consumers wanting the same missing index is the argument** --- recorded so it is not re-derived a fifth time --- and the fourth is what changes its status, because it takes an existing guarantee with it rather than only costing work.

**One consequence worth watching in the other direction:** sibling links inside the whole's graph give **AB-26** a home *below* the rooms instead of above, deflating **AB-25** further. **The loose end is PQ-8** --- those links are labeled by exterior licensing relations, so the label points outward from inside the object, and AB-28's floor-hole repair now reaches a dependent buried one level deeper. Ports do not help there and may make discovery worse.

**PQ-7 withdrawn under a worked scenario, 2026-08-06 --- I had called it uniquely bad and it was misaimed.** The claim was that ports break the same-host locality making `RelationalEdgeStillReferencedError` enforceable. **The scenario that killed it:** a rope tied to a cliff-top boulder puts the boulder, the rope node, *and* the edge `boulder -TiedTo-> OBJECT#ROPE:1` all in one graph, so the existing assert scans one graph and finds it. Requiring every connection --- port-qualified or not --- to be dissolved before removal **is** the shipped contract, unchanged.

**The correction relocated the cost rather than removing it, and that is the useful part.** The right question is *when* a port reference lives on a graph the node is absent from: under **multi-host + ports, never** (the node is in every room that can see a port, so locality holds); under **single-host at an Area + ports, always**. **So the exposure belongs to clause 4, not to P3** --- ports merely made it visible. Logged on the clause-4 row as a new charge. **Second time in this initiative that a cost assigned to a proposal turned out to belong to something the proposal was being compared against**, which is worth watching as a bias rather than a coincidence.

**What survives is not a new discipline but a new discoverability requirement**, and it folds into PQ-6: interior mutation can invalidate an exterior reference (fission releases port 1 while a room still names it), and the fix --- *a host may not release an externally-referenced port* --- is the existing contract one level over, enforceable only with AB-36's index.

**One genuinely bad thing did come out of it, and it is mechanical rather than architectural: PQ-10.** `isEphemeraTaggedId` splits on `#` and requires two sections, so **`OBJECT#ROPE:1` passes `isEphemeraObjectId`** --- the edge parses as well-formed, lands in `relationalEdges`, and then `edge.from === id` compares `'OBJECT#ROPE:1'` to `'OBJECT#ROPE'` and **the assert passes vacuously.** Valid to the parser, invisible to the check, which is worse than a loud rejection; `edgeReferencesObjectId` has the same hole. **And it upgrades PQ-9's separator choice from cosmetic to load-bearing:** `:` is silently swallowed everywhere, while a second `#` throws `Illegal nested EphemeraId` at every unconverted site. **Prefer the separator that fails loudly** --- the same instinct as BD-33/35's no-silent-strip rule.

**The motivation behind ports was stated and recorded: they are an attempt to find out whether the design can do without clause 1, because multi-hosting of wholes looks actually important.** Recorded because the working-hypothesis mechanism depends on knowing why a candidate was built --- **a scheme designed to make a rival framing viable must not be assessed as neutral.** Clause 1b now carries pressure from four directions (the same-mechanism requirement, ports re-pricing position 1, PQ-7's relocation onto clause 4, and a stated judgment that multi-hosting matters), tallied in one place so the four are not each re-derived. **H1 is not withdrawn.** The `H1-dependent` set is recorded there at **six rows** --- AB-6, AB-8, AB-9, AB-11, AB-15, AB-17 --- which is the measurement the tagging test exists to produce, and it says **H1 has been deciding less than its prominence in this document suggests.** Note what is *not* at stake: clause 3 is not uniquely H1's and clause 1a survives a withdrawal, so the live question is 1b and its two entailments, not the hypothesis wholesale. **Caution pinned alongside: do not let ports decide this by momentum.**

**Ports clarified as single-use, 2026-08-06 --- and I had been reading them as general-purpose named interfaces, which distorted several assessments.** One port records **one** ingress/egress between two graphs: `ROOM#A` connecting to both `RopeEnd1` and `RopeSpan1` is **two ports**, not one port with two interior edges.

**The consequence is larger than the clarification sounds: a port can name the graph it exits to, so a whole's own graph enumerates its referrers.** The port list *is* a reverse index, stored where it is needed. **PQ-6 --- the blocking item --- largely dissolves**, and with it PQ-7's residue: fission cannot silently release a port, because the port names `ROOM#A` and obliges the operation to include it. That lands hard rather than conveniently, because `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs **once, up front** and `MultiKeyUpdate` cannot be re-entered, so plan-time footprint computability is the constraint everything here has been shaped by.

**Which means I over-promoted AB-36 one exchange after promoting it.** The promotion rested on four independent consumers; single-use ports serve **two** (PQ-6, and AB-31's fission footprint), leaving stale Exit edges and AB-29's licensing-relation dependents untouched. **The reusable lesson is about the argument rather than the index: a recurrence count was doing the work of a proof, and a design change halved it overnight.** Four appearances is evidence something is needed; it is never evidence about *what*. The "do not re-derive a fifth time" note was struck.

**And it forced a correction to the encapsulation argument, which was this proposal's strongest claim.** I had written into AB-17 and AB-31 that external references "survive by construction." **Half true.** Ports make **interior repartitioning** non-breaking --- AB-17's coiling rebinds port 1 to the root inside the rope, and that payoff is real *because reabsorption preserves identity*. They do **not** make **identity changes** non-breaking: AB-31's fission turns the rope into `ROPE-upper` and `ROPE-lower`, so an external edge naming `OBJECT#ROPE:1` must be rewritten regardless of ports. **AB-31 gains the footprint half instead --- the reverse of what was recorded.**

**Two rows got better in ways the clarification was not aimed at.** **PQ-2** collapses into one record shape with one field varying --- a port's exterior end is *a graph plus optionally a local endpoint*, so attachment and presence are the same record, not two mechanisms. **And that answers half of AB-6 in a carrier neither of its options contemplated: presence stored *in the whole*, as its port list** --- neither a stored container set on the part nor a predicate derived over room edges, which is why the row's two-option framing never reached it. **PQ-3** mostly stops being a question: a coiled rope has one presence port and a spread one has three, so the count tracks reality with no transition to trigger, and the residual N-ports-to-one-room redundancy is the C5 case, which is a render concern where the leaning already put it.

**The new sharp question is PQ-1, widened: which graph owns the port record?** Single-use makes this harder rather than easier, because the port is no longer an indirection --- it **is** the relationship, so "where does the kind live" is a special case. Stored once in the whole means `ROOM#A` cannot answer "what is here and how is it connected" without opening every object present, on the commonest read path in the system; split halves means a dual write across two shards and the `repairObjectPlacementDrift` drift class again.

**PQ-1 settled 2026-08-06, and the answer avoided the trade the question appeared to force.** I had framed the fork as *stored once in the whole* (cheap writes, expensive room reads) versus *split halves* (the `repairObjectPlacementDrift` drift class). **The proposal is a third thing: split into halves holding *complementary* facts** --- an **egress list** on the interior graph (port -> outbound host) and an **ingress list** of port numbers on the exterior node. Neither half restates the other, so the hot read stays local: a room answers *what is here and how is it connected* without opening the object, and opening it is required only to learn *which part* a port lands on. **Detail costs a hop; presence and attachment do not** --- position 2 with the zero-hop case actually zero.

**Two defaults came with it, and the first is the stronger half of the whole port design.** *Failed addressing coarsens rather than dangles:* an edge to `OBJECT#BAG:4` with no legal port 4 resolves identically to an edge to `OBJECT#BAG`, per-hop. **That is a different answer to PQ-6/PQ-7 than an index gives** --- an index *prevents* dangling references, coarsening makes them *benign*, and having both is why the residual risk is now small. *Second:* a port with no exterior edge is a pure presence indicator, which is better than the optional-endpoint field I had proposed because **the absence of an edge is the signal** --- no second record shape.

**Resolved: the interior egress list is authoritative, and the ingress list is accepted as a denormalization inside truth that can drift.** Authority follows from what the halves are for --- the egress list must be complete for plan-time footprint computation, and it is the side whose lifecycle invalidates ports. The ingress list is fully derivable, so it is a materialized view living in `positionGraph` rather than in cache; **that was chosen over putting it in `positionCache` because the room's local read is too hot to depend on cache warmth**, and the precedent is direct (`Meta` placement is already a dual write with "`positionGraph` wins" and a repair sweep).

**Accepting drift creates an obligation, tracked rather than waved at: PQ-11.** The conflict rule follows from authority; the **repair story is open and nothing does this today.** One asymmetry worth exploiting: the ingress list is derivable from the egress lists of objects present, so repair is a **recompute rather than a merge** --- materially easier than `repairObjectPlacementDrift`'s. **And AB-35's scope needed clarifying**: its never-a-second-truth rule was written for `positionCache`, while this is derived data placed deliberately *inside* `positionGraph`. Not a violation, but the plan now has two derived-data sites under different rules, so the general principle was stated --- *derived data may live in truth when the read path demands it, provided a conflict rule and a repair story both exist.*

**One inference blocked before it could be drawn.** The coarsening default is **read-side only** and does not license relaxing BD-33/35's write-side assert-and-throw. Robust reads make the write check *feel* unnecessary; they do not make it so, because a silently-coarsening world accumulates imprecision nobody notices --- the silent-wrong-answer class this plan keeps choosing against. Smaller instance of the same: coarsening turns "pull this strap" into "pull the whole bag," correctly most of the time and silently always.

**C9 --- coiling the rope, 2026-08-06.** The rope's fourth state, owed since Phase 0 was written, and the first corpus entry that is a **worked operation** rather than a falsification case. Its result is what it does *not* require: hauling a three-room rope in rewrites port egress and touches **no edges at all**. The `part` links and sibling links are invariant. **Structure and position are independent**, which today's model cannot express --- relocating a segment means moving a node between graphs, and same-host-only relational edges force dissolve-and-recreate for any segment carrying a relation.

**The scope of "no edge changes" is the presence-only case, and the fiction endorses the limit.** A span tied to a tree in the room being left leaves that room referencing a port that no longer exits there --- which is `boundaryEdgeOutcomes`' existing carry / dissolve / defer / error verdict, and reads exactly right: *you cannot haul in a rope tied to a tree next door without untying it.* **Machinery and fiction agreeing on where the hard case is is the strongest signal available that a joint is real.**

**Clause 5 has nothing left to do, if multi-host + ports wins.** No whole-migration occurs, because the whole is present wherever its ports say. The queued concurrent-migration and thrash cases would target machinery that does not exist --- **the third queued test set to dissolve rather than run**, which is now frequent enough to be worth naming as a pattern: *this design keeps answering questions by removing the thing that raised them.* Encouraging, and also a reason to check that the removed thing was not load-bearing elsewhere.

**Two rows cleared, and the second needs its credit stated carefully.** **AB-17**'s hosting criterion becomes a one-line read --- "all ports share an egress host" --- and its reabsorption criterion gains a second mechanical clause (*does any port carry an exterior edge*). **AB-15's naming seam dissolves outright:** the room's node **is** `OBJECT#ROPE`, so the rope is present by name everywhere it passes and none of that row's three candidates is needed. **But the seam was created by single-hosting the whole**, so its dissolution is evidence about clause 1b, **not** about AB-15's own question, which is untouched. Recording that distinction matters more than the win --- a row whose *instance* dissolves is not a row that has been answered.

**Two questions the case raised that the scheme does not state. PQ-12:** the far end goes `C -> B` rather than `C -> A` because the rope is continuous, so **relocation follows adjacency** --- and if that is an invariant, AB-26's adjacency licensing appears on the **write** side, restricting where a part may move rather than merely licensing a connection. That is a stronger use of the same relation and should not be assumed to follow from the read-side version. **PQ-13:** the implied membership rule (*present iff a port egresses here*) asks whether a plain lantern carries a presence port. Uniformity makes a simple object the **degenerate case rather than a special case** --- one port, zero hops, the same argument that rescued position 2 --- at the price of ceremony on the commonest object in the game, on the hottest read path.

**PQ-1 reversed 2026-08-06, one exchange after it was settled --- the ingress list belongs in `positionCache`, not in `positionGraph`.** The settled answer had rejected the cache branch because "the room's local read is too hot to depend on cache warmth." **That defended a need the room does not have:** its own edges already carry what a coarse read wants, since `boulder -TiedTo-> ROPE:4` names the rope and "a rope is tied to the boulder" renders without any ingress list. The list's unique contribution is *which* ports are live locally, presence-only ones included --- a where-along fact, and **detail costing a hop was the whole deal.** The thing put into truth to protect the hot path was not on it.

**The general form, which is the durable part: judge a denormalization by what it saves.** The ingress list is **weak** --- per record it surfaces information from **one** item, saving one read of an object already known present (**1:1**). Membership is **strong** --- it saves scanning every object in the world (**N:1**). Strong belongs in truth; weak belongs in a read-heavy index. **That line also fixes what would otherwise have been a bad inference from C9's rule:** *present iff a port egresses here* is a **consistency invariant** between the egress list and membership, **not** a derivation a reader performs --- if membership were derived too, answering "what is here" would mean scanning the world.

**And there is a principled reading rather than merely a tidier one: ingress-in-truth was position 3 growing back.** Materialising a lookup answer into truth so the common case need not traverse, while the uncommon case traverses, is *two rules where one would do*. That is the shape this plan already caught itself in once by accident --- **second occurrence of the same instinct producing the same defect**, which is worth more than either instance.

**Three consequences, one of them a whole question removed.** **PQ-11 is retired the day it was opened** --- no denormalization in truth means no drift and no repair story owed; the one observation worth keeping (a derived list is repaired by **recompute, not merge**) moved to AB-35 as a property to design its contract around. **AB-35 goes back to one derived-data site with one clean rule**, reversing the two-sites note added hours earlier, and gains the ingress list as its first named consumer. **PQ-13's cost objection loses its hot-path half** --- with ports living only in the object's own graph, a plain lantern's presence port costs a room read nothing, so what survives is storage and write-path ceremony, not read cost.

**One refinement on "literally one place," because it locates the transaction footprint.** Relocation *within* the spread is **one record** --- rewrite the port's egress host. Crossing a boundary is **two**, since a room's port count going 0 -> 1 or 1 -> 0 changes membership, which stays in `positionGraph`. Hauling a rope through the middle of its own span is nearly free; only its endpoints cost.

**The reversal also removed the ugliest corner of the branch it replaced.** With the ingress list in truth, a stale entry was a **phantom** --- a port listed on a node the object no longer exits to --- and the coarsening default said nothing about that direction. In cache it cannot happen, and coarsening covers the remaining case (a room's graph cannot tell whether `ROPE:4` is live, so it resolves to `ROPE`). **The two decisions now support each other, which they did not before.**

**Edge kinds across a port need not match --- 2026-08-06, and it reframes what a port is.** The proposition that they should was an **artifact of reasoning from a rope**: `TiedTo` outside and `TiedTo` inside agree only because homogeneous extent makes a part the same kind of thing as its whole, so the port looks like an identity function relaying one relation inward. **A heterogeneous case breaks it at once** --- a power cord `ThreadsInto` a flashlight from the room's side, while inside, port 1 is `SolderedTo` the battery case. Both true, neither a copy. **So a port is a scale boundary at which the description changes, not a relay.**

**That withdraws the "kind lives inside only" leaning, and for the same reason the ingress-list question resolved: the facts are complementary, not redundant.** With legitimately different kinds there is no shared claim to disagree about, so each side stores its own and the two-writer worry never arises. No new mechanism is needed --- the exterior edge already carries `HostRelationalEdgeKind`.

**And coarsening turns out to be lossless at the level being read, which is stronger than the tolerance it was granted.** `PowerCord -ThreadsInto-> FLASHLIGHT:1` coarsens to `PowerCord -ThreadsInto-> FLASHLIGHT` and still reads *"a power cord threads into the flashlight."* **Because the exterior kind was never about the interior part**, coarsening drops only the interior fact --- the detail, by construction. Under the matching-kinds assumption coarsening looked like graceful degradation; under heterogeneous kinds it is not degradation at all.

**No compatibility matrix, and the argument is AB-19's rather than a new one.** Policing whether *threads into* may pair with *resting on* is the compile-fictional-physics-into-deterministic-structure move that row already rejected, at a smaller scale. **Recorded on AB-19 as a use of its rejection in a place it was not written for** --- worth flagging because the next few applications will also look like small, reasonable validation tables.

**Two rules added.** *The port is the unit of lifecycle, not either edge* --- independent kinds let the halves diverge under dissolution (a port soldered to a battery case but exiting nowhere), so releasing the port takes both. And *do not "optimize" the rope*: someone will read rope data, see the same kind twice, and remove the "redundancy." **It is coincidentally equal.** Build nothing that assumes matching and nothing that assumes differing.

**Answered 2026-08-05, and it was the question that dissolved:** whether born-spanning things (a river, a cable run) mean changeable granularity is a property some things have rather than a lifecycle everything passes through. **It is a property some things have, and that turns out to cost nothing** --- under H1 a river is simply an extent whose parts never co-locate, so the hosting rule gives the same answer forever and no lifecycle is required. The question looked significant only while "born spanning" was being treated as a category; once the wall was separated out (AB-30), the remainder was ordinary. Kept here rather than deleted because the *shape* of the mistake is the reusable part.

**AB-16's strongest argument withdrawn as circular, 2026-08-06.** The row claimed containment and extent *disagree about what is present* --- a claim it called "the most load-bearing property the position model has." Challenged and it did not hold. The containment half was stated against the shipped host-bound model; the extent half against the [drop-clause-1b candidate](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#candidate-drop-h1-clause-1b-and-the-whole-chain-with-it), explicitly not adopted --- so the two halves were never in the same frame. Worse, [C8](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c8-the-flashlight-that-vanished-when-someone-looked-at-it) is what scoped that candidate to extent-only in the first place, and the row then cited the resulting asymmetry as evidence *from C8*. **The finding was a consequence of a scoping decision made two paragraphs earlier.** An earlier hedge ("a second premise to test") had already sensed something wrong but mis-diagnosed it as a conditional on AB-34, which preserved the claim instead of retiring it.

**The failure mode is new and worth naming, because the three before it were a different shape.** Those were costs mis-assigned to a proposal that in fact belonged to its comparator --- an accounting error. This one is *evidence generated by my own scoping decision and read back as a finding*. **What survives is a cardinality reading** --- extent wholes have needed to span hosts, containment wholes so far have not --- and it is under two live challenges recorded on the row: whether it reflects anything underlying rather than the sample of examples considered, and whether it would fork any code even if true.

**AB-16 retired 2026-08-06, the same day its strongest argument was withdrawn.** Once the positional claim went, the row was examined generally and **nothing survived in the position model.** The **cardinality** reading (extent spans hosts, containment does not) turned out to be a size confound --- every extent example chosen was long, every containment example hand-sized --- and it is refuted from inside this plan: the **coiled rope in a backpack**, the granularity insight the whole design turns on, is extent that does not span. A **wagon in a doorway with a barrel in its bed** closes the converse. Spanning-ness and containment are independent axes; "both" is the ordinary case, not the edge case.

**The identity-scheme residue was a third error of the same day, and a new kind.** I claimed extent parts are identified by *where* (`ROPE:1`) and containment parts by *what* (`BATTERY`) --- **reading a port number as the name of the part behind it.** The plan's own two worked examples were already symmetric (`OBJECT#RopeEnd1`, `OBJECT#BatteryCase`); I wrote them that way and then read an asymmetry into them. **This is the second time ports have been misread**, after the reusable-interface reading corrected earlier the same day, and both propagated into assessments first. **C11 and the falsification of clause 1a, 2026-08-07 --- and the case exists because an assessment was pushed back on.** Asked whether H1 could be narrowed, this plan's assessment was that clause 1b had not survived the corpus but **1a was fine**, since a part spanning two rooms would simply be decomposed further. **That was challenged on the spot**, on the grounds that it presumes any object is either a whole or a part and not both --- and that a scale-invariant system cannot be built on a distinction that is a consequence of point of view and positioning rather than an attribute of the item.

**The challenge was right, and the failure is sharper than "an unsupported assumption."** [C11](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c11-the-rube-goldberg-machine) was proposed to supply the missing structure: a four-level chain in which `StringSpan` is a part of the string *and* a whole of its sub-spans at the same moment. Clause 1a says single-host; a multi-host reading of wholes says multi-host permitted; **one object, two rules.** They are jointly unsatisfiable the instant whole and part stop being disjoint. And 1a's justification turned out never to have been a derivation at all: *decompose further* has **no base case**, because extent can always be cut again.

**Why no earlier case caught it: the corpus is uniformly two-level.** C1--C10 each have a whole and its parts and nothing beneath, so no object in the corpus had ever occupied both roles. **Depth is now recorded as the corpus's known blind spot**, alongside the apprehension-scale axis C10 opened --- and the lesson generalises past this instance: a claim that survives C1--C10 has not yet met recursion.

**One claim retired within hours of being made.** The same assessment argued that the `multiPresent` entry gate survives multi-host wholes because part-mediated reference binds an utterance to the locally-present part. That rested wholly on 1a; with 1a gone a player can ground on an undecomposed multi-present object and the command is refused. **P1's five-code-sites argument is back in force, not answered** --- and the honest position is that ports supply a *fix* at that site (the question becomes "which port egresses here", which the speaker's room answers) rather than an exemption from it.

**What it does to the successor hypothesis, before that hypothesis was written.** The proposal on the table was H3 = *multi-present **wholes** under ports*. That is the same disjoint-kinds error one level up and dies with 1a. Whatever succeeds H1 must predicate hosting on something **uniform at every level** --- the working form being *presence is multiple and uniform, any object may be present in N hosts via its port set, and whole/part are relations read off a graph rather than attributes of an object*. **Recorded as the reason the withdrawal was not written the same day:** the successor's content changed under it twice in one conversation, and C11's finding 2 (decomposition as convenience) may change it again.

**The five axes, 2026-08-07 --- and the reason this was overdue.** Proposed as a recognition that the graph system's flexibility serves several distinct benefits: **positioning** (can the arrangement be recorded at all), **apprehension** (can a *human* attend at the scale their character would --- why "a place setting" beats "plate, cup, fork, knife, smaller fork, spoon, napkin"), **reasoning** (can an *LLM* be handed the right packet --- why the rope's connective topology belonging to `OBJECT#ROPE` is a win), and **authoring** (can a human write "a kitchen with all the expected fixings" and have it become deep in play).

**They hold, and the plan had been half-way to them since 2026-08-05 without being able to finish the thought.** The falsification-criteria section had already worked out *why "can it represent X" keeps passing* --- any sufficiently expressive graph expresses almost anything --- but that was a purely **negative** result. It named nothing to measure instead, and the consequence was two more days of building hard cases that discriminated nothing. **The axes are the missing positive half**, and the corpus retro-fit confirms it from the evidence side: the cases that sailed through (C5, C6 --- the latter adding literally zero `H1-dependent` rows) are positioning-primary, and the ones that bit (C3, C8, C10) are reasoning and apprehension.

**One refinement to positioning: it is a filter, not a weak ranking.** It fires hard exactly once --- C7 eliminated one-node-no-ports because a single node cannot record *where along* --- and then goes quiet. Both Ariadne representations now under discussion (twenty flat spans with topology, versus a nested multi-room `thread-span` whole) clear it identically. A design argued only on positioning becomes unfalsifiable after the first round, which is precisely the state this plan was in.

**One addition, accepted on the spot: mutation is a fifth axis and had been folded into positioning.** Positioning is about *recording* an arrangement; nothing in the original four is about *transitioning* it. `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs once and `MultiKeyUpdate` cannot be re-entered --- a constraint that has killed or re-priced more proposals here than apprehension has (PQ-7, clause 5, rollback trigger 3, C9's write costing). **It is not a benefit flexibility delivers but a constraint flexibility must survive**, so it *vetoes* rather than ranks, and the five axes deliberately do not all do the same job to a proposal.

**One demotion, offered and accepted: authoring is a peer in rank and not in kind.** Positioning is the substrate and apprehension and reasoning are two consumers of it; authoring is about provenance and about what a human gets to *not* write, so its requirement cuts across the other three. An improvised chef's knife must be positionally coherent, apprehensible, reasonable-about, **and** distinguishable from an authored one, because asset re-merge can clobber it --- AB-29 arriving as a goal rather than as a caveat.

**The conflict is what makes the frame a tool rather than a scorecard.** **Apprehension is lossy by design; reasoning is lossless by design.** The place setting is good UI *because* it hides the fork; the rope's topology is a good packet *because* nothing was dropped. The same abstraction must conceal its members from a description and expose them to an LLM asked whether there is a knife to grab. A benefit list with no conflicts in it decides nothing, since every proposal claims every benefit.

**It caught a live defect within minutes of being written.** *A port is a scale boundary, not a relay* concludes that coarsening is "lossless at the level being read." **That was stated without naming the reader, and holds for only one of them:** an LLM asked whether the strap can be untied has lost exactly the fact it needs. Coarsening is an apprehension mechanism and is a defect on the reasoning path. Recorded rather than fixed --- coarsening is calibration on the not-locked list, the likely repair is small (description coarsens, reasoning opens the bag), and a frame should not settle a decision in the act of being introduced.

**The tally's own first result is the sharpest thing in it: no case is primary on authoring, out of eleven.** Not an oversight to fill quietly --- a measurement. This initiative has been designing a representation for players and LLMs and has never evaluated one from the position of the person writing the world. C10 and C11 are both authored and both record that neither AB-14 trigger fires, so the corpus holds improvisation at human scale and authorship above it **with nothing crossing**. Phase 0 gained a requirement for the crossing case.

**And a sixth discriminating mode, found by the retro-fit rather than by design.** C11 bit on none of the five axes: clause 1a fell to **internal contradiction** --- one object taking two incompatible rules --- with no consumer noticing anything. A case can therefore discriminate by making a proposal inconsistent with itself. Rare, decisive, and deliberately not forced into an axis it does not belong to.

**The Ariadne nesting question, 2026-08-07 --- and a correction to an axis written the same hour.** This plan's first account of why a nested `thread-span` whole beats twenty flat spans was a **cost** argument: nesting turns "this is one continuous thread" from a derivation into a fact. **That is the shallow half**, and it was corrected by two additions.

**First: sufficiency at a scale, which is a correctness claim rather than a cost one.** Theseus tugs the thread to learn whether it is still tied at the far end. The nested whole is *a place to put a reasoning summary* --- "a long thread winding through many rooms" --- and that summary is **sufficient** for the question. A small sufficient fact is not a cheaper input to the same reasoning; it is the input that makes the reasoning **reliable**, because focused attention has fewer places to go wrong than correct reasoning over a large redundant structure. Compression is a side effect of getting the scale right.

**Second, and sharper: a snag ports through the span.** A relation on a constituent crosses the port and is visible at the whole, **unexpanded** --- so reading the span alone already reports that a snag exists, with *where* and *how* retrievable and **omittable**. In the flat form the snag sits on one of twenty spans and cannot be known without reading all twenty: **the information cannot be omitted, only missed.**

**That forced a correction to the reasoning axis, hours old at the time.** It had been written as *reasoning is lossless by design*. **Too strong.** An LLM does not need every interior fact; it needs a guarantee that **nothing relevant was dropped without saying so** --- *a summary may omit detail, may not omit the existence of a fact, and what it omits must be retrievable*. Weaker, achievable, and exactly what the porting mechanism delivers. Recorded as a correction rather than silently edited, because the stronger claim had already been reasoned from once.

**It also narrowed the apprehension/reasoning conflict.** They **agree** about scale --- both want a thread named as a thread at whole level --- and conflict only about **omission of members**: the place setting must hide the fork from the player and expose it to an LLM asked whether there is a knife. Which means a *candidate resolution* exists where the axes section had said the frame names the conflict and stops: a whole-level view reporting *contains cutlery, one knife-kind item* without enumerating serves reasoning without compromising apprehension. Recorded as a candidate on one case, not a resolution.

**And it re-diagnosed the coarsening defect flagged earlier the same day.** The first reading was "coarsening loses what reasoning needs." The real finding is that **"coarsening" names two operations with opposite requirements about silence** --- *failure recovery* (the port is not legal; there is no finer fact; silence is correct) and *deliberate summarization* (the port is valid and was not traversed; there is a finer fact; silence is the defect). **The repair is therefore not "reasoning always opens the bag"** but that the summarized form must announce that a finer fact exists, exactly as the span announces the snag. Cheaper, and it fixes silence rather than compression. **The two operations need different names before either is built.**

**What ports gain: a developed argument on a third axis.** Every worked argument for ports had been positioning (traversal composes) or mutation (encapsulation, plan-time footprint, AB-17/AB-31). The reasoning argument existed in the original sketch as a stated goal --- *looking at the rope alone tells you how its parts connect and where they connect externally* --- and was **never developed or priced**. The port is the **summarization seam**. Under the axis frame this is worth more than a stronger argument on an axis ports already win.

**Two things held back deliberately, both raised as objections to the case rather than found afterwards.** (1) **The two halves are not equally robust.** Porting is structural and generalises --- any relation crossing the boundary is visible whatever the question. A curated summary is question-relative: *a long thread winding through many rooms* answers *is it still attached* and not *can I climb it*. **So porting is load-bearing and the summary is the softer claim**, and the argument must not rest on the softer one. (2) **The summary has upkeep the comparison did not price** --- stale if authored, derivation-moved-to-write-time if derived. Now **AB-37**, and notable as the **first cross-axis cost the five-axis frame has surfaced**: mutation charging for a reasoning gain, which is the frame doing the job it was written for.

**Competitor check, run rather than asserted.** The flat form *can* answer "is there a snag" --- with a reverse index over constituents, which is AB-36. But that is a special-purpose index recovering what nesting supplies structurally: a second mechanism for a subclass, i.e. position 3. **Rescuable only by an epicycle.** Written out because "the alternative obviously cannot do this" is the habit that produced two reversed verdicts in this plan already.

**State declined as a sixth axis, 2026-08-07, and the decline produced two artifacts worth more than the axis would have been.** Asked whether the five axes should gain a *state* sibling to positioning.

**It fails the frame's own test on all three counts.** Positioning's audience is the world and its function is to filter; a state axis would have the **same audience** and the **same function** --- C12 fired exactly that filter, eliminating *no carrier anywhere*. And **no conflict was found**: C12's isomorphism runs six-for-six, so nothing good for recording arrangement is bad for recording causality. By the section's own words, *a benefit list with no conflicts in it is decoration.* Put most simply: **state has no audience the frame does not already have** --- the parties who care whether the door is powered are the player, the LLM and the author.

**And it would have contradicted AB-1 the day after AB-1 resolved.** Splitting the *evaluation frame* along the exact line the *representation* declined to split would make the frame assert a distinction the model denies. If they needed separate requirement axes, they needed separate substrates.

**Two clarifications to the positioning axis fell out of asking.** Its name under-describes it --- the axis covers arrangement *and* causal structure --- and **the name is kept anyway**, because it matches the shipped vocabulary; widening it would trade one confusion for another. And *"fires exactly once"* turns out to be **per relation kind**, not globally: C7 fired it for extent, C12 for causality. Expect one firing per kind introduced.

**The instinct was catching something real, and identifying what took the useful work.** C12's finding that **coarsening is sound on containment and unsound on causality** is not a requirement difference --- it is **one mechanism behaving differently on two relation kinds**, and the frame had nowhere to put that. **The lesson was recorded generally because it will recur: when the axes feel short of something, check whether it is a new axis or a missing per-kind fact.** The second is much more likely, and much cheaper.

**So the [relation-kind register](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#the-relation-kind-register) was opened, discharging a debt AB-1 created on the day it resolved.** *One substrate, distinct relation kinds above it* named **no kinds**. The register seeds five --- `part-of`, sibling/connection, spatial, `powers`/conducts, `attached-to` --- with only the spatial one built, and fixes what each row must eventually say so later additions are comparable rather than ad hoc.

**Its founding finding is more general than the entry that produced it:** ***soundness of a mechanism is a property of the relation kind, not of the mechanism.*** Coarsening looked universal for two days because every kind it had met was containment-shaped. **The same is now to be suspected of the seam, presence-by-absence, the coarsening defaults and single-use** --- each should be checked against a causal kind before being assumed to carry.

**Deliberately not graduated.** The register belongs in `AGENT.concepts.md` when it stabilises and is far from stable today --- three of five kinds do not exist. A durable doc listing unbuilt edge kinds is close enough to the wishlist anti-pattern to matter, and unlike the fractal vocabulary there is nothing settled to protect. **Booked as a Phase 6 obligation and flagged as a real one**, since the register is simultaneously the artifact an implementer most needs and the one least likely to be re-derived correctly.

**One consequence noted and deliberately left for its own discussion: `positionGraph` will be lying.** If causal edges share the substrate, they live in `EphemeraPositionGraph`, in a lane called `dataSource/positions`, described by vocabulary --- **including the already-graduated concepts entry** --- that says a whole *"has its own `positionGraph`."* None of those names will be true of what the structure holds. **Recorded now because it is cheap to record and expensive to discover mid-rename**, booked as a Phase 6 obligation at minimum, and held back from resolution here because the naming question was judged to have consequences of its own rather than being a footnote to the register.

**P4 recorded, 2026-08-07 --- settling, dataflow, and narrative heat.** This is the largest piece of design in the initiative that had existed only in conversation, and it is **prior conception rather than new invention**: the scheme comes from the **Ludic Network** worked out when `Variable` and `Compute` were abandoned --- *a connected graph-of-graphs of story elements*. **A design arriving twice by different routes is evidence**, and the second route was [C12](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c12-the-moonbase-power-system-and-the-cut-cable) reaching the same conflict from the practical side.

**The dichotomy turned out worse than the framing that raised it.** Eager cascade is not merely unbounded on write --- it is **structurally impossible on the shipped kernel**, because `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs once and a cascade whose extent is discovered by walking cannot declare its footprint. So settling is not the compromise between two workable options; **it is the only shape that fits.** The honest accounting was recorded with it: total work goes *up*, and what changes is which cost is bounded --- unbounded synchronous becomes bounded synchronous plus unbounded asynchronous, which is right precisely because the synchronous path is player-facing.

**Retraction was raised as the main objection and dissolved by a reframe worth recording as a method, not just a result.** The objection: assertion propagates naturally, retraction does not, and both motivating cases are retractions. The answer: *turning off the power is not the retraction of a positive state --- it is its own change, propagating as any other change does.* **That is not a restatement; it is choosing dataflow over truth-maintenance**, and retraction simply does not exist in that model, because the graph *is* the justification structure and every dependent is visited by construction. **The graph holds states, not beliefs.** Retraction was an artifact of modelling the world as a knowledge base rather than as a world --- the same instinct that produced *ludic* as a name, arriving this time at the algorithm.

**What survived the reframe, precisely:** value changes propagate, **topology changes do not**, because the edge that would carry the impulse is the thing being deleted. Rule: **edge deletion emits its own impulse before it ceases to exist.** And the codebase already forbids silent edge disappearance --- `removeObject` asserts and throws with deliberately no silent-strip variant, and `boundaryEdgeOutcomes` classifies every severed edge. **Fifth arrival of the boundary-edge discipline outside its brief, and the least expected**, since it was written for carrying a chest through a doorway.

**Two defects were inherited knowingly with the model:** glitches on diamonds (which AB-13's DAG makes real rather than theoretical) and oscillation on cycles (composition is a DAG; causality legitimately is not). The dataflow framing does not fix cycles; it makes the failure legible.

**Narrative heat is the optimization, and it is probably not only an optimization.** Ariadne's thread through thirty rooms proposes *"the string goes briefly taut"* to all thirty, most of them empty --- the noise of a tree falling in an unoccupied forest. So the network cascades a **second** quantity: heat, flowing **backwards** along links from where people are and what they touch; and impulses carry an energy payload that decays, splits, reinforces on heat, and therefore **stochastically travels toward heat.** Nearest prior art is **importance sampling with Russian-roulette termination**, which matters because it has a rigorous account of when stochastic termination stays *unbiased*.

**The distinction the scheme was missing, and it is the crux.** Two things travel the same links. **Transient narration** --- *briefly taut* --- may decay freely; nobody hears it and nothing persists. **Persistent state** --- *the string is now cut* --- may not: an impulse that dies in a cold corridor leaves segment 27 wrong **forever**, surfacing an hour later when someone walks in. **This is a property of the impulse, not of the edge**, so it gets its own column rather than folding into the relation-kind register.

**The repair falls out of the same mechanism, which is the main reason to think the scheme is sound rather than clever: heat *rising* pulls unsettled state to settle.** A character entering a cold room is a heat spike and is also **the read**. So heat guides impulses forward in space and reaches back in time to finish the ones that stalled --- laziness reintroduced exactly where laziness is correct. **Fourth independent arrival at the tri-state**, and the first where it is load-bearing rather than tidy.

**One amendment argued for and accepted: decay to *parked*, never to *dead*.** "The trap door did not fire because we rolled badly" is non-reproducible and undebuggable, and in the prototype setting indistinguishable from comic timing. **Stochastic in scheduling priority; deterministic in termination.** A parked impulse is a durable low-priority worklist entry that a heat rise promotes --- and the shipped retention policy already says in-flight rows are not TTL'd because *"opportunistically queued work is not TTL'd while waiting."* **Sixth pre-registration, and the rule that now follows: look for it before designing anything new here.**

**One wrinkle caught in "splits on splits": energy division is conservation, right for a pull and wrong for a power cut.** Thirty devices go dark; none goes one-thirtieth dark. **Split-versus-replicate is a per-relation-kind fact tracking whether the fiction conserves something** --- and it is the register's **first prediction** rather than a recorded observation, which is what that section was opened hoping to produce.

**The tension deliberately left unresolved (PS-4).** Heat is affordable *because it has no correctness obligation* --- stale by seconds is fine, and it may be batched, approximated and shed under load. But *what does anyone care about right now* is the signal C3's Coyote context, AB-22's affinity filter, AB-24's ambient tier and the reasoning axis have all been reaching for. **A mechanism several correctness-adjacent consumers depend on cannot be as sloppy as its affordability argument requires.** Flagged to resolve **before** building, most likely by separating cheap scheduling heat from whatever the context consumers actually need.

**Why CoyoteGame, and the seams in that choice.** The artifact is a delay --- trap door opens, pause, *then* the fall --- which is a defect nearly everywhere and **the defining comic beat** in cartoon slapstick. A second and stronger reason points the same way: C3 shows Coyote is also the domain that **most needs** the structure, since `CoyoteStagedObject` carries no edges and discards arrangement entirely. **But a forgiving genre never measures what it forgives:** nothing in Coyote will report that settling took eight seconds. Worse, Coyote will push to make the beat *reliable*, and a reliable beat is a **scheduled** delay --- genre content tuned into the causal substrate, the instance-taken-for-the-category error arriving by the back door. **Instrument settle time from the first wave; keep incidental latency and deliberate beat separate by construction.**

**And a sequencing consequence worth more than the joke that produced it.** Both motivating cases --- the cut cable and the trap door --- are **removals of support**. That is not coincidence: assertion is fictionally boring and technically easy, while removal is where the drama is and where the algorithm is hard. **Slapstick is almost entirely removal of support**, so the hard direction is what the prototype meets on day one rather than in a later phase.

**Two things P4 touches that were left deliberately unclaimed.** It is a **candidate escape from rollback trigger 3** (an unplannable migration cascade is the same problem in the same shape) --- but a briefly-wrong *host* changes what is in a room, which is a far harder thing to be eventually consistent about than a briefly-wrong door, so it must be argued rather than inherited. And it largely absorbs **AB-39**, whose answer under settling is *the last settled state plus pending impulses* --- **but the row stays open**, because P4 is an unpromoted proposal and if it falls the question returns intact.

**Finally, the name earned a second justification.** A graph is static structure; a **network** is a medium things propagate through. With impulses settling and heat flowing back the other way, *Ludic Network* stops being a nicer word for the ensemble and starts describing what the ensemble **does** --- a stronger argument than kind-neutrality alone.

***Parked, never dead* demoted to provisional, 2026-08-07 --- the day after it was accepted.** Two objections were raised against the amendment recorded immediately above, and both land.

**One: the parked set is an unbounded history.** Nothing in the amendment bounds it; it grows with every below-threshold impulse the world has ever produced. And the cost is not storage --- it is that **a heat rise promotes the whole accumulation at once.** A room cold for a simulated year, entered by a character, faces a settle proportional to a year of events **at exactly the moment a read is waiting**. The mechanism sold as the cheap path has its worst case precisely where its correctness guarantee gets invoked.

**Two: it aspires to the wrong discipline.** *Never dead* promises that **every causal lead is eventually followed up regardless of whether anyone ever cared.** There has to be a way to say *this happened, it might have mattered if anyone had cared, nobody did, stop pushing* --- and the scheme has only indefinite deferral. **This is a fiction requirement before it is a scalability one:** a world that can never drop a thread can never close one.

**The diagnosis, and it is a conflation in my own argument rather than a flaw in the mechanism.** The trap-door complaint --- *"did not fire because we rolled badly"* --- is about **silent, non-reproducible** loss. It is answered by a **recorded** termination, not by keeping the impulse **live**. *Auditable* and *revivable* are different properties; the section argued the first and claimed the second. **Parking survives; *never* does not.**

**Three repair directions recorded unadopted.** (1) **Collapse rather than queue** --- parked work as a *pending delta per node*, not a log, bounding it by graph size instead of event count. This is a consequence [the dataflow reframe](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#retraction-dissolves-under-a-dataflow-reading-and-that-reading-is-the-ludic-native-one) already implied and nobody drew, which is both encouraging and a reason to check it is not merely tidy --- its live risk is a relation kind whose impulses do not commute. (2) **Park only the unrederivable** --- under dataflow most state recomputes from current sources, and rederivation from a *cold* region is affordable because it is rare and local; the trilemma's rejected second path readmitted at the one scale where it was never the expensive option. (3) **Let absence of heat be terminal** --- the mechanism objection two asks for **already exists in the scheme**; what is missing is permission for it to *end* something.

**And the split from the day before probably decides most of it.** Decay was already established as free for **transient/narration** and lossy only for **persistent state**. If so, *never dead* was over-generalised from the single row that needed it. **PS-9, PS-10, PS-11 opened; PS-8 amended**, since its escape hatch --- *or whether parked, never dead makes it moot* --- now rests on the demoted claim.

**Worth noting as a pattern.** This is the second time in this initiative that a claim reached by a good argument turned out to have **smuggled a second, stronger claim across on the first one's warrant** --- the earlier instance being clause 1a, where *parts are single-host* rode in on a whole/part distinction that does not exist. Both were caught by the user pressing on the assumption rather than the conclusion.
