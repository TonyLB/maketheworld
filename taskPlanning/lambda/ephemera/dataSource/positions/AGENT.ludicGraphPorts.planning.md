# `ludicGraph` ports: implementing the boundary mechanism

**Status: LP0 fully landed 2026-08-16 (Object + Feature, then Area). Stage 2 waits on P7.** Next step is [LP1](#recommended-order) --- graduating PQ-9's separator from a lean to a decision.

**This is an implementation plan, deliberately, and the choice is load-bearing.** Ports were designed inside [`AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md`](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md), which follows the **design-stage variant** ([`AGENT.designVariant.md`](../../../../AGENT.designVariant.md)). This plan follows the **base framework** ([`taskPlanning/AGENT.md`](../../../../AGENT.md)) instead, because all four of the variant's overrides are wrong for this work: resolved rows here are *finished*, not graduated; its open decisions **do block slices**; it ships into a published package rather than producing tier-licensed evidence; and Progress wants a running log of what landed. [`AGENT.md`'s step 0](../../../../AGENT.md) warns that deciding this late is expensive, so it is recorded here rather than assumed.

**What this plan does not own.** Every design verdict about ports stays in the abstraction-layers plan as a PQ row and graduates there. **This plan cites those rows as premises and never restates their content** --- the same discipline the `ludicCache` corpus file uses for C-series cases, adopted here to keep two files from drifting. If a premise below looks wrong, the fix is upstream, not here.

**And the trap this plan is most likely to fall into, named up front.** [CC1b](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#recommended-order) warns that *an implementation that runs smoothly under opaque ids has produced a convenience, not a verdict*. **Building [PQ-4](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused)'s provisional keying does not settle PQ-4.** Nothing in this plan may be cited upstream as evidence that a provisional choice was correct; smooth implementation is the expected outcome of *either* branch. The rollback line recorded on PQ-4 stays live for the duration.

## Why this plan exists

**Ports are locked in design and absent from code, and no step anywhere owned building them.** Their shape is [H3 clause 1](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#h3-ports-as-the-boundary-mechanism-decomposition-as-a-modellers-choice), locked on 2026-08-06. But a survey of the abstraction-layers Recommended order on 2026-08-15 found **no step that implements them**: Channel C is CC0--CC10 and every step is about the cache, and Phases 0--7 are the design track. [PQ-10](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused) owns a bounded matcher fix and nothing owns the rest.

**That is a gap rather than a deferral, and the distinction matters:** a deferred item has a gate someone can re-open, an unassigned one is invisible until something trips over it. **CC1 is what tripped over it.**

**What is blocked.** [CC1](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#recommended-order) --- the `ludicCache` rebuild function --- cannot be written. Its edges terminate at port addresses ([CC1b](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#recommended-order)), and the reducer's innermost operation is a discrimination between node and port terminals. Neither exists.

**What is not blocked, and must go first.** **P7, the merge-reduce proposal, does not wait on this plan --- this plan waits on parts of P7.** Articulating the reducer is what *generates* requirements on ports, and two of them were generated on 2026-08-15 and are recorded nowhere else (see [Premises](#premises-and-where-each-is-recorded), items 6 and 7). **Building the port record before P7 lands would build it blind to its only consumer** --- the failure CC0 was created to prevent, one layer down. Hence the stage split below.

## What we are changing

**Three code areas, and their blast radii differ enough to sequence by.**

- **[`packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts) --- published.** `EphemeraLudicRelationalEdgeData.from`/`to` are `EphemeraObjectId`. **`ROOM#A` does not match `OBJECT#${string}` under any reading**, so a room cannot be an edge terminal today --- and `Room` becoming a legal *node* in CC0b did not make it a legal *terminal*.
- **[`packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/baseClasses.ts`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/baseClasses.ts) --- published, and touched only if a discriminator lands beside the tagged-id family.** `isEphemeraTaggedId` splits on `#` and **throws** `Illegal nested EphemeraId` above two sections.
- **[`ludicGraph/`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/ludicGraph/) --- local.** The matchers, `rootId` and the `CarryClosureFragment` collapse, and eventually the port record itself.
- **The graph *shape* changes too, and it is not only about ports.** `EphemeraLudicGraphData` has no root and no `ports` element; `EphemeraLudicGraphNode` is `Character | Object | Room`; `HostRelationalEdgeKind` is `'On' | 'Under' | 'Against' | 'Custom'` with **no containment kind at all**. LP4a--LP4d close those, and all three are **decided design that was never implemented** rather than open questions.

**One correction carried in from 2026-08-15 so it is not re-derived:** it is **not** true that a port address cannot be typed as an `EphemeraObjectId`. `EphemeraWrappedId<T>` is `` `${T}#${string}` `` and `${string}` matches `ROPE#ab6129d`, so **the nested form satisfies the type statically and assigning one compiles clean** ([PQ-9](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused), limit (i)). The entire separator difference is **runtime**, inside the predicate. The room widening is a real typing change; the port widening is about honesty and runtime behaviour, not about making code compile.

## The widenings, concretely

**Target declarations, so the steps below are implementable rather than descriptive.** Written as end-state; Stage 2's port-address terminal is deliberately absent and lands in [LP7](#recommended-order).

**Two of these are not simple union additions, and both were found by checking rather than assumed:**

**(a) `EphemeraAreaId` does not exist.** `'AREA'` is not in [`EphemeraId`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/baseClasses.ts)'s tag union --- `'ASSET' | 'FEATURE' | 'KNOWLEDGE' | 'ROOM' | 'MAP' | 'CHARACTER' | 'OBJECT' | 'MESSAGE' | 'MOMENT' | 'IMAGE' | 'SITUATION'` --- so **Area is a new component identity, not a widening.** The `OBJECT# / AREA# later` comment anticipated a type that was never minted. `Object` and `Feature` cost one union entry each; **Area costs an id type, a guard, an `EphemeraId` entry, and whatever asserts on that union.** Consider splitting it into its own slice.

**(b) `EphemeraPositionAdjacencyContainedId` is a fourth narrow union, and nothing above named it.** It is `Character | Object`, and it types the **`EphemeraId` of an adjacency row** --- the *contained* side of membership. `FEATURE#Niche` being a member of `FEATURE#Wall` makes a Feature a contained id, so this widens too or membership cannot be stored for the case [LD-8](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) decided.

```ts
// packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/baseClasses.ts --- new; Area only
export type EphemeraAreaId = EphemeraWrappedId<'AREA'>
export const isEphemeraAreaId = isEphemeraTaggedId<'AREA'>('AREA')
// ...and add 'AREA' to EphemeraId's union and to isEphemeraId's disjunction.

// packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraPositionAdjacency.ts
export type EphemeraMembershipHostId =
    | EphemeraRoomId | EphemeraCharacterId
    | EphemeraObjectId | EphemeraFeatureId | EphemeraAreaId

export const isEphemeraMembershipHostId = (value: string): value is EphemeraMembershipHostId =>
    isEphemeraRoomId(value) || isEphemeraCharacterId(value) ||
    isEphemeraObjectId(value) || isEphemeraFeatureId(value) || isEphemeraAreaId(value)

// the contained side --- Feature added for FEATURE#Niche in FEATURE#Wall
export type EphemeraPositionAdjacencyContainedId =
    EphemeraCharacterId | EphemeraObjectId | EphemeraFeatureId

// packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts
export type EphemeraLudicGraphNode =
    | { tag: 'Character'; universalKey: EphemeraCharacterId }
    | { tag: 'Object';    universalKey: EphemeraObjectId }
    | { tag: 'Room';      universalKey: EphemeraRoomId }
    | { tag: 'Feature';   universalKey: EphemeraFeatureId }
    | { tag: 'Area';      universalKey: EphemeraAreaId }

// Written out rather than aliased to EphemeraMembershipHostId, deliberately: the two are
// extensionally equal today and have different jobs, so drift must be visible. Assert the
// equality in a type-level test -- the divergence is the bug, not the duplication.
export type EphemeraLudicTerminalPrimitive =
    | EphemeraRoomId | EphemeraCharacterId
    | EphemeraObjectId | EphemeraFeatureId | EphemeraAreaId

// `owner` is the whole that ALLOCATED this port. Named `owner` rather than `host` on purpose:
// hostId means "the graph's owner" and ports[].fromHostId means "the exterior host referred in
// from", so a third `host` would be the fourth sense of the word in one file. See LD-10.
export type EphemeraLudicPortAddress = {
    owner: EphemeraLudicTerminalPrimitive;
    port: string;
}

export type EphemeraLudicTerminalId =
    | EphemeraLudicTerminalPrimitive
    | EphemeraLudicPortAddress

export type HostRelationalEdgeKind =
    | 'On' | 'Under' | 'Against' | 'Custom'   // spatial, player-driven -- unchanged
    | 'In' | 'PartOf'                          // containment, non-exclusive (premise 9)

export type EphemeraLudicRelationalEdgeData = {
    tag: 'Relational';
    from: EphemeraLudicTerminalId;   // was EphemeraObjectId
    to: EphemeraLudicTerminalId;     // was EphemeraObjectId
    kind: HostRelationalEdgeKind;
    relationLabel?: string;
}

export type EphemeraLudicGraphPort = {
    portId: string;
    fromHostId: EphemeraMembershipHostId;
}

export type EphemeraLudicGraphData = {
    hostId: EphemeraMembershipHostId;
    rootId: EphemeraLudicTerminalId;              // recorded, never derived (premise 10)
    nodes: EphemeraLudicGraphNode[];
    edges?: EphemeraLudicRelationalEdgeData[];
    ports: EphemeraLudicGraphPort[];              // egress list; interior-authoritative (premise 12)
}
```

**Why the terminal is structured rather than a `ROOM#A#portKey` string, decided 2026-08-15.** The string form was the plan of record, and [PQ-9](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused)'s separator lean existed to make it fail **loudly** at runtime, because *"there is no compile-time protection either way"* --- `EphemeraWrappedId<T>` is `` `${T}#${string}` ``, and `${string}` swallows `ROPE#ab6129d`, so a port address satisfies `EphemeraObjectId` statically. **The structured form expresses the distinction the string form could not**, which makes the runtime workaround unnecessary rather than better-implemented. Three consequences: [LP2](#recommended-order)'s discriminator stops being a parser and becomes `typeof x === 'string'`; the parsed shape **is** the stored shape, with no round trip; and `isEphemeraObjectId(edge.from)` becomes a **type error** rather than the vacuous pass PQ-10 caught. **It is also additive** --- stored edges with string terminals remain valid as the primitive branch, so no migration.

**Equality does not fix itself, and this is the residue to watch.** `edge.from === someObjectId` still compiles against `string | EphemeraLudicPortAddress` and still returns `false` for every port terminal --- PQ-10's original bug wearing new clothes. **What changes is that the *other* sites now fail at compile time**, so the residue is small and enumerable rather than diffuse. It needs a comparison helper, and [LP3](#recommended-order)'s regression test still earns its place.

**The string form survives at the storage boundary, deferred deliberately and with a gate.** `ROOM#A#{portKey}` remains the right **Dynamo** representation, and serde in and out of it may well beat storing a POJO. **Not needed immediately** --- edges live in `Meta::*.ludicGraph` JSON, which holds an object fine. **The gate that re-opens it: the first time a port address must stand free of an edge** --- a Dynamo key, an adjacency row, a cache key, a log line, an LLM prompt. **This is deferred, not unassigned**, and the distinction is the one this whole plan exists to fix.

**Which means PQ-9's separator is relocated, not retired --- correcting a claim made in conversation before this note.** Scoped to serde, its argument still holds and possibly harder: a `:` is *affirmed* by every id predicate and narrows to `EphemeraObjectId` with the compiler's endorsement, while a second `#` throws. **The question that decides how much it matters:** is the string form purely a serialization detail that never escapes the read boundary, or an addressable identity someone can hold? **Contained, the throw is harmless. Free-standing, PQ-9's hazard is live** --- and *free-standing* is exactly what the gate above describes. Keep [LP1](#recommended-order); narrow its scope to the boundary.

**`HOST_RELATIONAL_EDGE_KINDS` must gain both new kinds in the same change** --- it is a runtime `Set` used by `isEphemeraLudicRelationalEdgeData`, so a union widened without it rejects every containment edge at the guard while typechecking clean. **That is the silent-mismatch shape again**, and it is one line.

**Required-versus-optional, stated so it is not defaulted:** `ports` is **required and possibly empty**, on CC0's own argument for `crossings` --- an optional field re-introduces a two-case branch at every read site. `edges` stays optional only because it already is; **do not make `ports` match it.**

**And *required* here means required, with no read-boundary default --- which is [LPM](#recommended-order)'s whole job.** `rootId` and `ports` are absent from every stored payload, and `EphemeraLudicGraphFieldPayload` is `Omit<EphemeraLudicGraphData, 'hostId'>`, so making them required in the domain type makes them required in the stored shape. **The migration is a reset, not a backfill and not a tolerant guard:** clear the stored graphs first, then widen. Defaulting was considered and rejected --- see LPM's first bullet for the two defaults that *would* have been honest (`ports ??= []`, `rootId ??= hostId`) and why permanent tolerance for a never-again shape lost to a one-time wipe on a single dev instance.
