## Why this is opened

**The trigger is a loss in `ludicCache`, stated by the author on 2026-09-14 and traced in the same conversation.** A cache is one merged graph over many wholes; a `ludicGraph` is many little graphs each with its own boundary. Presence in the graph is an **indexing of nodes by a boundary site**, and the merge has nowhere to put the site. Three losses follow, and they are the measure this plan is graded against:

- **(A) Enclosure of an otherwise unrelated node.** In a bucket graph the bucket *is* the node list; in a merge there is no node list to be.
- ~~**(B) Identity of the crossing site.** `chains: EphemeraMembershipHostId[][]` names **hosts**, so a string entering one room at two places collapses to one hop.~~ **--- RESTATED 2026-09-15, in conversation with the author, after reading the field's one producer. The sentence conflated two boundaries and misdescribed the mechanism twice.** **(1) Nothing collapses.** [mergeReducer.ts:177](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/ludicCache/mergeReducer.ts) concatenates entries on an identity-key match, and the test at `mergeReducer.test.ts`'s *groups two independently-matched leg pairs* pins the result as `[[boxId], [boxId]]` --- **two entries, side by side, deliberately unmerged, exactly as LR-8 requires.** The count of routes survives; what is lost is that the entries are **identical**, so they cannot be told apart. **Discriminability, not arity.** **(2) It is not "at the edge level."** The edge identity is intact; what is coarse is the route annotation, and the hop is minted **per crossing port** --- `collapseCrossingPorts` iterates `childGraph.ports.filter(isCrossingPort)`, `kind !== 'Present'` --- while writing `childGraph.hostId`, which is simply the only identity in scope at that line. **A presence node is nowhere on that path, and per [PN-3](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) a presence node is never a crossing at all**, so this loss as originally stated is not one presence nodes could recover. **Where the conflation came from, since it is an easy one to repeat:** a hop is typed `EphemeraMembershipHostId`, which is *membership* vocabulary, so hops read as though they recorded membership. They do not. **THE LOSS THAT IS REAL, and it is the one worth carrying:** the same child folded into one cache **more than once** --- present in `ROOM#A` and `ROOM#B`, both inside the merge --- produces entries that all read `[CHILD]`, because the hop names **the container descended into** and not **the binding descended through**. That is a membership fact, it is invisible at host grain, and it is what [PN-2](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) is actually about. **What is NOT a loss the cache needs to recover: which crossing port.** The cache is derived; the `ludicGraph`s remain the source of truth for crossings, and wanting port identity *in the cache* is wanting the cache to answer without consulting them. **This field has already been graded down once on exactly that reasoning** --- `chains` carried `EphemeraLudicCacheCrossing[][]` (`{ edgeText, into }`) until 2026-09-10, when per-crossing description was removed and only the host entered was kept ([`types.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/ludicCache/types.ts)). **Re-graining hops to crossing ports would walk that decision back.**
- **(C) Presence is not a node-to-node relation.** Encoding it as an edge requires the index itself to be a node --- which is this plan.

**The cache is *true* and meaningfully *incomplete*, and adding `Present` edges alone does not fix it** --- that was the author's opening claim and it survived the conversation. What fixes it is giving the boundary site an identity the merge can carry.

**The second motive is asymmetry, and it is why this is a *hybrid* rather than a straight reification.** Component nodes want to be localized by crossing ports, so their relations with other hosts are mediated. Presence nodes want the opposite: they need a reference back to *what they are presence for*, and they benefit disproportionately from being addressable **from outside** the host. Ports gave that addressing for free, which is why they were a tempting first implementation. The hybrid keeps it.

## The proposal in six clauses

**The author's, 2026-09-14. Recorded verbatim in substance; the commentary is separated below so the clauses can be read as the specification.**

1. **A presence binding is a node.** For `ludicCache` it carries `consolidated: boolean` (undefined/false meaning not consolidated); it remains addressable from outside the host in port form, `OBJECT#CONTRAPTION#{presence node id}`.
2. **A host node may have NO presence sub-nodes linked to it**, which says its graph is **unexamined**.
3. **If a host consolidates *any* presence bucket, it adds *all* of its presence nodes** and connects them to the hosting node. Zero or all; never some.
4. **Each presence node carries `consolidated`** for its own bucket.
5. ~~**For a single-hosted node** --- exactly one presence node --- presence within that bucket is **implicit**, derivable from the hosting edges (`In`, `On`, `PartOf`).~~ **--- RETIRED 2026-09-15 by [PN-1](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only). The hosting edges it derives from are not written: `drop` and `take-hold` mint none, and `In` is never minted at all.** Clause 5 pays for itself only if those edges exist for another reason; they do not, so deriving costs exactly what writing costs (one edge per node per bucket) and keeps an arity branch besides. **The clause is struck, not weakened --- there is no arity-1 special case anywhere in the design.**
6. ~~**For a multi-hosted node,**~~ **`Present` links are written explicitly** for consolidated buckets. **--- widened 2026-09-15 by [PN-1](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only), absorbing clause 5: *every* consolidated bucket gets explicit `Present` links, at mint time, regardless of arity.** This is now the single rule for bucket membership, and the two clauses are one.

**Why clause 3 is the load-bearing one, and it should not be weakened for size.** It resolves a conflict between two things this design was otherwise holding at once. Carrying every presence port into every bucket (shipped) is what keeps **binding arity** readable from any bucket, which is what [D3](AGENT.ludicCacheRebuild.planning.md#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only)'s completeness claim rests on. The rule *a presence node is absent iff its bucket was not pulled* needs the opposite. **All-or-nothing per host gives both:** zero says *unexamined*, any says *the binding set is complete*, and the per-node flag says which were pulled. Neither scheme alone can say all three.

~~**Clause 3 also pays for clause 5, which answers the obvious objection to it.** An arity change (single-hosted becomes multi-hosted) flips a host from implicit to explicit and needs `Present` edges written retroactively for the first bucket --- which looks expensive until you notice that clause 3 **already** forces re-materializing all presence nodes for that host on any arity change. The conversion rides an operation that is whole-host anyway. **No row is opened for it; it is recorded here so it is not rediscovered as a cost.**~~ **--- MOOT 2026-09-15 with clause 5's retirement ([PN-1](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only)). There is no implicit-to-explicit conversion left to pay for, because nothing is ever implicit.** Kept struck rather than deleted because the observation it rests on --- that clause 3 forces whole-host re-materialization on any arity change --- is still true and still worth knowing; what lapses is only the cost it was defraying.

**What the proposal banks, checked against the three losses:** ~~**(B)** and **(C)** are recovered --- two entries into one room are two nodes, and the index is a node by construction.~~ **--- amended 2026-09-15 with [(B)'s restatement above](#why-this-is-opened).** **(C) is recovered outright** --- the index is a node by construction, and nothing about that claim moves. **(B) is not recovered by the clauses at all, and it never was:** "two entries into one room are two nodes" described two **crossing** sites, which this migration does not reify and which [PN-3](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) puts outside a presence node's vocabulary entirely. **What the clauses do reach is (B)-as-restated** --- *which binding was descended through* --- and even that is **reachable rather than banked**, because it requires [PN-2](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) to re-grain the hop and `collapseCrossingPorts` to be told which binding it is folding, which today it is not. **Read the plan's justification as resting on (A) and (C).** Both are independent of `chains`, both are what the six clauses actually build, and the plan stands unchanged if (B) is struck entirely --- which is the honest grading, and the reason this amendment does not disturb a single slice. ~~**(A)** is recovered *conditionally*, and the condition is [PN-1](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only).~~ **--- amended 2026-09-15: (A) is recovered UNCONDITIONALLY.** [PN-1](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only) retired clause 5, so bucket membership is never derived and never conditional on a graph property --- every consolidated bucket states its members with explicit `Present` links. **The one thing that remains conditional is not (A) but materialization coverage** --- whether an intervening host was pulled at all --- which is [PN-12](#open-decisions-implementation--plan-only)'s referential-integrity question, not a loss of the index. It also defeats **CC0's box-can-be-empty argument** against deriving consolidation state: an opened empty bucket is a presence node flagged `consolidated` with no members, distinguishable from no presence node at all, so the marker is the **node**, not its contents.

## What this plan disturbs, and what it leaves alone
