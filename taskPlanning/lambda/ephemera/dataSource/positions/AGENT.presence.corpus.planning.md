# Presence: the Phase 0 corpus

**Companion to [`AGENT.presence.planning.md`](AGENT.presence.planning.md), created 2026-08-21** with its first case. This file holds **Phase 0's worked-example corpus** and nothing else: the evidence base that plan's rows argue from. Open rows, the cover formulation, and the progress table stay in the control surface.

**Read this as part of the plan, not as an appendix.** A case here is the reason a PR row is worded the way it is.

**Admission rules are inherited from the parent corpus** ([both rules](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#phase-0-corpus)): a case against today's model names the code site where it stops; a case against an unbuilt proposal names the clause it falsifies and states what each candidate reading produces. **This corpus will be mostly rule 2**, because presence is largely unbuilt --- so *name the clause* is the load-bearing half here.

**This corpus is aimed differently than the plan originally said, and the re-aim is deliberate.** [PH0b](AGENT.presence.planning.md#recommended-order) called for **character-side** cases --- movement, connect, disconnect, eviction --- on the reasoning that they are the highest-frequency mutation in the system. **[PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register) made them uninteresting to this plan**: characters are single-ported, so presence is implicit for them and **the presence design never meaningfully touches a character**. A corpus aimed at them would have tested nothing. **The frequency argument was answering the wrong question --- it asked what the system does most, not what this design decides.** What this design decides is multi-host representation, so that is what the corpus probes.

**Standing job for this corpus, stated because of how the plan got here.** PR-4, PR-9 and PR-10 were all answered on 2026-08-21 **before any case existed**. Every case in this file is therefore written to **falsify** an answer rather than to form one, and a case that merely illustrates an answer is not pulling its weight.

This document is task-scoped and follows [`taskPlanning/AGENT.md`](../../../../AGENT.md).

**What [C11](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c11-the-rube-goldberg-machine) says**, and it is priced there rather than merely raised: *"if a lantern is present by membership node and a rope is present by port, there are **two presence mechanisms**, and the level at which an object switches between them is exactly the whole/part distinction this case just dissolved. The invariance claim then fails at the bottom of the ladder rather than at the top."*

**The error, first.** This finding originally read *PR-10 is a decision to have two presence mechanisms*, and booked PQ-13 as that row's principal open objection. **That conflated two different objects.** **PQ-13 is about the *port*** --- does a plain object's graph allocate one. **[PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register) is about the cover's *edges*** --- whether a one-bucket cover materializes `Present` edges inside the object's own graph. **PR-10 collapses an edge set; it never says an object stops having a port**, so it is not a decision to have two mechanisms and was not answered in defiance of C11.

**The reframe, and it is the substantive half.** **Trace where a lantern's presence actually lives.** The lantern is a node in `ROOM#X`'s graph; under [PR-9](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s totality every node of the room's graph falls in a bucket of **the room's** cover; a room is single-presence-ported, so that is one bucket, trivially. **The lantern's location is already covered --- by the room's cover, not by anything the lantern owns.** **So membership-node presence is not a second mechanism sitting outside the cover formulation. It is premise 12 again: one relation seen from two sides**, the room's side naming the object and the object's side holding the port. **The port carries information only when there is more than one bucket to distinguish** --- which is when the object spans, which is what PR-10 says.

**This dissolves C11's objection in its own terms, which is the test worth applying.** C11's worry was that the **switch point** between mechanisms is the whole/part distinction --- **a role, not a kind**, and therefore undefinable. **Under one-relation-two-halves there is no switch.** There is a `ports` list that is empty or is not, and emptiness is a fact about **bucket count**, not about what kind of thing the object is. **A lantern carried into two rooms does not change mechanism; it gains a port.**

**Two residuals, both smaller than PQ-13 as posed, and neither decided here.**

### PR-C1. The contraption in two rooms, and whether covers nest

**Origin:** proposed 2026-08-21 from conversation, **explicitly as a disproof attempt** against the three rows answered that day. It succeeded against two of them, which is why it is the first case in the file.

**Narrowed by the [PH0a harvest](#ph0a-harvest--the-presence-cases-that-already-exist) the same day (H-1), and the narrowing matters: this is not the first nested cover.** [C11](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c11-the-rube-goldberg-machine)'s `ROOM#A -> RGM -> String -> StringSpan` got there on 2026-08-07 and is **the first case that *requires* PQ-5 chaining rather than merely permitting it**; [C7](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c7-ariadnes-thread)'s nested `thread-span` is a second instance. **Cite them for the shape; this case does not re-establish it.** **What is this case's own is *alignment*** --- that a nested cover can be well-formed at both levels and still fail to say **which inner bucket goes with which outer one** --- and the repair in Finding 4. **Read Findings 2 and 4 as alignment findings; the nesting they sit on is C11's.**

**The setup.** Two rooms, and a whole that spans them at two levels of composition.

| | Whole | Its graph's parts | Present in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Level 1** | `OBJECT#Contraption` | `OBJECT#GrapplingHookGun`, `OBJECT#WingSuit` | `ROOM#A` **and** `ROOM#B` |
| **Level 2** | `OBJECT#GrapplingHookGun` | `Gun`, `CableSpan1`, `CableSpan2`, `Hook` | `ROOM#A` **and** `ROOM#B` |

**Where the parts actually are.** `WingSuit` is in `ROOM#A` only. `Gun` and `CableSpan1` are in `ROOM#A`; `CableSpan2` and `Hook` are in `ROOM#B` --- the gun is braced in one room and its cable runs through to a hook set in the other.

**What the design produces.** Under the [cover formulation](AGENT.presence.planning.md#presence-as-a-cover), with [PR-4](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s reading (d) and [PR-9](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s totality:

| Whole | Ports | Buckets | Overlap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Contraption` | `port_A` (`fromHostId: ROOM#A`), `port_B` (`fromHostId: ROOM#B`) | **A:** `{GrapplingHookGun, WingSuit}` **B:** `{GrapplingHookGun}` | `{GrapplingHookGun}` --- **non-uniform buckets** |
| `GrapplingHookGun` | `port_1`, `port_2` --- **both** `fromHostId: OBJECT#Contraption` | **1:** `{Gun, CableSpan1}` **2:** `{CableSpan2, Hook}` | **empty --- the buckets are disjoint** |
| `WingSuit` | one port, `fromHostId: OBJECT#Contraption` | one bucket | n/a |

Each root is in every bucket of its own graph by definition, per PR-9's point (4).

---

**Finding 1 --- it falsifies [PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s predicate, and PR-10 has been amended.** PR-10 was answered *presence may be left implicit for **single-hosted** graphs*. **`GrapplingHookGun` is single-hosted** --- its one host is `Contraption` --- **and it needs two buckets.** Applied as written, PR-10 would refuse to represent the `{Gun, CableSpan1}` / `{CableSpan2, Hook}` split at all.

**The repair is a predicate change, not a reversal: the test is presence-port count, not host count.** *Presence may be left implicit where a whole has exactly one presence port.* **Read *presence* as load-bearing there** --- ports also carry relational pass-throughs, so a whole may hold several ports and one bucket, and **the stored record has no discriminator between the two kinds**. That gap is booked in [PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register); this case does not turn on it, because both of `GrapplingHookGun`'s ports are presence ports. **The type already permits what the amended predicate needs** --- [`EphemeraLudicGraphPort`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts) is `{ portId, fromHostId }`, `Object` is one of the five `EphemeraMembershipHostId` kinds, and **nothing requires `fromHostId` to be unique across the list.** Two ports from one host is legal today.

**Where the second port comes from is the part worth stating outright, because it is what makes the amendment more than a word swap.** `GrapplingHookGun` did not acquire two ports by being hosted twice --- **it inherited them from `Contraption` being hosted twice.** Port arity **descends**: a whole that must appear in more than one of its parent's buckets needs one port per bucket it appears in, whatever its own host count. **So port count is not a local property.** Reading it off the thing itself is impossible; you read it off the thing's place in its parent's cover, and the parent's own count may be inherited in turn. **This is the honest complexity of the representation and it is booked as an obligation in [PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)** --- Obligation A for the write side (splitting a whole cascades a partition down every straddling part, to the depth of the composition) and Obligation B for the read side (resolve presence port-to-port, never port-to-host).

**The consequence PR-10 was answered for survives**, and this is the check worth recording: a character is single-hosted **and** single-ported (single-valued, terminally gated), so characters still carry no presence record. **The old predicate got the right answer for characters by luck** --- host count and port count coincide there --- and this case is what separates them. **Given inheritance, the character exemption also now depends on a fact it did not before:** a character's host is a room, and **rooms are not parts of a spanning whole**, so nothing above a character can hand it a second port. That is true of today's tag graph rather than guaranteed by it, and it is what to re-check if a character ever becomes hostable inside a composed object.

---

**Finding 2 --- it confirms PR-9's totality, and tests it from a side the rope cannot.** Every case before this one has been the rope, whose buckets **overlap** along a chain. `GrapplingHookGun`'s buckets are **disjoint**, and `Contraption`'s are **non-uniform** (one bucket strictly contains the other). Union is total at both levels; aggregation reconstitutes both graphs. **A design that had quietly assumed overlap, or assumed uniform buckets, breaks here and the cover formulation does not.**

**It also confirms the fractal.** `WingSuit` is a **single-bucket part inside a multi-bucket parent**, and at its own level it is single-ported, so its presence is implicit --- by the amended PR-10, with no special case for *my parent is partitioned*. The level a node is partitioned at is the level whose ports it has.

**Finding 3 --- ~~it re-opens PR-9's edge half, which was closed one turn earlier and closed too broadly.~~** **Corrected 2026-08-21 from conversation: it re-opened nothing, and the heading above is the error rather than a step toward it.** `CableSpan1` and `CableSpan2` are one cable: there is an edge between them, and **its endpoints are in different buckets of the same graph.** **That is a true and useful observation, and it is not a problem** --- [PR-9](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s point (1) had already settled that the cover ranges over **nodes only**, so an edge is never a bucket member and *an edge in no bucket* names nothing missing. **Cross-bucket edges are required by this design rather than tolerated by it**, which [C7](AGENT.abstractionLayers.corpus.planning.md#c7-ariadnes-thread) established on 2026-08-05: its span-parts connect **across rooms**, and the case turns on those connections recording *"which exit each connection traverses."* **What this finding actually contributes is a question for [PR-8](AGENT.presence.planning.md#open-decisions-design--plan-only)** --- given a bucket, what sub-graph does it induce, and what becomes of an edge with one endpoint outside --- **a reduction convention, not a cover semantics.** **The two readings below are struck and kept visible because the failure is instructive**, and it is a failure of this case's write-up rather than of the case.

**PR-9 had dissolved this question, and the dissolution does not reach this case.** The argument was that a boundary-spanning edge is already two edges terminating at ports, from [CC1b](AGENT.abstractionLayers.planning.md#recommended-order)'s port-joining read in reverse. **That argument is about *graph* boundaries --- one host's shard and another's --- and this edge crosses no graph boundary.** Both spans are nodes in `GrapplingHookGun`'s own graph. **Bucket boundaries and graph boundaries are not the same thing, and PR-9 assumed they were.**

**All of that is correct and none of it needed a repair, which is what this write-up missed.** PR-9's dissolution was **surplus to its own answer**: point (1) had already put edges outside the cover, so the dissolution was closing a gap that point (1) had established could not exist. **Finding that the dissolution does not reach this case therefore costs nothing** --- it removes an argument nothing was resting on. **The write-up instead treated the dissolution as load-bearing and went looking for a way to restore it**, which produced reading (a) below. **That is the drift, and its mechanism is worth naming: an answer was given (*nodes only*) but the reflex behind the question --- that every edge must be *placed* somewhere --- was never retracted, so it kept generating work the answer had already made unnecessary.**

### PR-C2. The flashlight, the power cord, and the port that locates nothing

**Origin:** written 2026-08-22 as a disproof attempt against [PR-11](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register), which was answered from conversation the same day and has no case. **The fiction is not new and that is the point** --- it is the worked example already sitting in [*A port is a scale boundary, not a relay*](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#a-port-is-a-scale-boundary-not-a-relay-2026-08-06) (2026-08-06), promoted here because PR-11's answer cites it as the reason a port kind cannot be derived from an edge, and a row should not rest its central rejection on an example no case has ever graded.

**It is the mixed-port case [PR-C1](#pr-c1-the-contraption-in-two-rooms-and-whether-covers-nest) booked and declined.** PR-C1's Finding 1 states the gap outright --- *"ports also carry relational pass-throughs... and the stored record has no discriminator between the two kinds"* --- and then says *"this case does not turn on it, because both of `GrapplingHookGun`'s ports are presence ports."* **This case is the one that turns on it.** Both rules of admission fire: it names a clause it falsifies --- **PR-11's *"the same union the relational edge type already carries, unrestricted"***, which Finding 3 shows reuses half a type --- and a shipped code site where it stops ([`ephemeraMeta.ts:351-353`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts)).

**Both true, neither a copy** --- the 2026-08-06 finding, restated only far enough to run the case against it. The port-qualified exterior terminal is LP7's shape, shipped 2026-08-22, so this is no longer a proposal.

**What the design produces.** Per [the port record's field scope](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/AGENT.contract.md#port-records-field-scope-and-the-conflict-rule) the egress list is *port to outbound host*, and the outbound host is the owner of the graph the exterior edge lives in --- `ROOM#A` in both rows below.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `port_presence` | `ROOM#A` | the flashlight's **presence in `ROOM#A`** | **none materialized** --- [PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register) leaves a single-bucket cover implicit; see Finding 2 | `Present` | **Yes** |

---

**Finding 1 --- `fromHostId` provably cannot discriminate, and this is the first case that shows it rather than asserting it.** Under premise 2 the flashlight holds **two ports with the same `fromHostId`** --- `ROOM#A` --- one presence-bearing and one not. PR-C1 established that two ports from one host are legal (*"nothing requires `fromHostId` to be unique across the list"*); it did not establish that two such ports can differ in **role**. **They can, and no function of `{ portId, fromHostId }` separates them:** `portId` is a compact opaque token by [PQ-4](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused), carrying no semantics by construction, and `fromHostId` is equal. **So nothing weaker than a stored field on the port answers PR-11's question.** The answer is not merely convenient; the alternatives are exhausted on this case.

**A smaller correction it forces, on PR-C1's own phrasing.** PR-C1 Finding 1 states the amended predicate as *"presence may be left implicit where a whole has **exactly one** presence port."* Under H-3 residual (i) the flashlight has **zero** presence ports and its presence is correctly implicit --- covered by `ROOM#A`'s own single-bucket cover, exactly as H-3's lantern is. **[PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s register entry already says *at-most-one*, which is right; PR-C1's prose is the loose one and should be read against the register.** Recorded rather than edited into PR-C1, since that case is a dated record of what was argued.

**Finding 2 --- an edge passes *through* a port, so `kind` is one fact seen three times and `relationLabel` is not. LP6's agreement obligation survives; what it needs is a grain and a precondition.**

**This finding was first written as a falsification of LP6 and was wrong, on a vocabulary error worth recording rather than deleting.** [*A port is a scale boundary, not a relay*](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#a-port-is-a-scale-boundary-not-a-relay-2026-08-06) says *"the two edges joined by a port need not carry the same **kind**, and usually will not"*, and offers `ThreadsInto` / `SolderedTo` as the pair. **That sentence was written 2026-08-06, when *kind* meant the fiction-level relation name.** [`ephemeraMeta.ts:317-333`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts) has since split that word in two: a **closed seven-value `HostRelationalEdgeKind`** and a **separate `relationLabel`**. **At today's grain `ThreadsInto` and `SolderedTo` are both `Custom`, and they agree.** Reading the 2026-08-06 sentence in today's vocabulary produced a falsification of LP6 that the case does not support.

**The mechanism, restated from conversation 2026-08-22.** An edge **passes through** the port rather than terminating either side of it. So the **first-level `kind` is invariant across the boundary** --- an `In` exterior edge implies an `In` port and an `In` interior edge --- and the port's `kind` is not a third fact to be reconciled with two others. **What genuinely varies across the boundary is only the `Custom` payload**, which is the scale change the 2026-08-06 finding was actually about: *threads into* at room scale becomes *soldered to* at flashlight scale, and neither is a copy.

**So the two rules that looked like they forbade LP6's check do not, and the distinction is the grain.**

| Source rule | What it governs | Does it reach `kind`? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***"Build nothing that assumes matching and nothing that assumes differing"*** (the rope) | The **fiction-level name** --- today's `relationLabel`. The rope's `TiedTo` on both sides is coincidentally equal **as a label** | **No.** At `kind` grain matching is required, not coincidental |
| ***"No compatibility matrix... Do not"*** | Policing **coherence between different relations** --- is *threads into* compatible with *resting on* | **No.** An invariant that the same kind appears on both sides is not a matrix; it is one equality |

**One break does survive, and it is the precondition LP6 is missing.** `port_presence` has **no exterior edge and no interior edge**: the flashlight is in one room, `ROOM#A` is single-presence-ported, and [PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register) leaves that cover implicit, so **no `Present` edge is materialized on either side.** Agreement holds vacuously and **cannot be validated** --- there is nothing to compare against.

**Read that as a limit on the *check*, not on the *kind*, and the distinction is the finding.** The port is minted **as** a presence port; its constructor knows what it is building and writes `Present`, exactly as it writes `fromHostId`. **Consistency with edges is a constraint the kind must satisfy, never the source it comes from** --- so a presence port with no materialized edge is not underdetermined, it is trivially consistent. **This is the sharper form of PR-11's rejection of edge-derivation:** the row argues from the implicit case *having no edge to read a kind off*, which shows derivation is **insufficient**; what shows authoring is **right** is that the constructor holds the fact at mint time and nothing downstream adds to it. **The consequence for LP6 is only that its check must be conditional on an edge existing**, and a vacuous pass is the correct result rather than a gap.

**What LP6 should say, reworded rather than deleted:** the port's `kind` **must equal** the kind of each edge passing through it, at `kind` grain, **checked wherever an edge exists and vacuously true where none does** --- never extended to `relationLabel`, which is scale-relative by the 2026-08-06 finding and must not be matched.

**Finding 3 --- `Custom` is not self-sufficient, and this is a live question inside [PR-11](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register) rather than a finding the case closes. It names shipped code.** [`ephemeraMeta.ts:351-353`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts) makes `relationLabel` **conditionally required**: an edge with `kind: 'Custom'` and no non-empty label fails `isEphemeraLudicRelationalEdgeData`. **So the relational edge does not carry `HostRelationalEdgeKind`; it carries `HostRelationalEdgeKind` plus a label that six of the seven values leave optional and `Custom` demands.** `port_cord` is a `Custom` port, and **PR-11's *"the same union the relational edge type already carries, unrestricted"* reuses only the first half of that pair without saying so.**

**Finding 2 makes this the *only* place the boundary is not settled.** Kind passes through unchanged; label does not. **`ThreadsInto` and `SolderedTo` are two payloads on one port**, and the port is exactly where the scale change they represent happens. So the question is not *which edge does the port describe* --- it describes both, at kind grain --- but **whether the port must carry the payloads it changes between.**

| | Reading | What it produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **(A)** | **`kind` alone.** A `Custom` port records that it is `Custom` and nothing more; each label stays on its own edge | The port cannot say *which* custom relation it fronts. **No identified consumer needs it to:** PR-11's two named consumers ([PR-10](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s implicitness predicate, [PR-9](AGENT.presence.planning.md#settled-register)'s totality checker) filter on `kind === 'Present'`; a walk arriving from outside **came along the exterior edge** and already has `ThreadsInto`, and continuing inward reads `SolderedTo` off the interior edge --- [the both-sides navigability constraint](AGENT.abstractionLayers.ludicCache.corpus.planning.md#mechanism-findings-staged-for-p7) supplies both labels **at the point of use, by traversal** |
| **(B)** | **`kind` plus both payloads** --- an exterior label and an interior one, on the port | Puts the scale change in one record, which is what the port **is** ([*a scale boundary at which the description changes*](AGENT.abstractionLayers.proposals.planning.md#a-port-is-a-scale-boundary-not-a-relay-2026-08-06)), and matches the port already being **the unit of lifecycle** (*"releasing the port takes both halves"*). **Costs a second writer:** the exterior label is an exterior fact stored interior-side, free to drift from the exterior edge that also holds it |

**(C) is dead. (B) is taken, settled from conversation 2026-08-22 --- and the case had leaned (A), so the reasoning that moved it is recorded rather than the verdict alone.**

**The lean was: storing a label duplicates a fact that already has a writer.** The question is never whether a constructor *can* write a label --- per Finding 2 it can write anything it holds at mint time, and every field on the record is authored that way --- but whether storing it makes a second copy. A **presence** port's `kind` duplicates nothing: on the implicit case no materialized edge carries it, so the record is its only home. A `Custom` port cannot exist edgeless, so both its labels are held by edges that are always present. **On that reading (B) buys nothing and costs a writer.**

**The decision rationale, and it is a reversibility argument: it is far easier to trim a field that never finds a read case than to add one later.** **Note what that argument does and does not rest on here.** It is *not* leaning on migration cost --- LPM shipped a clearing tool and the reset has been executed and verified, so adding a field later is cheap on this codebase in a way it would not be elsewhere. **What it rests on is that the cost of carrying an unread field is small and the cost of a missing one is a design re-open**, which is an asymmetry the corpus has repeatedly paid for in the other direction.

**And the lean was weaker than it looked, on a point the case can state and should have stated first.** *Recoverable by traversal* is doing unexamined work in the (A) argument. **The interior label is local; the exterior label is not.** `ThreadsInto` lives on an edge in `ROOM#A`'s graph --- **a different shard.** A consumer holding only the flashlight's graph cannot recover it without fetching the parent, so (A) makes *what is this port for* answerable locally only for `kind` and not for the payload. **(B) is therefore the same move [`fromHostId`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts) already makes** --- an exterior fact denormalized interior-side so the interior can answer without a cross-shard read --- and [the port record's two-scope rule](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/AGENT.contract.md#port-records-field-scope-and-the-conflict-rule) has already accepted that shape once. **This is the stronger argument for (B) and it is independent of the reversibility one.**
