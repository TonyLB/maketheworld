## Why this initiative exists

The shipped position model has exactly one granularity: the human-convenience scale of Object and Character nodes on a host graph, related by peer-to-peer edges (`On`, `Under`, `Against`, `Custom`) within a single host. That model is a fine starting point and is not in question. What is in question is whether it can carry complexity **without becoming unworkable to reason about** --- for an LLM in particular, but the burden is not only the LLM's.

The gap is structural, not ergonomic. **Membership is the only level-crossing relation the model has, and it is flat**: a node is in a host or it is not, with no way to be in it *as part of something*. Every other relation is intra-host by construction --- [`applyStepSequenceCore`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/manipulation/kernel/applyStepSequenceCore.ts) derives a relational step's shared host live and **throws** on mismatch.

**Membership is not, however, single-host at the storage layer** --- a point that was overstated in an earlier draft of this file and is worth stating precisely, since AB-8 turns on it. See [Multi-host presence: what is actually true today](#multi-host-presence-what-is-actually-true-today) below.

So there is no way to say "these five things together are a thing," or "this thing has parts," or "this thing extends across those rooms," except by encoding it in a vocabulary that has no levels in it --- which pushes the level structure into every consumer's head, to be re-derived by inspection each time. That is the burden.

### The four seed examples

These are the origin cases, recorded verbatim in intent because the corpus in Phase 0 is built *from* them and must not lose their variety:

1. **Place setting.** Plate, cup, fork, knife, spoon `On` a table. At some point it should be possible to reason about and display "table with a place setting" rather than six nodes and five edges.
2. **Dismantled flashlight.** Improvise "parts" --- battery, bulb --- at a level *below* the object, on demand.
3. **Powered door.** A door that opens only when power is on, where power is an area-wide fact affected by repairs to a generator elsewhere. Demonstrates that abstractions likely convey **state** as well as position.
4. **Rope through several rooms.** Pulling it is mediated not by an individual room but by an Area or by an abstraction.

### They are probably not one structure

The examples separate along at least three axes, and conflating them is the main design risk this plan exists to manage:

| Axis | Seed examples | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Composition (part/whole)** | 1, 2 | Level-crossing, rooted, spatially coherent. The two examples are mirror images of **one** relation --- the difference is only which level pre-existed and which was improvised into being. Place setting: members primary, whole minted. Flashlight: whole primary, parts minted. |
| **Functional aggregate carrying state** | 3 | Members deliberately **not** co-located; membership is participation in a system, not containment. Carries state; the state gates behavior elsewhere. |
| **Multi-host extent** | 4 | One thing present across several hosts --- **representable today**, contrary to an earlier draft; see [Multi-host presence](#multi-host-presence-what-is-actually-true-today). Distinguished by needing **adjacency-mediated connections** among its parts (AB-26 --- *not* a path ordering; see that row), and by making the aggregate a candidate **manipulation host** peer to Room and Character. |

**Do not assume these collapse into one `Abstraction` type, and do not assume they stay separate.** Phase 1 decides it, against the corpus. Everything downstream reads differently depending on the answer, which is exactly why it is the root question.

### Clean fractal, dirty implementation

**Named 2026-08-06, alongside the *Abstraction Fractal* candidate on AB-0.** The fractal framing promises **scale-invariance** --- the same part-of relation at every level, no privileged one --- and the corpus does not fully deliver it. That is not one problem but two, and conflating them would either discard a good model or excuse a real gap.

| | What it is | How to treat it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Implementation dirt** | The clean model holds, and the implementation deviates from it deliberately, for efficiency: human-scale anchoring, caches, fast paths, shorthands | **Expected and legitimate.** Keep the clean fractal as the *specification of correctness*, and enumerate each deviation as an optimisation over it. A deviation that cannot be stated as "the clean answer, computed faster" is not dirt --- it is a model change wearing dirt's clothing |
| **Conceptual limit** | The clean model itself is not scale-invariant, independent of any implementation | **A real limit on the metaphor**, which the name must not paper over |

**Already-known instances of each.** *Dirt:* AB-15's default binding level is exactly human-scale anchoring --- the ontology privileges no level, the **pragmatics** privilege the authored one; and the render cache is a literal cache over the clean answer, so this is not hypothetical. *Conceptual limit:* **AB-13's DAG.** Multiple parents means the structure is not a tree-like recursion, and no amount of implementation care makes it one --- that is a property of composition, not of storage. ~~**AB-16 is unclassified** and worth deciding deliberately.~~ **Classified and closed 2026-08-06: neither.** Homogeneous extent versus heterogeneous separable parts is **not a break in scale-invariance and not an implementation convenience** --- it is not a distinction the position model draws at all. See the retired **AB-16**. Kept here because the *question* was the right one to ask; the answer just came back "no difference," which this section had not offered as a third outcome and should have.

**Why the split earns its keep:** it lets the clean model be held as correctness while the implementation is permitted to deviate, with every deviation named as an optimisation rather than quietly absorbed into the model. The failure mode it guards against is the opposite --- efficiency shortcuts accumulating until nobody can say what the model *was*, which is the state the position lane's four routes reached with single-host (see [C2](#c2-the-rope-in-two-rooms-refused-at-the-gate)).

### Multi-host presence: what is actually true today

Verified against the code 2026-08-05, because the rope case (AB-8) had been written up as breaking a hard invariant, and that is not accurate. **Multi-room object presence is representable today with no schema change.** What stands in the rope's way is narrower and more specific.

| Layer | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **Adjacency storage** | Natively multi-host. `getMembershipContainers` returns an **array**; adjacency rows are `(OBJECT#, POSITION#ROOM#...)` with nothing unique-constraining them. `MembershipDiff.froms` is plural and [`AGENT.contract.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/AGENT.contract.md) explicitly permits `froms.length > 1`. |
| **Membership compile gate** | **The hardest pressure, and the earliest.** Corrected 2026-08-05 while mining C2 --- an earlier draft named the end-state planner as "the" pressure. [`complexityPreGates.ts:29`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/complexityPreGates.ts) returns `complexityClass: 'multiPresent'` for `containers.length > 1`, and `preGateOutcomeToTerminalError` maps it straight to player-facing error copy. A multi-present object is **refused before planning is reached**. |
| **End-state planner** | A second, softer pressure. [`computeEndStateRoomDiff`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/manipulation/adapters/computeEndStateRoomDiff.ts) computes `froms = priorContainers.filter(!== to)`, so ordinary place / spawn / remove scrub every other room as **normal operation**, not as repair. A planner behavior, not an invariant. |
| **Relational compile** | A third, and different again: [`compileRelationalFromSkeleton.ts:168`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/compileRelationalFromSkeleton.ts) populates `hostByObjectId` only `if (containers.length === 1)`, so a multi-present object is **silently hostless** to Expansion rather than refused. |
| **Cross-host move route** | Bypasses the gate *and* the planner --- `executeObjectMove` grounds through the Synthesize executor from a host pair. So the constraint is **not one rule applied unevenly across routes; it is three different rules on three routes.** See [C2](#c2-the-rope-in-two-rooms-refused-at-the-gate) for the full table. |
| **Kernel step shape** | `MutationKernelTransferStep` has `fromHostIds: ReadonlySet<...>` but a **singular `toHostId`**. There is no add-to-many primitive. Three-room presence needs three pure-add steps or a widened field. |
| **Relational edges** | Strictly intra-host regardless. A rope in room A cannot relate to a bollard in room B under any current shape. |
| **Presentation** | Affordance compose reads `graph.objectIds` per room, so a multi-room rope renders as a **whole object in each room** with no notion of partial presence. Likely the first thing a player would notice. |
| **Drift repair** | [`repairObjectPlacementDrift`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/membership/repairObjectPlacementDrift.ts) **has no production caller** --- only its own test. The `Object Placement Drift Finding` sweep that would feed it is deferred and does not exist in [`lambda/diagnostics/`](../../../../../lambda/diagnostics/). Nothing is eliminating multi-room presence at runtime. |

**A four-way disagreement to resolve deliberately, not silently** (three when first written; the compile gate is the fourth, added 2026-08-05). The durable docs currently assert the opposite of the intended design: [`AGENT.contract.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/AGENT.contract.md) says "**Steady state:** at most one room per `OBJECT#`; multi-room adjacency is drift," and [`AGENT.concepts.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/AGENT.concepts.md) says "Objects follow the same steady-state rule (nodes only)." **Storage** permits multi-host while the **membership compile gate** refuses it outright and the **relational** route silently ignores it; the stated design (2026-08-05) is that a rope should eventually be present in several rooms. Either the clause was scoped to **characters** and over-generalized to objects when object membership shipped, or the design moved and the docs did not. **Fix the docs as part of Phase 1**, whichever way it resolves --- an unwired repairer plus a contract clause forbidding what the design intends is exactly the kind of stale normative text that gets obeyed by a future reader who has no reason to doubt it.

### Improvisation is the objects lane's mechanism, generalized

Settled 2026-08-05, and it changes what several rows cost. **`ASSET#IMPROVISATION` is not a flag on a special kind of object --- it is an asset layer in the participation stack.** Improvised bodies merge through composite `ComponentData` like any other layer, and spawn-time `DEFAULT` situation prose deliberately routes objects through the **same** `ensureAuthoredCatalog` render path Feature / Knowledge / Character use rather than a bespoke stub. So the mechanism's actual purpose is already the general one this initiative needs: **mint a component nobody authored, such that nothing downstream can tell the difference.** What it lacked was a name for that purpose. Full detail: [`objects/AGENT.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/objects/AGENT.md).

**What arrives already built:** the four-way split (merge body / play meta / semantic embedding / placement), a two-step existence-then-placement sequence, the **S1** compensating delete, the orphan sweep and its delete-only repair, and a best-effort **LLM-in-the-loop discipline** (Bedrock embeddings and enrich-generated prose, both failure-tolerant and non-blocking) that AB-18's cost and latency policy should start from rather than re-derive.

**This sharpens AB-12 from a philosophical question into a cost question.** If Objects are already abstractions at every level, minting a battery or a place setting **is** minting an `OBJECT#` --- the lane needs no generalization at all, only use. The Room / Feature half is different in kind: every row key, module, and type in that lane is `OBJECT#`-shaped. The two bets therefore differ by roughly *no new storage* versus *a new storage shape*, which is far more decidable than the ontology argument.

**Three gaps, all bounded, all real:**

| Gap | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **No composition relation** | Improvisation mints a **free-standing** object and places it in a room. A *part* needs the level-crossing edge as well --- so spawn+place becomes spawn+place+compose, or the compose **is** the place if AB-4 resolves part-of as a host relationship. Either way the S1 compensating-delete story needs revisiting, since it currently compensates a two-step sequence. |
| **Recognition fits less cleanly than Improvisation** | Improvisation creates a thing from nothing, so its existence rows are the whole story. Recognition creates an aggregate **over things that already exist**: its body is nearly empty and its **member set is the content**. The lane has no concept of a component whose identity is a set of other components. |
| **`EMBEDDING#IMPROMPTU` is `shortName`-derived** | Reasonable for a battery, questionable for "the place setting on the table." Whether an aggregate gets a semantic vector, and over what text, is undecided. |

**Naming consequence --- carried as AB-0's concrete form.** If Improvisation names the *mechanism*, then Improvisation and Recognition are **not peers**: Recognition is a trigger that invokes the mechanism, and operational necessity is the other trigger. The word is currently doing both jobs, which is the same conflation AB-14 was restructured to remove. Two ways out, unresolved: **(a)** Improvisation and Recognition stay the two triggers and the shared mechanism takes its own name; **(b)** Improvisation names the mechanism and the downward trigger is renamed.

**One speculative consequence worth keeping.** Because `ASSET#IMPROVISATION` is a *layer*, an improvised thing can later be **authored over**. A recognized snare trap could graduate into canon --- a real partial answer to AB-19's cost objection that an authored pattern vocabulary limits players to what someone anticipated.

### Coyote as prior art: bounded LLM reasoning that is not bounded structurally

Identified 2026-08-05 as the **existing first-iteration attempt** at reasoning about player-assembled contraptions --- deliberately flawed and ad hoc, and the closest thing to a working answer for AB-19. Read [`coyoteGame/AGENT.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/coyoteGame/AGENT.md), [`coyoteGame/AGENT.tropes.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/coyoteGame/AGENT.tropes.md), and [`coyotePlanAffinities.ts`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/coyotePlanAffinities.ts) before designing anything in this area.

**What it does, structurally:** a `CoyoteStagedObject` snapshot assembled from `positionGraph` + `Meta::Object` + the improvisation pair, grouped by room; each object carrying `tropeAffinities` (roles with graded `aptness`, plus `environmentAffordances` / `affordancesProvided`) written at **Acme enrich time**; then a multi-hop LLM pipeline (candidates -> planSelect -> narrativeBeats -> outcome) with structured parse between hops and an internal-only `gimmick` narrative spine.

**What generalizes --- keep these:**

| Pattern | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- |
| **Annotate at mint time, not at reasoning time** | One Bedrock call at object creation writes durable structure; the reasoning step then *consumes* it. This is the direct answer to AB-18's cost concern and the reason AB-22 exists. |
| **Closed structural vocabulary, open narrative content** | Roles and tropes are enumerable; `gimmick` is deliberately free text with no closed enum. Matches the standing rule that only genuinely enumerable vocabulary should be hardcoded. |
| **Graded `aptness`, not booleans** | Affordance is a matter of degree, and downstream hops can rank rather than filter. |
| **Multi-hop with structured parse between hops** | Each hop's output is validated JSON, so a later hop reasons over structure rather than prose. |
| **Internal-only reasoning handoff** | `gimmick` never reaches the client. Reasoning scaffolding and player-facing narration are separate artifacts. |

**What is ad hoc --- and it is precisely the gap this initiative would fill:**

- **There is no persistent assembly-level object anywhere in it.** Every prediction reconstructs "what did the player build" from a **flat bag** of staged objects plus per-object role affinities. The contraption is never a thing.
- **Context is scoped to rooms, not to the contraption** (`loadCoyoteRoomObjectsByRoom`, across all Coyote game rooms). The LLM receives everything staged, relevant or not.
- **Therefore its boundedness is circumstantial, not structural.** It comes from the world being tiny and the genre closed --- six tropes, a hardcoded `boulder | cactus | ...` affordance enum, a handful of rooms. Remove any of those and nothing bounds the context.
- **The seam-label hack** (prompt labels vs. canonical `EphemeraRoomId`, an explicitly one-way contract carrying a six-item warning list about reversing it) reads as a symptom of the same cause: prompt context assembled ad hoc rather than projected from a real structure.

**The load-bearing conclusion:** an abstraction layer would supply the boundedness **structurally** --- the contraption is a named subgraph, and that subgraph *is* the context. Coyote answers the annotation question per-object and the scope question per-room; the missing piece is per-**assembly**.

### `thinking` as the scheduling substrate: shipped ledger, unbuilt queue

Identified 2026-08-05 as the intended home for deferred recognition, and **verified against code** because the distinction between what exists and what does not is load-bearing. Read [`thinking/AGENT.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/thinking/AGENT.md).

**Shipped and reusable:** durable `Meta::Schedule` rows on a `TASK#${workItemId}` partition, a status vocabulary of `scheduled | claimed | cancelled | completed`, job bootstrap and adjacency, rollup to `Job Completed`, client dashboards, and `fetchThinkingResult` lookup by `workItemId`. The retention policy already anticipates this exact use: **"in-flight rows --- `scheduled` / `claimed` schedules ... do not set `deleteAt`, so *opportunistically queued work* is not TTL'd while waiting."**

**Not shipped --- and this is the actual work:** **nothing writes `claimed`.** Only `scheduled`, `completed`, and `cancelled` have producers; `claimed` appears in the type union and in `persistThinkingJobError`'s `IN_FLIGHT_SCHEDULE_STATUSES` for cancellation, with **no claimer anywhere**. Every thinking job today is bootstrapped at the start of a synchronous pipeline (Coyote hypothesis, Acme enrich) and completed within that same Lambda invocation. **The schedule rows are observability records of work already happening, not a queue anything pulls from.** A worker, deferred dispatch, and promotion-on-demand all remain to be built.

**One guarantee that comes free, and it closes AB-18's convergence requirement.** `Meta::Result` is written with `ephemeraDB.nonCollidingPutItem` --- conditional on `attribute_not_exists`, **first write wins**, duplicate finalize returns `alreadyFinalized`. That is exactly what "the deferred pass and the promoted foreground path must not produce two snares" needs, already shipped and already retry-safe. Two independently-derived requirements (AB-18's convergence, and promotion-on-demand from the thinking side) landing on one existing mechanism is evidence the framing is sound.

## Proposal P1: single-host objects + hosting at the containing node

**Status: adopted provisionally as [H1](#working-hypotheses), 2026-08-05.** Recorded first as an unjudged proposal, then promoted --- **not** to a decision, but to a *working hypothesis whose job is to ground further design*. Every answer derived from it is tagged `H1-dependent` so the rollback set stays known; see [Working hypotheses](#working-hypotheses) for the tagging test, the falsification cases, and the named rollback triggers. It touches AB-4, AB-8, AB-9, AB-16, AB-17 and the AB-21 identity question, so it is written up here rather than inside any one row.

### The proposal

1. **Remove multi-host capability for Objects.** An Object has exactly one host. (Features may remain multi-host --- see the note below.)
2. **Give Objects a direct lookup of placement data on their `Meta`**, rather than a variable set of adjacency rows.
3. **Spanning things are modelled as parts.** "Tie rope to rock" then "drop rope into abyss" improvises **two rope parts**: one stays in the cliff-top room tied to the rock, one goes into the abyss.
4. **The whole is hosted at the node high enough in the containment tree to contain all of its parts.** The rope object itself hosts in the **Area** containing both rooms, with part connections (likely two-way) to its parts.
5. **Migration is automatic and bidirectional.** Haul the rope up, all parts in one room, and the rope migrates down from Area into that room.

### What it buys (assessed against code, 2026-08-05)

| Gain | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Every threatened invariant survives** | `computeEndStateRoomDiff`'s end-state scrub stays correct; adjacency stays single-valued; `MutationKernelTransferStep`'s singular `toHostId` needs no widening. |
| **The contract clause is vindicated, not stale** | "At most one room per `OBJECT#`" becomes **true as written** --- the design moves to meet the contract instead of the contract being rewritten. Materially cheaper than the widening path, and it retires the three-way disagreement recorded under [Multi-host presence](#multi-host-presence-what-is-actually-true-today). |
| **The relational-edge problem dissolves** | Rope tied to rock is an ordinary **same-host** edge between the cliff-top *part* and the rock. This was the sharpest structural objection to multi-room ropes; it needs zero new machinery. |
| **Presentation is correct by construction** | Each room's `graph.objectIds` holds the part actually present, so no room renders a whole rope it only partly contains. |
| **The drift class genuinely shrinks** | A single-valued host cannot exhibit multi-room drift. `repairObjectPlacementDrift`'s multi-room scrub branch becomes **structurally impossible**; only field-vs-graph lag survives. A deletion, not a relocation. |
| **Improvisation is reused unchanged for parts** | A part is an Object; minting one is minting an `OBJECT#` into `ASSET#IMPROVISATION`. Only the composition edge is new (already tracked as gap 1). |

### What it costs --- concentrated almost entirely in the host containment tree

| Cost | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Area is further away than "parked"** | `EphemeraMembershipHostId = EphemeraRoomId \| EphemeraCharacterId` ([`ephemeraPositionAdjacency.ts`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraPositionAdjacency.ts)), and **there is no `EphemeraAreaId` in `baseClasses.ts` at all**. Area exists only WML/asset-side (`AREA#downtown`, `AREA#region`). This introduces an ephemera-side identity, not merely a deferred host kind. |
| **Containment is authored downward, with no upward index** | `StandardAreaData` carries a `positionGraph` listing its members, so "Area contains Room" is nodes in the Area's graph. **Nothing maps room -> area.** The rule needs the upward direction on every part move --- structurally the same missing thing as the parked reverse-edge-reference index. |
| **Is it a tree at all?** | Area membership is authored blueprint **merged at participation order**, so containment may be **asset-stack dependent** --- while an object's host must be world truth, not perspective-relative. `AREA#region` beside `AREA#downtown` also suggests nesting depth. And two rooms with no common Area leave the LCA undefined without a world root. **This is the load-bearing unknown for P1.** |
| **Migration is an invariant needing transactional cascade** | Every part move recomputes its whole's host, which may move the whole, which may cascade to wholes-of-wholes. `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs **once, up front**, and `MultiKeyUpdate` cannot be re-entered --- so plan time must walk composition *upward* to pre-lock every host the cascade might touch. |

### What it changes conceptually

- **Two kinds of hosting, needing a name.** Room-hosting means *physically there*; Area-hosting is *bookkeeping*. Area-hosted objects presumably never surface in affordances --- state it, or someone writes compose that surfaces them.
- **It makes AB-9 acute rather than dodging it --- and [C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends) escalates that from acute to routine.** With all parts back in one room, the whole **and** its parts sit in that room's graph: "you see a rope, a rope end, and a rope end." Description must pick a level, unavoidably. C5 shows this is not a transient state during manipulation but the **permanent steady state of anything anchored**: a chain mounted at one end and attached to a chest at the other sits in the cell's graph as three nodes indefinitely. Level selection is therefore a default read-path concern, not an edge case.
- ~~**Migration-down and reabsorption may be the same event (AB-17).** Coiling the rope is all-parts-co-located, which is both "migrate the whole into the room" and "dissolve the parts."~~ **Falsified 2026-08-05 by [C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends).** Co-location and load-bearing are independent: the chain's parts are all in one cell *and* still carry the relations anchoring it, so the whole migrates down while the parts must persist. **P1 collapsed two conditions into one** --- AB-17's criterion was already written as a disjunction ("no part is separately referenced **or** separately positioned") and clause 5 tests only the second. The two events stay separate; **AB-17** carries the split criteria. Struck rather than softened, per this plan's rule on recording supersessions.

### What it does not resolve

- **How parts *connect* is out of P1's scope.** Parts plus a whole give membership, not structure. **This is AB-26, and it is not a sequence** --- connections are adjacency-mediated edges ("the clifftop end connects to the shaft section by way of the 'into the abyss' exit"), which branch. P1 and AB-26 are complementary, not competing: P1 decides *where the whole is hosted*, AB-26 decides *how the parts relate*.
- **Multiple parents (AB-13) is fine**, incidentally: each whole computes its own LCA independently, so an object being part of two wholes creates no conflict.

### On the two sub-proposals

**`Meta` direct lookup:** single-host makes the reverse index single-valued, collapsing a variable row set into one field --- a genuine simplification of *shape*. It remains a dual write with the same atomicity discipline and the same "`positionGraph` wins" conflict rule, so it **shrinks** the drift class rather than removing it.

**Features stay multi-host:** this is **forward-looking, not preserved.** Features are not graph nodes at all today (the node `tag` union is `'Character' | 'Object'`). It is consistent with their documented narrowing --- never membership-moved, never a relation subject, therefore no transfer semantics to get wrong --- but it describes a capability yet to be built.

**And the phrasing may be wrong, not just unbuilt (added 2026-08-05, see AB-30).** "Features stay multi-host" presumes Features are the kind of thing that *has* hosts. If a wall between two rooms is better modelled as an **edge** than as a node, it is not multi-host --- it is a non-member, and the sub-proposal is answering a question that does not arise. Note the asymmetry this sub-proposal currently carries unremarked: H1's entire thrust is **removing** multi-host for Objects, while this clause **preserves** it for Features. That is either a real distinction with a reason, or the clause is describing the problem in the vocabulary H1 just abandoned. AB-30 decides which.

## Working hypotheses

**Why this section exists (2026-08-05).** This plan's governing posture has been that *deciding is premature* --- there is useful discussion left in most of these questions, and closing them early would foreclose it. That posture is unchanged. What changed is the recognition that a question can also be under-discussed for the opposite reason: with no grounding assumption, downstream rows have nothing to argue *against*, and the discussion stays abstract rather than premature.

**A working hypothesis is the middle move.** Adopt a candidate explicitly as a *test of how well it grounds further design*, tag every answer derived from it, and keep the derived set visible so it can be rolled back as a unit. If a hypothesis dies, the paper trail says exactly which answers die with it and where to branch from with a different candidate. **This is not a decision, and a hypothesis surviving is not evidence it is right** --- only that nothing has yet killed it.

### The tagging test, and why it is the point

Mark a row **`H1-dependent`** only if its answer is **derived from** the hypothesis --- it would not make sense, or would be answered differently, if the hypothesis were withdrawn. Do **not** mark a row merely *compatible* with it.

| | Example | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Derived from** | "Part-connections need an Area-level graph" --- only holds because P1 hosts the whole at the Area | `H1-dependent` |
| **Merely compatible** | AB-26's "adjacency *licenses* an edge, never derives one" --- true under framing (a) as well | untagged |

**The distinction is not bookkeeping; the count is a measurement.** A tag applied to everything makes rollback total, which is the same as having no trail at all. Applied honestly, the size of the dependent set says how much work H1 is actually doing: a small set means the hypothesis was cheap and most of the design stands on its own; a large one means it is load-bearing and the lock deserves more scrutiny than it has had. Record that count at [Phase 3](#recommended-order)'s consistency pass, where the set is finally known.

### H1: single-host objects, hosting at the containing node

**Statement:** [Proposal P1](#proposal-p1-single-host-objects--hosting-at-the-containing-node), in full.

#### H1 is a conjunction, and its clauses are tested separately

Adopted 2026-08-05 after "H1 is untested against a serious attempt" turned out to be **too coarse a statement to be true or false.** [C7](#c7-ariadnes-thread) is a passed discriminating test --- of *one clause*. **They are also not independent, which the first version of this table got wrong.** Corrected 2026-08-06: **clause 1 applied to *wholes* generates clause 4, and clause 4 generates clause 5.** Enforce single-host, decompose a spanning thing into parts, and the *whole* still exists --- if it is a graph node it needs a host, and its parts are by construction spread across rooms. So "where does the whole live" must be answered, and clause 4 is that answer. Then, since the host is defined as *the node containing all parts* and parts move, the host must change: **clause 5 is not a feature, it is what maintaining clause 4's invariant consists of.** Three of the five clauses are therefore one commitment with two entailments, and testing them as peers would have over-counted H1's independent content.

The surviving half is still worth having: P1's claimed gains --- compile gate, end-state planner, singular `toHostId`, per-room compose --- only require that no object *is* multi-present, so they are satisfied by 1a plus whatever answers 1b.

#### Three positions on reference, and what clause 1b actually costs

**Raised 2026-08-06 as the sharpest pressure clause 1b has taken, and the first arriving from a *requirement* rather than a scenario.** The requirement (now on **AB-32**): *reference and perception must reach a spanning whole and a local whole by the same mechanism.* If "the thread in room B" and "the flashlight on the table" resolve by different machinery, that difference propagates into every consumer.

**The argument as first put:** you can have **one scale-invariant processing rule** or **single-hosting**, not both --- because a single-hosted spanning whole cannot be a node in each room it passes through, so reaching it needs a second path, and a second path is an epicycle. **Substantially right, and it turns out there are three positions rather than two.**

| Position | Reference mechanism | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Multi-host the whole** | Local lookup everywhere --- the thread is a node in A, B and C; the flashlight is a node in A | **Not free, and this cost was missing from the original framing.** [C7](#c7-ariadnes-thread) showed one node cannot record *where along* the thread anything attaches, so segments are **still** required --- room B then holds both the thread node and its local segment, two nodes for overlapping things, and "pull the thread" in B has two plausible bindings. Every mutation writes N graphs. **Substantially re-priced 2026-08-06 by [P3](#provisional-p3-ports-as-host-boundary-bindings)** --- see below |
| **2. Single-host, traversal as the *clean* rule** | Local nodes **plus their reachable ancestors**. Zero hops for an undecomposed flashlight (the node present *is* the whole); one hop for a thread | **Breaks the dichotomy.** A loop that sometimes iterates zero times is one mechanism, not two --- differing hop count is not an epicycle. Cost is that it **inverts clean and fast**: today's `graph.objectIds` lookups become the *optimisation*, needing a stated validity condition. Legitimate [dirt](#clean-fractal-dirty-implementation) by that section's own test --- a lookup returning what the traversal would return is "the clean answer, computed faster" |
| **3. Single-host, lookup as the clean rule** | Local lookup, plus a separate path for spanning wholes | **This is the position the argument kills**, and correctly: the second path is not an optimisation of the rule but a *different rule for a subclass* --- an epicycle in the precise sense |

**The narrowed dichotomy, which is the durable form:** ~~scale-invariance or single-hosting~~ --- **you can have single-hosting or lookup-as-the-clean-rule, not both.** Scale-invariance survives single-hosting only if the clean rule is traversal-shaped.

**And the plan is currently sitting in position 3 without having chosen it.** [Part-mediated reference](#part-mediated-reference-how-h1-answers-its-own-replacement-trigger) was introduced as the answer for spanning wholes while local lookup remained the unexamined norm for everything else --- **two paths, never reconciled.** So the epicycle this argument predicts is not a future risk; it is already in the design as written, and was introduced the moment the spanning case got its own answer.

**Position 1 was re-priced 2026-08-06 by [P3](#provisional-p3-ports-as-host-boundary-bindings), and the table above is now understated in its favour.** The charge against it was C7's: one node cannot record *where along*, so segments are still required and room B holds two overlapping nodes with an ambiguous binding. **Ports answer that without single-hosting** --- room B holds the thread node plus a **port reference**, the port names exactly which segment, and the where-along record lives in the rope's interior graph. Multi-hosting under ports is also cheaper than priced: the rope is *listed* in two rooms but there is exactly **one rope graph**, so nothing interior is duplicated --- closer to a presence index than to real multi-hosting. **Clause 1b therefore takes a new hit: not a falsification, but its competitor got materially cheaper.** The narrowed dichotomy below is unaffected; the choice *within* it shifts.

**Position 2 acquired a structural realisation the same day: [P2](#proposal-p2-separate-the-storage-shard-from-the-containment-relation).** `positionGraph` stopping at host boundaries is the clean traversal model; `positionCache` is the expanded denormalization that makes it fast. **That is what position 2 was describing without having a mechanism for**, and it is the first thing in this design to *pass* the [clean/dirty](#clean-fractal-dirty-implementation) test rather than be excused by it.

**One reason position 2 is cheaper than it first sounds:** C7's derived-presence question already pushes *description* toward traversal independently --- something must work out that a thread passes through room B. If description traverses anyway, reference traversing too is one mechanism serving both rather than a new cost.

#### Candidate: drop clause 1b, and the whole chain with it

**Recorded 2026-08-06 as a candidate, not adopted --- and narrowed the same day to *extent only*.** Follows directly from the entailment above: if 1b generates 4 and 5, then declining 1b removes all three.

**Scope correction, [C8](#c8-the-flashlight-that-vanished-when-someone-looked-at-it).** As first written this candidate was derived **entirely from C7**, an extent case, and generalised without being checked against a containment case sitting in the same document. Applied to a flashlight it is destructive: decomposition would make the whole non-positional, so the flashlight leaves the room's graph, affordance compose describes three components, and --- because the referent catalog reads the same `objectIds` --- **"pick up the flashlight" stops grounding**. Looking closely at a thing would make it unreferrable, while fictionally nothing happened.

**So the candidate holds for extent and inverts for containment:** extent makes the *whole* non-positional, containment makes the *parts* non-positional. Positional status looks like a property of the **composition kind**, not a global rule. ~~which is AB-16's distinction surfacing positionally~~ --- **that inference was withdrawn 2026-08-06 and must not be re-drawn from here.** This paragraph is a *scoping decision about this candidate*: the candidate breaks containment, so it is confined to extent. Reading the resulting asymmetry back as evidence that the two part kinds differ is circular, because the asymmetry is what this paragraph just imposed. See **AB-16** for the full withdrawal. Everything below is therefore scoped to **extent wholes**; the LCA machinery it would remove is correspondingly confined to **scattered containment** rather than eliminated. **Untested premise:** that containment and extent are cleanly separable --- a ship's rigging may be both.

**The observation that motivates it: nothing reads the whole's host.** P1 states Area-hosted objects presumably never surface in affordances. [Part-mediated reference](#part-mediated-reference-how-h1-answers-its-own-replacement-trigger) establishes that the whole is never catalog-visible and is reached by **traversal from a part**. Description reaches it the same way. So neither the reference path nor the description path consults clause 4's host --- **it is bookkeeping nothing reads.**

**The alternative.** The whole becomes a **non-positional record**: a `Meta` row carrying the member set and the part-connection graph, *not* a node in any position graph. Parts remain graph nodes; the whole is structure **over** them. It then has no host, and clauses 4 and 5 do not arise.

**This is already a listed option, not a new invention.** AB-4 asks whether the level-crossing relation is membership with an Object host, a new edge kind, **or something that is not an edge at all** --- and the third option is exactly what makes the whole non-positional. It has been in the row since it was written.

**What it would buy:**

| Gain | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **P1's gains survive untouched** | All four --- gate, planner, `toHostId`, compose --- require only that no object *is* multi-present. A whole that is neither a graph node nor an adjacency row cannot be multi-present at all |
| **AB-25 deflates** | It exists almost entirely to serve clause 4, and is currently both H1's critical path **and** its only live rollback trigger. It would not vanish (AB-30's walls may still need containment answers) but would stop being what H1 lives or dies on |
| **Trigger 3 evaporates** | No migration means no cascade, no concurrent-migration race, and no thrash --- three queued test cases dissolve rather than needing to be run |
| **No `EphemeraAreaId` needed for this purpose** | P1's largest single unbuilt dependency was an ephemera-side Area identity, required so wholes had somewhere to host |

**What has to be true for it to work --- and both are real questions, not formalities:**

1. **Composition must live in the record, not in position-graph edges.** ~~If wholes can themselves be parts of bigger wholes, this collapses.~~ **Decider corrected 2026-08-06 --- the original made the requirement/representation conflation this plan has just named.** Wholes-nest is **answered yes** (AB-32) and the candidate **survives it**: a record-based whole holds a member set of ids, an id can be another whole's, so nesting is free and hosts recurse to leaf parts. **The real decider, as a requirement: must a relation ever attach to a *whole* rather than to one of its parts?** Relations need nodes, so a yes forces wholes to be nodes and this collapses. Every case so far says no; "the place setting is on the table" is the untested shape. **The candidate still hinges on AB-4** --- it is that row's third option carried to its consequence --- but by way of AB-32.
2. **Lifecycle needs an owner.** With no host, what cleans up a whole when its parts are all deleted? Orphan-sweep shaped, and the objects lane has precedent (delete-only repair, the S1 compensating delete), but it is currently the whole's host that would make it discoverable.

**Clause-targeted tests this framing suggests**, replacing the habit of building one elaborate scenario and hoping it stresses something:

- **Clause 1a** --- hunt the corpus for a spanning thing that **resists decomposition**. If none exists, 1a holds and is close to free. AB-30's wall is the nearest candidate and is already excluded from the decomposition story, which is suggestive.
- **Clause 1b** --- the decisive question is not a scenario but **AB-4**: does composition live in the record or in position-graph edges? Record means [the whole can be non-positional](#candidate-drop-clause-1b-and-the-whole-chain-with-it) and 1b, 4 and 5 all go; edges means wholes must be graph nodes and the chain stands. **Answer AB-4 before running any clause 4 or clause 5 test** --- three queued cases target machinery that may not need to exist.
- **Clause 2** --- dual-write drift: does the reverse index stay single-valued across a failed transaction, and does the parent index from [part-mediated reference](#part-mediated-reference-how-h1-answers-its-own-replacement-trigger) land on the same `Meta` row, making it a multi-writer hotspot?
- **Clause 4** --- AB-25's three sub-questions, of which the sharpest is that Area membership is **asset-stack dependent**: if the LCA is perspective-relative then an object's host is not world truth, which is disqualifying rather than expensive.
- **Clause 5** --- concurrent migration and thrash (both queued), plus AB-31's fission, where **one event migrates two wholes in opposite directions**.

**Why it was promoted rather than left open.** Two arguments, of unequal strength. The weaker one, stated first and repaired: *the route non-uniformity means any single consolidated representation beats the status quo.* True about the inconsistency, but **symmetric** --- it argues equally for consolidating on multi-host. What actually selects H1 is that the five sites are not symmetric in **cost**: under H1 the membership compile gate, the end-state planner, the relational host map, the singular `toHostId`, and per-room affordance compose all become **correct as written**, no changes at any of them; under framing (a) every one needs widening. Five sites that independently decided single-host is weak evidence that single-host is natural and strong evidence that widening is expensive.

**What it buys the design, beyond the code sites.** It predicts a specific answer to AB-1 rather than dissolving it. Extent stops being its own axis (the rope is parts plus a whole, so AB-8 collapses into AB-4/AB-26), and the functional-state aggregate plausibly shares the same *carrier* --- a generator-and-door system hosts at the Area by the same rule --- while the **relation kind** differs, since a generator is not *part of* a door. That is AB-1's "one substrate, distinct types above it" option, arrived at from a mechanism rather than chosen from a list.

**What it does not buy: net simplification.** H1 retires AB-8 and buys **AB-25**, which is not close to answered and is a bigger commitment than the proposal's wording suggests --- `EphemeraAreaId` does not exist ephemera-side at all, no room -> area index exists anywhere, and containment may not be a tree. It also makes **AB-9 acute** rather than deferrable (a rope and two rope ends in one graph; description must pick a level), and AB-9 is the scope-controlling row. Forcing AB-9 is arguably good. It is not a reduction, and the promotion should not be defended as one.

### What counts as a falsification case (learned the hard way, 2026-08-05)

Three cases in, two of the constructed ones had failed as tests --- one dissolved because it was not a test at all, one passed by acquiring dependencies --- while the corpus was meanwhile producing genuinely hard *scenarios* that H1 sailed through. **The diagnosis matters more than the individual cases: representational adequacy is nearly free, and it is not what H1 claims.**

**The tell is the tag count.** [C6](#c6-the-rope-the-candle-and-the-impromptu-timer) --- rope, candle, impromptu timer, about as baroque as play gets --- added **zero** `H1-dependent` rows. Everything it established is equally true under framing (a). **A case a competing model passes identically has no selective power over the choice between them.** It was measuring composition-in-general, which was worth doing, and was not a test of this hypothesis.

**Why "can it represent X" keeps passing.** Any sufficiently expressive graph model can express almost any situation --- there is always another node and another edge available. Fictional complexity is not structural difficulty. Meanwhile **H1's load-bearing claims are mechanism claims**: *the whole hosts at the node containing all its parts*, and *migration is automatic and bidirectional*. Nothing run so far has required the LCA to be **computed** rather than asserted to exist, or a migration to be **planned** against `computeStepSequenceFootprint`'s once-only footprint.

**So a case earns its place by being at least one of:**

| Kind | Test |
| --- | --- |
| **Discriminating** | H1 and framing (a) give **different** answers. If both handle it the same way, it is a composition case, not an H1 case --- file it as corpus, not as falsification. **Two clauses, and the second was added after the first proved insufficient. (1) Show the competing model's answer step by step; do not assert it.** "Framing (a) handles this trivially" turned out to be wrong on inspection --- (a) needed the same derivation H1 did. **(2) Then check whether the resulting state is *faithful*, not merely reachable.** Clause 1 alone still produced a wrong verdict on [C7](#c7-ariadnes-thread): the steps were shown, (a) came out cheaper, and the reason it was cheaper is that it **cannot represent the object** --- one node, no notion of where along the thread anything attaches. **An alternative that produces a wrong state cheaply is not winning.** Both failures were the same habit: evaluating the alternative on the dimension that is easy to count rather than the one that decides. |
| **Mechanism** | The LCA rule must actually run, and the resulting migration must be plannable in one footprint pass. |
| **Interaction** | H1 meets shipped machinery built assuming single-level objects --- carry closure's boundary-edge classification, the orphan sweep, Coyote's bulk clear, the eviction ladder. |

| **Carry closure meets a composition edge** *(queued --- clause 3)* | **Interaction.** Pick up a chest with a rope tied to it, the far end in the next room. [`boundaryEdgeOutcomes`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/positionGraph/expandValidate/interactionUnderTransfer.ts) classifies incident edges `carry` / `dissolve` / `defer` / `error` --- **is a composition edge a boundary edge, and which verdict?** This plan has three times found that discipline anticipating cases it was not written for (AB-28, AB-29, [C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends)). Worth deliberately testing somewhere it might not, rather than continuing to expect it. |
| **Concurrent migration** *(queued --- clause 5)* | **Mechanism.** Two characters holding opposite ends, both moving in the same instant. Each transaction recomputes the whole's host from its own view and locks a **different** footprint. Whether the retry sees a coherent state is a `MultiKeyUpdate` question rather than a modelling one --- and it is the kind of thing that is cheap to check now and expensive to discover in production. |
| **Migration thrash and its narration** *(queued --- clause 5)* | **Mechanism, and partly a product question.** A character pacing across a room boundary re-hosts the rope on every crossing. Nothing happened fictionally --- but the presentation kernel narrates on host change. If each crossing narrates, the design is visibly wrong to a player; if it does not, something must suppress it, and that suppression rule does not exist. |

**Rollback triggers, named up front so "dead end" is not decided by mood:**

1. **AB-25 resolves as *containment is not a tree and cannot be made one*** --- H1 then has no node at which to compute a host, and no amount of downstream cleverness recovers it. This is H1's critical path and its most likely cause of death.
2. ~~**A born-spanning case reads wrong** under permanent Area-hosting.~~ **Retired 2026-08-05 --- it was not a test** (see the falsification table above). **Replaced by: the whole becomes unreferrable while Area-hosted.** If "pull the rope" cannot bind to the rope in the state H1 exists to model, the design has moved the difficulty rather than solved it. **Recorded as a weakening, not a win:** retiring a badly-built trigger leaves H1 *less* falsifiable than this section originally claimed, and the replacement is a genuine substitute only if it is actually run. **Run 2026-08-05 and passed conditionally** --- see [part-mediated reference](#part-mediated-reference-how-h1-answers-its-own-replacement-trigger). **This trigger is now spent**, so trigger 1 (AB-25) is the only live one remaining, and H1's falsifiability now rests almost entirely on it. Weigh that when Phase 2 reaches AB-25: it is both H1's critical path and its last remaining test.
3. **The migration cascade proves unplannable** --- every part move recomputes its whole's host and may cascade to wholes-of-wholes, while `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs **once**, up front, and `MultiKeyUpdate` cannot be re-entered. If plan-time upward walking cannot pre-lock the affected hosts, H1 is unimplementable on the shipped kernel regardless of how well it models the fiction. **This trigger got *more* likely on 2026-08-05, not less.** [C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends) shows parts persist through migration rather than dissolving, so dragging a chained chest out of a cell and back cycles the whole up to the Area and down again with the part structure intact --- **a high-frequency operation on a structure that is not changing.** Had reabsorption coincided with migration-down, cascades would at least have been rare events at decomposition boundaries. They are not; they are ordinary movement. Weight trigger 3 accordingly when Phase 2 reaches AB-25.

### Part-mediated reference: how H1 answers its own replacement trigger

**Proposed and assessed 2026-08-05.** Written here rather than inside a row because it touches AB-6, AB-11, AB-13 and AB-15 at once --- same reason [P1](#proposal-p1-single-host-objects--hosting-at-the-containing-node) is written up separately.

**Defect found 2026-08-06, and it is in this scheme's *framing* rather than its mechanics.** As introduced, part-mediated reference was the answer **for spanning wholes**, while local `graph.objectIds` lookup stayed the unexamined norm for everything else --- **two reference paths, never reconciled.** That is [position 3](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs), the one an epicycle argument correctly kills: a second path for a subclass rather than an optimisation of one rule. **The fix is not to discard the scheme but to promote it**: make traversal the *clean* rule for all reference, with zero hops in the local case (an undecomposed flashlight's node *is* the whole), and let today's lookup be a fast path with a stated validity condition. Everything below then describes the general rule rather than a special case.

**The scheme.** In any room where "pull the rope" is a sensible thing to do, **a part of the rope is present** --- an end, a span. That part takes the identification-grounding, because it is what is locally there. It carries a **parent index** naming the whole it belongs to. Reference walks *up* that index to the rope object, which then accumulates the state of its parts across every room in order to evaluate what pulling does.

**Why this is the better answer, not merely an answer.** The alternative was to make Area-hosted wholes surface in room affordances, which would require compose to reach up the containment tree and would drag non-present things into every room's catalog. Under part-mediated reference, **P1's "Area-hosted objects never surface in affordances" stays true as written.** The whole is never in a catalog; reference reaches it by traversal rather than by presence.

**What it costs, checked against shipped machinery:**

| Step | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Part is catalog-visible in its room | **Works today, unchanged** --- the part is an Object node, so `roomObjectCatalogForCharacter`'s `positionGraph.objectIds` scan already finds it |
| Grounding binds "rope" to the part | **The one real gap.** See the naming seam below |
| Part -> whole traversal | **New**, a `Meta::Object` parent field. Cheap, but a new dual write with the usual atomicity and drift story |
| Whole -> all parts, all hosts | **New**, and it forces AB-6 (below) |
| "Pull" applied across hosts | **Shape exists.** `MultiKeyUpdate` already folds N effects into one `transactWrite` |

**It answers AB-11 with the cheap option.** "Pull" is not a new step kind --- the whole **expands** into per-part steps, which is precisely Synthesize's Expansion sub-role, already growing one ungrounded change into an ordered sequence for carry closure and `sameHost` repair. **The kernel's step vocabulary does not grow.** That is the outcome AB-11 exists to determine, reached by a scheme that was not designed to reach it.

**Three consequences it forces, recorded so they are owned rather than rediscovered:**

1. **AB-6 resolves toward *stored*.** Evaluating the pull needs every part and its host known **before** the walk begins, because `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs once, up front. A *derived* member set cannot supply that without already knowing where to look.
2. **The parent index is AB-25's missing upward direction, one level down.** AB-25 records that containment is authored downward with no room -> area index; this is the same gap at object scale. **Distinct from** the reverse *edge*-reference index the plan has flagged three times --- different index, same missing direction.
3. **The naming seam, and it is the step that does not fall out of existing machinery.** The catalog matches on `normalizedShortName`. The player says "pull the **rope**"; what is present is a part. If the part's short name is "rope end," the whole's name is not in the catalog and grounding has nothing to bind to. Candidates: parts alias the whole's short name, a two-phase match consults the parent index, or the catalog widens to include reachable wholes. **This is AB-15 arriving concretely** --- unqualified reference should bind to the authored level, and under H1 the authored level is exactly the thing not locally present.

**And one smaller consequence: make the parent field plural from the start.** Multiple parents (AB-13) means a part belonging to both a rope and a knotted pair, where "pull the rope" is ambiguous. The ambiguity can be deferred; the field shape should not be, since widening it later is the more expensive order.

**Trigger 2 therefore passes --- conditionally, and the condition is the point.** The trigger asked whether the whole stays referrable while Area-hosted. It does. But it passes *because* AB-6 resolves to stored, AB-15's binding question has a workable answer, and the parent index gets built. **H1 survives by acquiring three dependencies, not by needing nothing** --- a normal outcome for a hypothesis doing real work, recorded as conditional so a later reader does not read "passed" as "free."

**The failure mode this section is designed against is sunk cost, not error.** Conditional adoption goes de facto permanent once the dependent set is large enough that rollback means redoing fifteen answers --- at which point H1 survives on accumulated cost rather than merit, and this section documents a decision nobody is willing to act on. **The mitigation is ordering, not discipline:** run the two falsification cases *next*, while the rollback set is still near-empty. The tagging is insurance for what accumulates afterwards, which is when insurance is worth buying.

**Rollback mechanics.** IDs are stable and never reused, so withdrawing H1 does **not** renumber anything: every `H1-dependent` row returns to **Open** with a note naming H1 as what invalidated it, exactly as [Phase 3](#recommended-order) already does for rows a later answer invalidates. The branch point is this section.

### Candidate H2, not adopted: AB-12's privileged-level reframe

Named here so the next load-bearing candidate is explored through this mechanism rather than re-inventing one ad hoc. **AB-12** --- everything is an Object at *some* level --- has the same shape as H1: it would dissolve rows rather than answer them, and it is currently recorded as "do not treat as settled" with no way to explore it conditionally. **Deliberately not promoted yet:** two hypotheses in flight at once would make the dependent sets overlap, and a row tagged both `H1-dependent` and `H2-dependent` cannot be rolled back as a unit --- which is the whole mechanism. Promote H2 only after H1 resolves, or in place of it if H1 dies.

## Proposal P2: separate the storage shard from the containment relation

**Recorded 2026-08-06 as a proposal, not adopted, and deliberately *not* promoted to a working hypothesis** --- H1 is still in flight, and two hypotheses at once break the rollback mechanism for the reason [candidate H2](#candidate-h2-not-adopted-ab-12s-privileged-level-reframe) records. P2 is written up here rather than inside a row because it touches AB-2, AB-3, AB-4, AB-9, AB-16, AB-30 and AB-32, and because it reframes what [the three reference positions](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs) were arguing about.

### The conceptual error it starts from: hosting creates a phantom edge

Membership is expressed by **containment in a collection**, not by an edge. [`ephemeraMeta.ts`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraMeta.ts):

```typescript
export type EphemeraPositionGraphData = {
    hostId: EphemeraMembershipHostId;
    nodes: EphemeraPositionGraphNode[];
    edges?: EphemeraPositionRelationalEdgeData[];
}
```

`hostId` is a **scalar sibling** of `nodes` --- the host is not in its own node list --- and `EphemeraPositionRelationalEdgeData`'s `from`/`to` are both `EphemeraObjectId`. So every node carries an implicit *contents-of* link to its host that is **not merely unlabeled but untypeable**: the edge could not be written into `edges` even without a kind, because one endpoint has no node to be.

**Three consequences, and the first is the strongest evidence.**

1. **The phantom is already materialized in storage, with the label slot designed out.** [`ephemeraPositionAdjacency.ts`](../../../../../packages/mtw-interfaces/ts/ephemeraPositionAdjacency.ts)'s `EphemeraPositionAdjacencyRow` is `{ EphemeraId: containedId, DataCategory: 'POSITION#' + hostId }` --- an edge record with a from, a to, and **no third column**. The link was recognized as a link and then flattened into a two-column index. That is a stronger claim than absence: it shows the shape was chosen, not overlooked.
2. **There is no containment relation kind anywhere.** `HostRelationalEdgeKind = 'On' | 'Under' | 'Against' | 'Custom'` --- support and adjacency, no `In`, no `Contains`, no `PartOf`. The system cannot currently say "in" about *anything*. So `part-of` would not be displacing a competitor; it would be the first relation of its family, and **AB-4's whole question is being asked in a vocabulary that has no word for the answer.**
3. **It is the third angle on a defect already recorded.** [`positionGraph/AGENT.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/positionGraph/AGENT.md) notes "No root concept --- and a rooted graph already exists next door," with `CarryClosureFragment = {rootId, members, edges}` as the shape the class has no root to lend. **A host node is that root**, which makes this **AB-3**'s pre-registered trigger, arriving from the hosting side rather than the closure side.

### Objects will be hosts --- and that makes the phantom urgent rather than hypothetical

`EphemeraMembershipHostId = EphemeraRoomId | EphemeraCharacterId`, whose own doc comment reads *"v1 eligible membership hosts (room + character inventory; **OBJECT# / AREA# later**)."* **Read that as a roadmap, not a limit.** This game is in Zork's lineage; "put the lantern in the box" is not an optional feature, so object-hosting is coming.

**And object-hosting built on the phantom model walks straight into [position 3](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs).** If `OBJECT#` joins the union as currently shaped, `BOX` gets its own `positionGraph` and containment is expressed by **graph nesting** --- phantoms all the way down, one per level, none labeled, none traversable as an edge, and "what is in the room" becomes a recursive read across N shards. That is single-hosting with lookup as the clean rule: the position the same-mechanism argument kills. **So the sequencing matters more than the limitation did** --- every nesting level added first is another site to unwind later.

### What is actually wrong: one construct doing two jobs

The host union is **not** arbitrary, and the reason is worth stating before proposing to change it. Room and Character are both **things that move while retaining their contents** --- a character walks between rooms with an inventory. That is a real sharding justification, and it is exactly why a pick-up-able box belongs in the union too. **The roadmap is coherent *as storage*.**

The problem is that the same construct also carries the containment *relation*:

| Job | What it needs | Fractal-compatible? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Storage shard** | A boundary that travels with a mobile container | Yes --- physical, and traversal should not see it |
| **Containment relation** | A labeled, traversable, nestable link | **No** --- a collection cannot carry a kind |

**Separate them and both get better.** Shard by mobility, keeping `MultiKeyUpdate`'s `transactWrite` unit intact; express containment as labeled edges within whatever shard holds them.

### The proposal, in three clauses

1. **`positionGraph` is strictly constrained not to extend into nodes that are themselves hosts.** The graph stops at each host boundary. **Already the documented fractal** --- [`AGENT.concepts.md`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/AGENT.concepts.md#fractal-position-graphs-container-scale-and-edges) has `Area.positionGraph -> Room.positionGraph -> Container graph`, with "Object `OBJECT#` / Area hosts deferred until needed." This clause promotes a description to a constraint.
2. **`positionCache` is a denormalized read structure** holding, *as useful to optimization*, more expanded graphs that show the deeper structure across multiple host nodes. **Not documented anywhere today** --- this is new design, not recall.
3. **Edges in a `positionGraph` may address a specific *part* of a node, without that part being listed as a node in the host holding the edge.** "Rope" is a node in the room's graph; `rope: rope-end` is an endpoint of a `tied to` edge in that same graph.

### Why clause 3 is the prize: it decouples decomposition from membership

This is the mechanism the design has been missing, and it clears four open items at once:

| Open item | Under part-addressed endpoints |
| --- | --- |
| **AB-9 made acute** --- "you see a rope, a rope end, and a rope end" | **Dissolves.** One node; the ends are endpoint qualifiers, not members. Nothing to select between at the membership layer |
| **[C7](#c7-ariadnes-thread)** --- room B holds both a thread node and a segment, "pull the thread" ambiguously bound | **Dissolves.** One node plus part-qualified edges. The cost [position 1](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs) was charged disappears |
| **[C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends)** --- three nodes in the cell's graph, permanently | **Refined.** One chain node and two edges addressing `chain: end-a` / `chain: end-b`. The "permanent multi-level steady state" was an artifact of parts needing to be nodes |
| **[C8](#c8-the-flashlight-that-vanished-when-someone-looked-at-it)** --- improvising interior detail unhosts the whole | **Dissolves structurally.** Part-qualified structure is added; the flashlight node never leaves the room |

**Four cases, one mechanism, and none of them was what it was designed for.** P1 never did that. Weigh it accordingly --- but see the caution below, because one of the four is doing less work than it appears.

### Where clause 3 risks recreating the very defect it fixes

If `rope: rope-end` appears in an edge and parts are nodes nowhere, then **part existence is asserted by reference** --- an endpoint naming something with no declaration. That is structurally the same defect as an unlabeled host link, one scale down.

**The reading that avoids it, and it is the one this plan adopts for the write-up:** `rope: rope-end` is an **address into the rope's own graph**. The rope hosts its parts exactly as the box hosts its contents --- same mechanism, and consistent with objects becoming hosts. The room's edge then reaches *across* a host boundary **by address rather than by node**, clause 1 still holds because the room's graph contains no part-node, and part identity has a home. **So "objects will be hosts" turns out to be load-bearing for the part story as well as for `put lantern in box`** --- a second, independent argument for it.

**The unresolved half, and it is the next topic rather than a gap to paper over.** Clause 3 as stated cleanly handles the **within-host** case: the rope is a node in the room, and the tie attaches to one of its ends. The **cross-host** case is not settled --- if Ariadne's thread hosts once, an edge in room B addressing one of its segments has an endpoint outside room B's graph, which is exactly what clause 1 forbids traffic in. **Making addressing work in all directions is the open problem**, and the candidate on the table is **ports**: a host object exposes named ports living on its *boundary*, whose job is to connect outside references to inside addresses. Recorded as **AB-34**; not yet assessed.

### Considered and rejected: flatten instead of nest

Raised and withdrawn the same day, kept because the reason is the load-bearing one. **The alternative:** let the room's graph hold `box`, `lantern`, and the `In` edge between them --- nesting expressed as edges *within* one graph rather than as graphs within graphs. It gets uniform traversal with no cross-shard hop.

**It is worse than what exists, and mobility is why.** Under host-nesting, picking up the box moves one shard and its contents come along free. Under flattening, every pickup **migrates N nodes and edges** out of the room's graph into the character's. It optimizes the read path by breaking the write path that host-binding exists to make cheap. **The middle path keeps shard-mobility *and* gets uniform traversal, by paying for reads in the cache rather than in the structure.**

### The invariant that keeps clause 2 honest, with local precedent

[`packages/mtw-wml/documentation/schema/README.md`](../../../../../packages/mtw-wml/documentation/schema/README.md) records this codebase backing **out** of denormalizing child data upward --- "an enormous source of unnecessary complexity. Avoid the temptation" --- replaced by **selectors** computing condensed views from the source-of-truth tree. Different subsystem, and the analogy is limited: that was denormalization into *authored source*, not a runtime cache. But the discipline it landed on is exactly what clause 2 needs.

**Pin it as a rule, not an aspiration: the write path never touches `positionCache`; it is invalidate-and-rederive only.** Otherwise the cache becomes a second truth and the phantom returns as a coherence problem --- the same failure one layer up. Tracked as **AB-35**.

### How it relates to H1, and to the clean/dirty test

**P2 does not decide H1, and does not compete with it.** Clause 3 is orthogonal to single-hosting: it changes what parts *are* (addresses, not necessarily nodes), which changes what clauses 1b, 4 and 5 **cost** without settling them. Clause 1a and clause 3-of-H1 (spanning things are parts) survive unchanged.

**What P2 does supply is the structural realisation of [position 2](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs)** --- traversal as the clean rule, lookup as the optimisation. `positionGraph` is the clean fractal; `positionCache` is the deliberate dirt. **That makes it the first thing in this design to *pass* [the clean/dirty test](#clean-fractal-dirty-implementation) rather than be excused by it:** a cache returning what the traversal would have returned is provably "the clean answer, computed faster," not a model change wearing dirt's clothing.

### What it does not resolve

- **Cross-host addressing** --- AB-34, the ports question, and the immediate next topic.
- **Cache invalidation and derivation scope** --- AB-35.
- **Whether the host link becomes an actual edge, or the host merely becomes a node** --- AB-33. The proposal argues the relation should be labeled and traversable; it does not settle the representation, and that is deliberately the same discipline AB-4 / AB-32 just imposed on composition.
- **Lifecycle of a whole with no host** is untouched --- still open on [the non-positional-whole candidate](#candidate-drop-clause-1b-and-the-whole-chain-with-it).

## Provisional P3: ports as host-boundary bindings

**Recorded 2026-08-06 as a provisional, deliberately ahead of its open questions.** This is **AB-34**'s candidate answer, worked out far enough to be argued against. **Not promoted to a working hypothesis** --- H1 is still in flight, and the reason [candidate H2](#candidate-h2-not-adopted-ab-12s-privileged-level-reframe) gives applies unchanged. The nine questions below are recorded **as questions**: several have an obvious-looking answer and a leaning is noted where one exists, but **no leaning here is a decision**, and they are to be worked rather than assumed.

### The requirement, stated independently of any hosting model

**This is what makes ports worth designing before H1 resolves.** Whether wholes are multi-host, single-host, or hosted at an Area, there is a **common requirement to address relations and links across a containment boundary, in both directions:**

- A whole at a **non-local** level --- relations to the rope *as a whole* must jump **out** of the room's graph. Part relationships especially: either they cross a level, or composition needs an entirely separate graph system.
- A whole at a **local** level --- addressing its parts means reaching **in**, to things contained not in the room but inside the rope's own parts list.

Both directions are the same problem, and no hosting decision removes either.

### The scheme

Numbered **ports** act as ingress/egress borders, turning a cross-`positionGraph` connection into something traversal can parse. Worked example, taking multi-host presence for the moment (see [the consequence for H1](#consequence-ports-re-price-the-three-positions-table) --- that assumption is not neutral):

- A rope is present in `ROOM#A` and `ROOM#B`.
- `ROOM#A`'s graph holds a **`tied to`** edge between a rock and **`OBJECT#ROPE:1`** --- port 1.
- `OBJECT#ROPE`'s own `positionGraph` holds: a **root node** for `OBJECT#ROPE` itself; a **`part`** edge from the root to `OBJECT#RopeEnd1`; and a **`tied to`** edge from **port 1** to `OBJECT#RopeEnd1`.
- Traversal composes those into an edge from an object in `ROOM#A` to a part inside `OBJECT#ROPE`.
- **Interior structure:** `part` links from the root to every part, plus **sibling links** between parts expressing how they connect.
- **Candidate `present` edge kind:** "this part is part of the object's presence in this room."

**Two properties fall out, and they are the design's stated goal:** looking at the rope alone tells you **(a)** how its parts are connected and **(b)** where and how they connect externally.

#### Ports are single-use, and that is not a detail (clarified 2026-08-06)

**A port records *one* ingress/egress between two position graphs --- it is not a general-purpose named interface with fan-out.** If `ROOM#A` connects to both `RopeEnd1` and `RopeSpan1`, that is **two ports**, not one port carrying two interior edges. An earlier reading of this section treated ports as reusable boundary interfaces; that reading is wrong and several assessments made under it needed correcting.

**The consequence that changes the most: on the egress side, a port can tell you which position graph it exits to.** So a whole's own graph **enumerates its referrers** --- the port list *is* the reverse index, stored exactly where it is needed. That is why **PQ-6, the blocking item, largely dissolves**, and with it PQ-7's surviving residue.

**Why that lands harder than convenience:** `computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs **once, up front**, and `MultiKeyUpdate` cannot be re-entered. A port-carrying graph can compute its own footprint at plan time --- open the rope, read its ports, and every host the operation touches is known. Fission therefore **cannot** silently release a port, because the port names `ROOM#A` and obliges the operation to include it. *A host may not release an externally-referenced port* becomes locally enforceable, which is exactly what it lacked.

**And it narrows the encapsulation argument, which this plan had overstated.** Ports make **interior repartitioning** non-breaking --- coiling rebinds port 1 from `RopeEnd1` to the root, entirely inside the rope. They do **not** make **identity changes** non-breaking: when fission turns the rope into `ROPE-upper` and `ROPE-lower`, an external edge naming `OBJECT#ROPE:1` must be rewritten to name `OBJECT#ROPE-upper:1`, because the object's identity changed --- which is true with or without ports. See the correction on **AB-31**.

#### The port record: split, complementary, with the interior authoritative (settled 2026-08-06)

**This is [PQ-1](#open-questions-ab-34-sub-questions-ids-stable-never-reused)'s answer.** It resolves the question the [single-use](#ports-are-single-use-and-that-is-not-a-detail-clarified-2026-08-06) clarification opened, and it avoids the trade that question appeared to force.

**The shape --- two halves holding *different* facts, each stored where it is read:**

| Half | Lives on | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Egress list** | the interior graph, as a new top-level element | port number -> outbound host: `[{ port: 1, host: ROOM#A }, { port: 2, host: ROOM#A }, { port: 5, host: ROOM#B }]` |
| **Ingress list** | ~~the exterior graph's **node**~~ **`positionCache`** (corrected 2026-08-06, below) | which of that object's ports are live *here* |

**Why this dodges the drift trade.** PQ-1's fork was "stored once in the whole" versus "split halves," where split meant two records asserting **the same** fact. These assert **complementary** facts, so the expensive read stays local: `ROOM#A` answers *what is here and how is it connected* without opening the bag. Opening it is required only to learn *which part* port 4 lands on, which is a detail-level question and exactly the thing that should cost a hop. **That is [position 2](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs) working as advertised, with the zero-hop case genuinely zero hops.**

**Two defaults the shape enables:**

1. **Failed addressing coarsens rather than dangles.** An edge to `OBJECT#BAG:4` where 4 is not a legal port --- or where no interior edge leaves port 4 --- resolves **identically to an edge to `OBJECT#BAG`**. *If the chain of addressing fails, the last successfully addressed host is the default target.* "Tied to the bag's strap" degrades to "tied to the bag," which is fictionally acceptable almost always. **Per-hop:** each graph resolves one hop and coarsens locally; a failure does not unwind a whole chain.
2. **A port with no exterior edge is a pure presence indicator**, when the interior carries a `present` link from that port. Better than an optional-endpoint field, because **the absence of an edge *is* the signal** --- no second record shape. [C7](#c7-ariadnes-thread) in a corridor: the room's THREAD node carries port 7, the thread's graph has `port 7 -> present -> ThreadSpan3`. Present, attached to nothing.

**Authority: the interior egress list wins.** It is the half that must be complete for plan-time footprint computation (`computeStepSequenceFootprint` runs once), and the half whose lifecycle invalidates ports.

~~**The ingress list is therefore a denormalization inside truth, and that is accepted deliberately** --- a materialized view in `positionGraph` rather than in cache, on the `Meta`-placement precedent, with the `positionCache` alternative rejected because the room's local read is too hot to depend on cache warmth. The drift obligation is tracked as PQ-11.~~

**Reversed 2026-08-06, one exchange after being settled --- the rejection reason did not survive inspection.** The room's local read was the justification, and **the room's own edges already carry what a coarse read needs**: `boulder -TiedTo-> ROPE:4` is in the room's graph and names the rope, so "a rope is tied to the boulder" renders with no ingress list at all. The list's unique contribution is knowing which ports are live here *including presence-only ones* --- **a where-along fact, and detail costing a hop was the deal.** The thing put in truth to protect the hot path turns out not to be on it.

**The strength argument, which is the general form.** A denormalization earns a place in truth by what it saves. The ingress list is **weak** in exactly the sense that matters: per record it surfaces information from **one** item, saving one read of an object already known to be present. The membership node is **strong**: it saves scanning every object in the world.

| | Saves | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The node** --- rope is present in Room A | scanning all objects --- **N:1** | strong; **stays in `positionGraph`** |
| **The ingress port list** --- ports 1, 2 live here | one read of an object already known --- **1:1** | weak; **belongs in `positionCache`** |

**So [C9](#c9-coiling-the-rope-back-in)'s rule --- *present iff at least one port egresses here* --- is a consistency invariant between the egress list and membership, not a derivation any reader performs.** A far smaller obligation than the drift repair it replaces: **PQ-11 is retired.**

**And there is a principled reading, not merely a tidier one: ingress-in-truth was [position 3](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs) growing back.** Materialising a lookup answer into truth so the common case need not traverse, while the uncommon case traverses, is *two rules where one would do* --- the shape this plan already caught itself in once, by accident. An analogy rather than an identity, but close enough to record, because it is the second time the same instinct produced the same defect.

**What the reversal costs, and why it is already covered.** A room's graph alone can no longer tell whether `boulder -TiedTo-> ROPE:4` names a live port --- that needs opening the rope. **Which is precisely the coarsening default's job:** it resolves to `ROPE` and renders "tied to the rope." The two decisions now support each other, which they did not before: with the ingress list in truth, a stale entry was a **phantom** the coarsening rule said nothing about. That asymmetry was the ugliest corner of the rejected branch and it goes with it.

**One rule that must not be inferred away.** The coarsening default is a **read-side resolution rule only**. It does **not** license relaxing BD-33/35's write-side assert-and-throw --- `removeObject` still refuses to drop a node an edge references, and a caller still owes an explicit dissolve first. The tempting inference is that robust reads make the write check unnecessary; they do not, because **a silently-coarsening world accumulates imprecision nobody notices**, which is the silent-wrong-answer class this plan has repeatedly chosen against. A smaller instance of the same: coarsening turns "pull this strap" into "pull the whole bag," correctly most of the time and silently always.

**Chaining falls out for free (PQ-5).** An interior graph addresses its own nodes' ports exactly as a room does, so a bag's interior edge from port 4 may target `ROPE:2`. No extra machinery.

#### What a port number is not (added 2026-08-06)

**Written because this section has now generated two misreadings of the same family, each of which propagated into assessments before it was caught.** The section says clearly what a port *is*; it said nothing about what a port number is **not**, and the negative turns out to be the load-bearing half.

A port number is a **boundary slot on the host object**, allocated by that host, meaningful only as one end of one crossing. It is **not**:

- **A name for the interior node behind it.** `OBJECT#ROPE:1` does not identify a part. The part is an ordinary nominal id --- `OBJECT#RopeEnd1` --- and the port merely has an edge to it. The two worked examples in this section are already symmetric on this point: `port 1 -[TiedTo]-> OBJECT#RopeEnd1` and `port 1 -[SolderedTo]-> OBJECT#BatteryCase`.
- **A reusable public interface.** One port records **one** crossing; two connections to the same host are two ports. See [Ports are single-use](#ports-are-single-use-and-that-is-not-a-detail-clarified-2026-08-06).
- **A fan-out point.** A port has exactly one interior edge and one exterior referrer.
- **Evidence about the interior at all.** Numbering is a property of the *boundary*; whether the interior identifies its parts positionally or nominally is a separate question (**PQ-9**), which the port answers neither way.

**The error this last bullet prevents actually happened.** The retired **AB-16** briefly held that extent parts are identified by *where* and containment parts by *what*, citing `ROPE:1` --- reading the boundary slot as the part's name. It was the last surviving argument for that row, and it was an artifact of this section.

#### A port is a scale boundary, not a relay (2026-08-06)

**The two edges joined by a port need not carry the same kind, and usually will not.** This reverses the leaning recorded on **PQ-1 (original)** and changes what a port is understood to be.

**The rope made matching kinds look necessary, and that was an artifact.** `boulder -TiedTo-> ROPE:1` and `port 1 -TiedTo-> RopeEnd1` agree because a rope end is *the same kind of thing* as a rope --- homogeneous extent, where a part is a smaller instance of the whole. Under homogeneity a port looks like an identity function, quietly relaying one relation inward.

**A heterogeneous case breaks it immediately.** A power cord run into a flashlight: the exterior edge is `PowerCord -[ThreadsInto]-> OBJECT#FlashLight:1`, and the interior edge is `port 1 -[SolderedTo]-> OBJECT#BatteryCase`. Both are true, neither is a copy, and they describe the connection at the level each graph operates on. **They are two relations at different scales, joined by the port** --- so a port is a **scale boundary at which the description changes**, not a relay.

**Consequence for storage: each side stores its own kind, and nothing is duplicated.** The earlier leaning ("the kind lives inside only") rested on avoiding a two-writer problem across two shards. **That premise is gone** --- if the kinds legitimately differ there is no shared fact to disagree about, and the consistency worry evaporates for the same reason the ingress list's did: complementary, not redundant. **No new mechanism is needed either:** the exterior edge already carries `HostRelationalEdgeKind`, and the interior edge is an ordinary edge in the flashlight's graph.

**Consequence for coarsening, and it is better than tolerable --- it is lossless at the level being read.** Under matching kinds, coarsening felt like degradation: *tied to the rope's end* -> *tied to the rope*. Under heterogeneous kinds there is no degradation at all: `PowerCord -ThreadsInto-> FLASHLIGHT:1` coarsens to `PowerCord -ThreadsInto-> FLASHLIGHT`, which reads **"a power cord threads into the flashlight"** --- unchanged and still correct. **Because the exterior kind was never about the interior part, coarsening loses only the interior fact, which was the detail by construction.**

**No compatibility matrix --- and this is an existing decision applying, not a new one.** The tempting next step is to police coherence across the boundary (is *threads into* compatible with *resting on*?). **Do not.** That is a small version of the compile-fictional-physics-into-deterministic-structure move **AB-19 already rejected**: an abstraction's job is to be the right-sized packet of context for an LLM step, not to replace it with a lookup. Cross-boundary coherence is authoring's and improvisation's responsibility; reasoning about consequences is the LLM step's.

**Two rules that follow.**

1. **The port is the unit of lifecycle, not either edge.** With independent kinds the halves can diverge under dissolution --- sever the exterior edge, leave the interior one, and a port is soldered to a battery case while exiting nowhere. Rather than defining that state, **releasing the port takes both halves.** Consistent with [single-use](#ports-are-single-use-and-that-is-not-a-detail-clarified-2026-08-06).
2. **Do not "optimize" the rope.** Someone reading rope data will see `TiedTo` on both sides and conclude the kind is stored redundantly. **It is coincidentally equal, not redundant.** Build nothing that assumes matching and nothing that assumes differing.

### The strongest argument for ports is not the traversal one

Direct addressing (`OBJECT#ROPE/OBJECT#RopeEnd1` written into the room's edge) would also satisfy traversal. **What ports add is encapsulation** --- and the payoff lands squarely on the two hardest lifecycle rows, neither of which ports were designed for:

| Row | Under direct addressing | Under ports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AB-17 reabsorption** | Coiling the rope dissolves `RopeEnd1`; every external edge naming it must be found and rewritten across hosts | Rebind port 1 to the root. **Nothing outside the rope changes.** One internal edit |
| **AB-31 fission** | The member set repartitions and external references dangle --- which is the question the row opened with | Ports rebind; external references survive by construction |

**The port is the object's published interface; its parts are the implementation.** Interior refactoring being non-breaking is exactly what those rows were failing to get.

### The root node is required, not incidental

The scheme needs `OBJECT#ROPE` to be a node in its own graph, because `part` edges must emanate from the whole. **That is AB-3's pre-registered trigger firing a third time** --- once from composition (where the trigger was written), once from hosting ([AB-33](#proposal-p2-separate-the-storage-shard-from-the-containment-relation)), now from ports. At which point `CarryClosureFragment = {rootId, members, edges}` is simply what a ported object's graph looks like.

### Consequence: ports re-price the three positions table

**The "assume multi-host for the moment" framing is not a neutral simplification.** [Position 1](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs) was charged with C7's objection: one node cannot record *where along*, so segments are still required, so room B holds both a thread node and a local segment with "pull the thread" ambiguously bound.

**Ports answer that objection without single-hosting.** Room B holds the thread node and a **port reference**; the port names exactly which segment; the where-along record lives in the rope's interior graph where it belongs. And multi-hosting under ports is cheaper than the plan ever priced it --- the rope is *listed* in two rooms but there is exactly **one rope graph**, so the interior is not duplicated. That is closer to a presence index than to real multi-hosting.

**So clause 1b takes a new hit --- not a falsification, but its competitor got materially cheaper.** The narrowed dichotomy stands; the choice *within* it shifts toward the branch that keeps lookup-as-the-clean-rule.

**A second consequence, on AB-25 and AB-26:** sibling links living inside the whole's graph give the part-connection graph a home **below** the rooms rather than above them. AB-26 no longer needs an Area-level carrier, which deflates AB-25 further --- Area was largely being summoned to host things above rooms. See **PQ-8** for the loose end in that claim.

### The motivation, stated plainly (2026-08-06)

**Ports are an attempt to find out whether the design can do without [clause 1](#h1-is-a-conjunction-and-its-clauses-are-tested-separately)** --- because multi-hosting of wholes looks, on reflection, **actually important**. Recorded because this plan's working-hypothesis mechanism depends on knowing *why* a candidate was built, and a scheme designed to make a rival framing viable should not be assessed as though it were neutral.

**This does not withdraw H1**, and nothing below is a decision. What it does is put a fourth, different kind of pressure on clause 1b, which is worth tallying in one place:

1. **The same-mechanism requirement** (AB-32) --- an argument from requirements, producing [the three positions](#three-positions-on-reference-and-what-clause-1b-actually-costs) and the narrowed dichotomy.
2. **Ports re-pricing position 1** --- the C7 where-along objection, which was the main charge against multi-hosting, is answered by ports without single-hosting.
3. **PQ-7's restatement** --- the cross-host enforceability cost turns out to belong to **clause 4** (entailed by 1b), and it vanishes under multi-host + ports.
4. **A stated judgment that multi-hosting matters** --- weaker than the others as evidence, and load-bearing as a statement of intent.

**What withdrawal would cost, so the decision is made with the number in view.** The `H1-dependent` set currently stands at **six rows: AB-6, AB-8, AB-9, AB-11, AB-15, AB-17** --- and AB-9's tag covers only the C6 escalation, not its question. That is a small set for a hypothesis this prominent, which is exactly the measurement [the tagging test](#the-tagging-test-and-why-it-is-the-point) was built to produce: **H1 has been deciding less than its position in this document suggests.** Note also that clause 3 (spanning things are parts) is *not* uniquely H1's and survives a withdrawal intact, as does 1a --- so what is actually at stake is 1b and its two entailments, not the hypothesis wholesale.

**Do not let ports decide this by momentum.** The scheme's independence from any hosting model is its stated virtue; if it also happens to make one model cheaper, that is evidence to weigh, not a conclusion to arrive at by having built it.

### The failure mode all nine are measured against

**An address with no declaration is [the phantom edge](#proposal-p2-separate-the-storage-shard-from-the-containment-relation) one scale down.** Ports exist precisely to give the address a declared home on both sides. Any answer above that leaves a reference implicit, unindexed, or unenforced re-introduces the defect the scheme was built to remove --- which is what makes PQ-6 and PQ-7 the blocking pair rather than two of nine.

### C1. Look at the character standing here; look at the north wall

The north wall is worse than unresolvable: it is not merely absent from the catalog, it has no node kind it *could* occupy.

**Found while verifying.** The same function takes the *character's own* room as `containers[0]` (line 70) --- a third silent single-host assumption, in the read path rather than the write path. Noted for C2 rather than developed here.

**Annotations.** Read-side --- part kind n/a (this is the participation question, upstream of composition) --- Improvisation, and specifically AB-21's **latent** flavor (a room has walls whether or not anyone wrote them) --- description --- exercises none of the three gaps directly, because it is upstream of minting: the thing cannot be *referred to*, so the question of whether it would be *minted* never arises.

### C2. The rope in two rooms, refused at the gate

**Origin:** the BD-16(3) `multiPresent` park.

**Today's model.** Adjacency stores `(OBJECT#, POSITION#ROOM#...)` rows with nothing unique-constraining them; `getMembershipContainers` returns an array. Multi-room presence is therefore **storable**.

**Limit hit --- and it is five layers disagreeing about one object, in three different ways.**

| Layer | Behavior on a two-room rope | Site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adjacency storage | Permits it | `getMembershipContainers` returns an array |
| **Membership compile** | **Terminal error to the player** | [`complexityPreGates.ts:29`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/complexityPreGates.ts) --- `containers.length > 1` -> `multiPresent` -> `preGateOutcomeToTerminalError` |
| **Relational compile** | **Silently omits it** | [`compileRelationalFromSkeleton.ts:168`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/compileRelationalFromSkeleton.ts) --- `if (containers.length === 1)` populates `hostByObjectId`; otherwise the object simply has no host for Expansion, and `getCurrentHostForExpansion` returns `undefined` |
| End-state planner | Scrubs every room but the target | `computeEndStateRoomDiff` --- `froms = priorContainers.filter(!== to)` |
| Presentation | Renders a whole rope in each room | Affordance compose reads `graph.objectIds` per room |

Refuse, ignore, scrub, duplicate --- four incompatible answers, none of them wrong locally. **This is the corpus's sharpest single argument that the level structure currently lives in each consumer's head rather than in the model**, which is the burden [Why this initiative exists](#why-this-initiative-exists) names.

**Note the gate is route-scoped, not global.** `evaluateComplexityPreGates` has exactly one caller, [`compileMembershipAtomic.ts:121`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/enrich/objectManipulation/compileMembershipAtomic.ts). The relational route and `executeObjectMove` / `orchestrateObjectMove` never reach it. The plan already recorded that the cross-host move route bypasses the end-state planner; the non-uniformity is therefore **worse than recorded** --- it is not one constraint applied unevenly but three different constraints on three routes.

**Annotations.** Write-side --- **homogeneous extent** (AB-16) --- Improvisation, downward and operation-driven (lowering the rope is what makes the ends separately addressable) --- mechanism --- exercises gap 1, **no composition relation**: improvisation would mint two rope parts as free-standing objects with nothing expressing that they are one rope.

**Rows grounded.** AB-8 (primary), AB-16, AB-25, AB-26, AB-27.

### C3. The snare trap Coyote cannot name

**Origin:** the Coyote genre-bounded reasoning context park.

**Today's model.** [`loadCoyoteRoomObjectsByRoom`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/coyoteGame/utilities/coyoteRoomObjectSnapshot.ts) walks `getGameRooms()`, and per room takes `[...graph.objectIds]`, stitching each into a `CoyoteStagedObject` of `{ objectId, shortName, stableKey, tropeAffinities? }`.

### C4. The place setting at the Faerie Court

**Origin:** the situational-meaning / mark-state-axis park.

**Today's model, and this corrects the plan's own premise.** The situational axis is **not** "built and unpopulated." It is **populated for Rooms and structurally absent for everything else**: [`requestIntake.ts:11`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/renderOrchestration/requestIntake.ts) states it as a rule --- "**Feature/Knowledge/Object/Character hosts:** no `Meta::Room`; always `markState: { markValue: [] }` and `allowGeneration: false`" --- and [`findRender.ts:95`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/renderOrchestration/findRender.ts) gates generation on `isEphemeraRoomId`. Rooms meanwhile carry real state: [`handleApiStateChange.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/state/handleApiStateChange.ts) merges `markState` into `Meta::Room.state.marks`, with [`computeDefaultMarksForRoom.ts`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/state/computeDefaultMarksForRoom.ts) supplying defaults.

**Limit hit.** The empty pin on non-Room hosts is **not a deferral waiting to be switched on** --- it is downstream of there being no state carrier. `Meta::Room` is the carrier, and no other host kind has one. So an interpretive frame can attach to *a room* and cannot attach to *a thing*: the Faerie Court can be a Faerie Court, but the place setting on its table cannot be situationally significant, because there is nowhere on the place setting for significance to live.

**This links AB-23 to AB-7 in a way the plan did not record.** Situational meaning for anything below room scale needs a diegetic-state carrier for non-Room hosts, which is AB-7's question --- flagged there as "possibly out of scope" and now with a second consumer.

**Annotations.** Read-side, becoming write-side exactly if Recognition mints --- heterogeneous separable (AB-16) --- Recognition --- **description in one frame and mechanism in the other, which is the case's whole point** --- exercises gap 3, `EMBEDDING#IMPROMPTU` being `shortName`-derived, since "the place setting" is the plan's own named example of a questionable embedding.

**A finding about the annotation scheme itself.** C4 does not have a description-vs-mechanism value. At an inn the place setting is inert and its recognition is pure projection under AB-9's guardrail; at the Faerie Court the same six objects carry consequence. **AB-19's tag is therefore a property of *(case, frame)*, not of the case** --- so a per-case column cannot hold it. This does not need a new row: AB-23 already says the member-affinity filter is context-dependent, and this is that statement arriving from the corpus side. It does mean Phase 2 should not read the AB-19 tally as a partition.

**Rows grounded.** AB-23 (primary), AB-7, AB-19, AB-22, AB-24.

### C5. The chain mounted at both ends

**Origin:** not a park --- raised in conversation 2026-08-05 as a sharper instrument than rope state (iv) for the same test. **This is the first [H1](#h1-single-host-objects-hosting-at-the-containing-node) falsification case run**, and it falsified a sub-claim; see below.

**The case.** A chain in a dungeon cell, mounted to the wall at one end and attached to a chest at the other. Later the chest is dragged into the next room and then dragged back.

**Why it is a better instrument than rope state (iv).** Re-coiling a rope changes co-location **and** load-bearing at the same moment, so it cannot distinguish them. The chain holds co-location fixed --- everything is in the cell --- while leaving the ends load-bearing. It isolates the variable.

**Today's model.** Not representable in full. Chain-end -> chest is an ordinary intra-host Relational edge and works today. Chain-end -> **wall** does not exist, because Features are not graph nodes at any tag ([C1](#c1-look-at-the-character-standing-here-look-at-the-north-wall)). So the case's more interesting anchor needs AB-12's Room/Feature half before it can be stated at all --- **noted rather than worked around**, since the half that can be stated is enough to carry the finding.

**What it establishes.** **Co-location and load-bearing are independent axes**, so migration-down and reabsorption are different events. All three nodes (chain, chain-end-1, chain-end-2) must persist in the cell's graph for the relations to have endpoints, *while* the whole is correctly hosted in the cell rather than the Area. Dragging the chest out and back cycles the chain up to the Area and down again **without ever dissolving a part**.

**The guard is already shipped, and it throws.** Reabsorbing a part means removing its node, and [`removeObject`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/positions/positionGraph/AGENT.md#known-limitation-deferred) **asserts and throws** `RelationalEdgeStillReferencedError` when a Relational edge still references it (BD-33/35, with deliberately no silent-strip variant). So dissolving chain-end-2 while it is attached to the chest is not a subtle data-loss bug --- it fails at the kernel. The load-bearing test for the relational half is therefore a **graph query** (`nodeHasRelationalEdge`, already on the class), not an LLM judgment, and the unsafe version is already impossible. Third instance of the same convergence AB-28 records: the boundary-edge discipline anticipated the case.

**A third decomposition trigger, previously unnamed.** The chain's ends exist *while everything is co-located*, so decomposition was not triggered by spanning. It was triggered by needing an endpoint **finer than the whole** to bear a relation --- "chain the chest to the chain" requires a chain-end. That is on-demand Improvisation under AB-14, and it restores the symmetry the granularity insight claimed while relocating the axis P1 guessed at: **parts exist while they are needed as distinct loci, by position *or* by relation; the whole hosts wherever it must to contain them.** Two independent questions that P1 collapsed into one.

**Annotations.** Write-side --- **homogeneous extent** (AB-16) --- Improvisation, on-demand and relation-driven (the newly named third trigger) --- mechanism --- exercises gap 1, **no composition relation**.

**Rows grounded.** AB-17 (primary --- splits its criterion), AB-9 (escalated from acute to routine), AB-14, AB-16, AB-12 (via the wall anchor), AB-27.

### C6. The rope, the candle, and the impromptu timer

**Origin:** not a park --- a scenario already used in conversation as a specimen of the hi-jinks creative players actually attempt. Raised 2026-08-05 as a hard test.

**The case.** Tie a rope to the bedpost. Throw the rope out of the window. Place a lit candle **under** the span of rope between bedpost and window. Climb down; wait for the candle to burn the rope through; retrieve most of the rope from below.

**What it establishes, and it is not what it was aimed at.** Offered as a test against AB-25, it is really **AB-13's** case: one part with two wholes, neither containing the other. **This is a DAG requirement, not a DAG preference** --- a tree cannot express it at all. It is stronger evidence than AB-13's existing fork example, because the fork's three wholes (place setting, silverware set, inheritance) are categories of one kind that a reader can talk into a nesting, whereas C6's two wholes are different **in kind**: the rope is a physical object and the impromptu timer is a mechanism.

**The constraint it puts on AB-25.** The two wholes host at **different heights** --- the rope spans room and garden so it hosts at the Area; the timer's members are both in the room so it hosts in the room. Walking up from the shared span therefore yields parents at inconsistent levels, so **hosting height does not correlate with composition depth** and no implementation may treat the parent index as "the next level up." AB-25's core question --- whether Room/Area containment is a tree --- is untouched.

**Granularity, again from the third trigger.** The candle is under the **span**, not an end, and the span must exist as a distinct locus *because the candle relation needs something to attach to*. That is [C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends)'s newly-named third decomposition trigger firing in a scenario built for an unrelated purpose --- two independent cases producing it is reasonable evidence it is real.

**Two gaps it opens.** Dissolution is **per-whole**: the timer dies when the candle burns out while the rope persists, so the shared span is released by one parent and retained by another, which AB-17 could not previously express. And the scenario's whole payoff --- the rope **burning through** --- is **fission**, one whole becoming two, which no row owned. Now **AB-31**.

**One separation this case forces, so the expensive claim does not ride in on the cheap one's evidence.** *The representation must permit the timer* --- multi-parent composition, per-whole dissolution, fission --- is a hard requirement, and C6 is strong evidence for it. *The system spontaneously recognizes rope-plus-candle as a timer* is AB-18's unbounded search, and C6 is only an aspiration for it. Same discipline AB-12 applies to the Object versus Room/Feature bets. Worth noting the case is nonetheless a good advertisement for AB-22's affinity filter: a candle carrying `trigger` aptness beneath a rope under tension is exactly the arrangement cheap to notice **without** a pattern library.

**It does not threaten H1.** Every object in it is single-hosted and every whole hosts at the node containing its parts. Recorded explicitly because H1's other two falsification cases are spent, and a hard case that H1 passes incidentally must not be miscounted as a test that was aimed at it.

**Annotations.** Write-side --- **both part kinds in one case** (homogeneous extent for the rope, heterogeneous separable for the timer's candle-plus-span) --- Improvisation for the span, **Recognition** for the timer --- mechanism, emphatically --- exercises gap 2, since the timer's body is empty and its member set is the whole of its content.

**Rows grounded.** AB-13 (primary, and its best example), AB-31 (opens it), AB-17, AB-25 (constrains), AB-16, AB-18, AB-22.

### C7. Ariadne's thread

**Origin:** the character-held rope end, run 2026-08-05 as [H1](#h1-single-host-objects-hosting-at-the-containing-node)'s first *discriminating* falsification case, and then recognised for what it actually is. **Naming it correctly is not decoration** --- the myth is a specification, and players who know it will attempt it.

**The case.** Theseus ties one end of a thread at the labyrinth entrance and unspools it as he walks, room by room, so he can retrace the route. Later he follows it back. Along the way: a bystander could trip over it, it could be cut, and someone could follow it *inward*.

**Why it is stronger than the two-room rope.** **(i)** It is *many* rooms, so it tests the mechanism at scale rather than at its minimum. **(ii)** Its entire purpose **is** the path --- "which rooms does this pass through" is not an incidental question about the object, it is the object's reason for existing. **(iii)** It is unspooled **progressively**, one threshold at a time, so it is the per-crossing cost iterated twenty times rather than paid once. **(iv)** It is **retraced**, making the read path a first-class operation rather than an afterthought.

**A structural convergence worth noticing.** The thread **is** **AB-26**'s part-connection graph made literal: a record of which exits were traversed, with the licensing relation on each connection. The data structure the plan proposes and the fictional object turn out to be the same shape. **And it sharpens AB-26's own correction:** the part graph is not *assumed* to be a sequence, but a thread's happens to be one, because a thread is unspooled continuously. A net's is not. So "not a sequence" means the shape cannot be presumed --- not that paths never occur.

**What the case establishes --- the bystander test.** Someone standing in a labyrinth room who is *not* Theseus tries to grab the thread trailing past.

| Framing | What happens | Cost | **Is the resulting state faithful?** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **(a) multi-host** | Add the room to the thread's container set; it becomes a node in that room's graph and is grabbable | One pure-add per room --- **but `MutationKernelTransferStep`'s singular `toHostId` means the kernel cannot express a pure-add today** | **No.** The thread is **one node**, so the bystander grabs the *entire thread*, not a middle. Attachments (tied at the entrance, tied to a pillar, held by Theseus) sit on three hosts with **nothing recording where along the thread each is**. A container set is unordered, and the route is not recoverable from exits --- a labyrinth has multiple paths. **(a) cannot represent this object.** |
| **H1** | There is no thread node to add --- the whole is at the Area and only *parts* are room-present. Requires **minting a span-part** in the room, placing it, connecting it via AB-26, updating the member set, recomputing the LCA | A write **per threshold crossing**, times the length of the labyrinth, each unwound on the way back subject to [C5](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends)'s load-bearing check | **Yes.** The bystander grabs the local segment; each attachment sits on a specific part; the part-connection graph records which exit each connection traverses, so the route is explicit. |

**Verdict, after two reversals recorded below: C7 is a *passed discriminating test of [H1 clause 3](#h1-is-a-conjunction-and-its-clauses-are-tested-separately)* --- spanning things are modelled as parts.** The strongest result a discriminating test can give: not that H1 survived, but that the alternative **broke**. It says nothing about clauses 1, 4 or 5, which were used in working the case and never stressed --- nothing here asks whether the LCA is computable or the cascade plannable. The axis it actually tests is **one node versus parts** --- and conceding parts lands in AB-8's framing (c), which H1 embodies. An (a)-with-segments variant would be framing (c) with multi-host still permitted, at which point single-host is a secondary question rather than a settled one.

**And "mint it lazily, on reference" does not rescue H1 --- it is circular.** A player cannot refer to what they cannot perceive, so the trigger cannot be reference. **The constraint that falls out is the durable finding: presence must be established at *description* time, not reference time**, because description is upstream of reference. That kills lazy minting rather than repairing it, since description runs on every look and mint-on-describe is eager minting with extra steps.

**Three live options, no choice made:** eager minting (a write per crossing); **derived presence** --- description walks the part-connection graph and collects the rooms the licensing relations traverse, which is AB-9's projection answer applied to *presence* rather than to level; or accept that only Theseus can interact with it, which is cheap and, for a thread lying across a floor, hard to defend.

**The finding that reframes the comparison: framing (a) needs the same derivation.** Someone must still infer that a character entering a room while holding one end means the thread now occupies that room --- a flat container set does not supply that either. **(a) stores the result at write time; H1 would compute it at read time.** So the real discriminator is **stored versus derived presence**, which is AB-6 one level over, and the trade is the familiar one: stored is cheap to read and needs drift repair, derived is always correct and pays per describe --- though the render cache already exists to absorb exactly that.

**Held and present are not mutually exclusive, and nothing can currently say both.** AB-4 records that today's membership is exclusive: an object is in a host. A thread is held by Theseus *and* lying across the floor of the room he is standing in. For a coin in a pocket exclusivity is right; for a spanning thing it is not. **This is the gap underneath the whole case**, and it is why both framings need an explicit step.

**Two more the case supplies cheaply.** **Cutting the thread** is AB-31's fission with the stakes made concrete --- the severed portion no longer connects to the entrance, so the *identity* question ("is this still Ariadne's thread?") has a real answer that matters to a player. And **the ball it unspools from** is a diminishing reservoir generating parts, which is an unusual composition shape the corpus has no other instance of; noted, not developed.

**Annotations.** Both sides --- read-side for retracing and description, write-side for unspooling --- **homogeneous extent** (AB-16) --- Improvisation, on-demand, per crossing --- mechanism --- exercises gap 1, and gap 3 for whether a thread gets a semantic embedding at all.

**Rows grounded.** AB-6 (stored vs derived, at presence level), AB-4 (held vs present), AB-8, AB-9, AB-15, AB-16, AB-26, AB-31.

### C8. The flashlight that vanished when someone looked at it

**Origin:** seed example 2, run 2026-08-06 against [the non-positional-whole candidate](#candidate-drop-clause-1b-and-the-whole-chain-with-it) --- which it broke. The case had been in this plan since day one and the candidate was never checked against it.

**The case.** Someone looks at Room A: a flashlight is there. They look *carefully* at the flashlight, and its parts are improvised --- battery, casing, bulb. **Is the flashlight now absent from the room, replaced by three components?**

**Under the candidate as written, yes, and worse than that.** Decomposition makes the flashlight a *whole*; a non-positional whole is not a graph node; so `graph.objectIds` for Room A holds battery, casing and bulb and no flashlight. Affordance compose describes three components. **And [`roomObjectCatalogForCharacter.ts:76`](../../../../../lambda/ephemera/dataSource/actions/roomObjectCatalogForCharacter.ts) builds the referent catalog from the same `objectIds`**, so "pick up the flashlight" stops grounding. **Looking closely at a thing makes it unreferrable.**

**The conceptual error: decomposition changed positional status while nothing happened fictionally.** Someone looked. The flashlight is still on the table.

**The finding --- containment and extent want *opposite* answers.**

| Composition kind | Positional | Non-positional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Containment** (flashlight, place setting) | the **whole** --- it is the thing in the room | the **parts** --- they are inside it |
| **Extent** ([Ariadne's thread](#c7-ariadnes-thread), rope, chain) | the **parts** --- each is somewhere | the **whole** --- it is not anywhere in particular |

~~This is **AB-16**'s heterogeneous-separable versus homogeneous-extent distinction surfacing as a *positional* difference --- evidence that the row's two part-kinds are genuinely distinct things.~~ **Withdrawn 2026-08-06, and this sentence is the exact site of the error.** The comparison put the *shipped* host-bound model on the containment side against the [unadopted drop-clause-1b candidate](#candidate-drop-clause-1b-and-the-whole-chain-with-it) on the extent side, and --- worse --- **this case is what scoped that candidate to extent in the first place**, so the asymmetry it reports is one this case imposed rather than found. **AB-16 was retired on the strength of this correction.** What C8 actually shows is undamaged and stands below: decomposition must not silently un-ground a referent.

**What it does to the candidate: narrows it, does not kill it.** The non-positional whole was derived **entirely from C7**, an extent case, and generalised without being checked against a containment case sitting in the same document. Not wrong --- **overgeneralised from one part-kind**, which is the instance-taken-for-the-category error already twice in this plan's catalogue. ### C9. Coiling the rope back in

**Origin:** the rope's fourth state, owed since Phase 0 was written, run 2026-08-06 against [P3](#provisional-p3-ports-as-host-boundary-bindings) on the **multi-host** framing. Unlike C5--C8 this is not a falsification case: it is a **worked operation**, and the interesting result is what it does *not* require.

**The state.** `OBJECT#ROPE` has parts `RopeEnd1`, `RopeSpan`, `RopeEnd2`, in Rooms A, B and C. The rope's own graph carries three ports --- 1 -> `ROOM#A`, 2 -> `ROOM#B`, 3 -> `ROOM#C` --- each **presence-linked** to the corresponding part. Each room's graph holds an `OBJECT#ROPE` node whose ingress list names the port live there.

**The operation.** Standing in Room A, haul the rope in. `RopeSpan` moves B -> A: **change port 2's egress to `ROOM#A`** --- and that is the entire write, since [the ingress list lives in `positionCache`](#the-port-record-split-complementary-with-the-interior-authoritative-settled-2026-08-06), not in the rooms' graphs. `RopeEnd2` moves C -> B the same way, and because that was `ROOM#C`'s last port, **the `OBJECT#ROPE` node leaves `ROOM#C` entirely.**

**The write count, stated precisely, because it is where the transaction footprint lives.** **Relocation *within* the spread is one record** --- rewrite the port's egress host, nothing else. **Crossing a boundary is two**, because a room's port count going 0 -> 1 or 1 -> 0 changes membership, which stays in `positionGraph`. So hauling a rope through the middle of its own span is nearly free, and only its endpoints cost.

**The result, and it is the case's point: no edge changes at all.** The `part` links and sibling links are untouched. **Structure and position are independent** --- the rope's internal model is invariant under moving through the world, and only presence changes. Today's model cannot express this: relocating a segment means removing a node from one graph and adding it to another, and since relational edges are same-host-only, a segment carrying a relation needs dissolve-and-recreate.

**Scope of "no edge changes," and the fiction agrees with the limit.** It holds for **presence-only** ports. Were `RopeSpan` tied to a tree in Room B, the tie is `tree -TiedTo-> ROPE:2` in B's graph, and moving port 2's egress leaves B referencing a port that no longer exits there. That is not a defect --- it is `boundaryEdgeOutcomes`' existing **carry / dissolve / defer / error** verdict --- and it reads correctly: *you cannot haul in a rope that is tied to a tree in the next room without untying it.* **The clean case is exactly the presence-only case.**

**What it does to [H1](#h1-single-host-objects-hosting-at-the-containing-node) clause 5: leaves it nothing to do.** Under H1 coiling migrates the *whole* down from the Area into a room, with the cascade and plan-time pre-locking clause 4 demands. Under multi-host + ports **there is no whole-migration** --- the whole is present wherever its ports say, and coiling shrinks the port set. Clause 5's queued concurrent-migration and thrash cases target machinery that would not exist. **Third time a queued test set has dissolved rather than run.**

**Two rows it clears outright.**

- **AB-15's naming seam** --- flagged as the one step of [part-mediated reference](#part-mediated-reference-how-h1-answers-its-own-replacement-trigger) that does not fall out of existing machinery. Here the room's node **is** `OBJECT#ROPE`: the rope is present *by name* in every room it passes through, with the ports carrying the where-along. No catalog widening, no aliasing, no two-phase match.
- **AB-17's hosting criterion** --- "all parts co-located" becomes "all ports share an egress host," a read off the egress list rather than a judgment. The *reabsorption* criterion still needs the separately-referenced test: `nodeHasRelationalEdge`, **plus** "does any port carry an exterior edge."

**The membership rule it implies:** an object is present in a room **iff at least one of its ports egresses there**. That is AB-6's presence half answered concretely. **Note what the rule is *not*, after the ingress-list reversal:** it is a **consistency invariant** between the egress list and the room's membership node --- **not** a derivation any reader performs, since scanning every object to answer "what is here" would be absurd. Membership stays in `positionGraph`; only the port-level detail is derived. See **PQ-13** for what uniformity implies on plain objects.

**The rule it needs and does not state:** port 3 goes C -> B rather than C -> A because the rope is continuous and its far end travels the path. So **relocation follows adjacency** --- and nothing in the scheme says so. **PQ-12.**

**Annotations.** Write-side --- **homogeneous extent** (AB-16) --- neither Improvisation nor Recognition; this is *reabsorption-adjacent lifecycle*, which no AB-14 trigger covers --- mechanism --- exercises no improvisation gap, and that absence is itself the finding.

**Rows grounded.** AB-6 (presence derivation), AB-15 (naming seam dissolved), AB-17 (criterion (i) mechanized), AB-26 (adjacency on the write side), H1 clause 5 (nothing to do), AB-8.

## Discussion record

Reasoning trail behind the Open-decisions rows, kept for provenance. Not needed to know what to do next --- only *why* the questions are shaped the way they are, and what a future reader would otherwise have to re-derive. Following the pattern of [`AGENT.backtrackChannel.planning.md`](../actions/AGENT.backtrackChannel.planning.md)'s History section.

**Seeded 2026-08-05 from conversation.** The initiative began from a scalability observation: the current treatment of objects is a fine starting point, but complex manipulation on top of it would become unworkably burdensome to reason about --- for an LLM in particular. The proposed answer was aggregations reasoned about in summary, at levels both above and below the human-convenience scale of objects. Four seed examples were given (place setting, dismantled flashlight, powered door, rope through several rooms) and are recorded above.

**The examples were immediately suspected of not being one structure.** Three axes were separated on first pass --- composition, functional aggregate carrying state, multi-host extent --- and AB-1 exists because collapsing them prematurely, or keeping them separate unnecessarily, are equally live failure modes. Notably, the place setting and the flashlight are **mirror images of one relation**, differing only in which level pre-existed and which was improvised: the place setting's members are primary and the whole is minted, the flashlight's whole is primary and the parts are minted.

**A factual correction, 2026-08-05.** An earlier draft asserted that multi-host presence "breaks the at-most-one-host invariant" and that `repairObjectPlacementDrift` "exists to eliminate it." Both were overstated; the verified position is now in [Multi-host presence](#multi-host-presence-what-is-actually-true-today). The correction materially changed AB-8: framing (a) is far cheaper than it first appeared, so extent's cost is dominated by the modelling choice rather than by any storage obstacle.

**The granularity insight, 2026-08-05 --- the pivot the rest of the design turns on.** A rope coiled in a backpack is a plain object: no sub-part is separately addressable, and pulling it from the pack yields the whole coil by default. Lowered into an abyss, room-level atomicity breaks down and the ends become distinct loci. **The rope did not change; the useful granularity did.** That yields a candidate criterion --- decompose when parts become independently addressable or independently positioned --- which runs *symmetrically* in both directions: the place setting aggregates because its members stopped being separately interesting, the rope decomposes because its parts started being. Evidence that aggregation and decomposition are one axis rather than two mechanisms.

**Consequences drawn the same day.** (i) The transition must run backwards --- re-coiling the rope must restore one object --- which the flashlight example hides and which makes reabsorption mandatory rather than optional (AB-17). (ii) Rope parts are unlike flashlight parts: homogeneous slices of a continuum versus heterogeneous separable things (AB-16). This weakened an earlier claim in this plan that AB-8's segment framing cheaply collapses into AB-4. **[Corrected later the same day: the "ordered along a path, load-bearing for `pull`" half of this was wrong --- see the AB-26 entry below.]** (iii) Tying the rope to a rock puts a relation on an *end*, not the whole --- which under a single multi-room node leaves the relational step's host ambiguous, and under segments is cleanly same-host. Evidence for segments, arrived at from the relational side rather than the positional one.

**The reframe, 2026-08-05 --- AB-12.** Asking *which* things can be promoted led to the observation that `<Object>`, `<Room>`, and `<Feature>` are **already abstractions**: a battery is an object, so is the flashlight it is part of, so is the casing that is part of that. There is no natural floor or ceiling, so pinning `<Object>` as a privileged origin with `<Part>` below and `<Abstraction>` above is not philosophically sound --- it would build two hierarchies for one relation. **The question may therefore not be "what is this strange `Abstraction` type" but "how do we mint and reabsorb Objects at different levels of abstraction, as they are needed."** Recorded as a candidate, deliberately not adopted: deciding is premature and there is useful discussion left.

Two objections were raised against the reframe and are carried as their own rows rather than resolved. **AB-13:** "levels" invites a scalar, but composition has overlap and multiple parents, so the shape is a partial order, not a ladder. **AB-12's own second half:** Room is a *host*, not a node, so extending the reframe to Room and Feature unifies hosts with nodes --- AB-2's most radical answer --- and contradicts the documented decision that Features are deliberately narrower participants. It would, in exchange, *explain* that narrowing rather than assert it (a part cannot be membership-moved away from its whole without an act of decomposition). Object and Room/Feature are to be weighed as separable bets.

**Two further candidate answers recorded, not adopted.** *Minting triggers (AB-14):* operational necessity and attention are unlike needs, and keeping them separate --- operations mint durably, attention selects a projection level and mints nothing --- would prevent description-time LLM inference from becoming a write path. *Authoring (AB-15):* if no level is ontologically privileged, an authored `<Object>` is plausibly the **default binding level** for unqualified reference, revisable by circumstance, which preserves a real role for authoring without pinning the ontology.

**AB-14 reshaped, 2026-08-05 --- Improvisation and Recognition.** The row's first framing (operational necessity vs. attention) was superseded on the observation that **attention is not a minting trigger at all** --- it was a projection concern in a trigger's clothing, and the row therefore described one minting operation plus a note about description. The replacement pair, motivated by the necessity of narration and the structure of the knowledge-representation system, is two genuine operations, both write-side and both consequences of player action: **Improvisation** invents detail an in-flight operation requires but nobody has authored (the flashlight's interior under a screwdriver), and **Recognition** notices that a player's arrangement has composed into something above the level they were working at (the snare trap). The displaced guardrail --- description selects among existing levels and never creates one --- moved to AB-9, where it belongs and where it is more legible as scope control.

The narration obligations were what distinguished the two most sharply. Improvisation's narration **is** the minting event: the battery and the corroded bulb come into existence for the player by being described. Recognition's narration is a **contract with the player**: it is how they learn the world has accepted their construction as meaningful, and without it a player cannot tell whether a clever arrangement counts for anything and will re-attempt it looking for magic phrasing. This is the argument that Recognition can never be a silent internal summarization pass, and it is why AB-19 (description vs. mechanism) is a real fork rather than a shading.

**Two asymmetries recorded the same day, both loading onto Recognition** (AB-18): Improvisation is *bounded by its operation* while Recognition is an unbounded search over arrangements possibly separated in time; and a wrong Improvisation is cheap (plausible detail for a player who asked for detail) while a wrong Recognition changes what exists and what is subsequently legal. The candidate that would resolve much of both at once --- restricting recognition-for-mechanism to an **authored pattern vocabulary** --- is recorded on AB-19, along with its cost: players could then only construct what someone anticipated.

**AB-14 refined again, 2026-08-05 --- the pair was marking a real distinction but naming it wrongly.** The *action* is improvisation in **both** cases: a sufficiently confident recognition of cups, plates and flatware mints a `Table Setting` component of which the others become parts, which is the same act as minting a north wall. What actually differs is **when the mint must happen, and that is downstream of whether a pending operation is blocked on it.** "Lean the table against the north wall" cannot resolve until the referent grounds --- synchronous, on the command's critical path. A loop of rope laid on a road may be understood as a snare immediately, thirty seconds later, or never, with no material loss of responsiveness. **Same persistence layer, potentially very different decision processes** --- which the previous session's finding independently supports, since `ASSET#IMPROVISATION` is a merge layer and therefore renders provenance invisible downstream.

**Timing is the symptom; criticality is the axis.** Preferring the second framing is not pedantry --- it predicts three things the timing framing does not, each now carried as its own row. **(AB-20, failure semantics):** an on-demand mint is a correctness obligation whose failure fails the command, while recognition is discretionary enrichment whose failure owes nobody anything --- and both disciplines already ship side by side in the objects lane. **(AB-20, authorization):** on-demand minting needs no confidence gate because *the player's reference licenses the existence*, whereas recognition volunteers an unprompted claim --- so AB-18's false-positive cost follows from authorization rather than being an arbitrary policy asymmetry. **(AB-18, convergence):** the mode is a property of the moment, not the thing --- "check the snare" puts recognition on the critical path, so one mint is reachable from both a blocking and a deferred path and the two must converge.

**AB-19 reframed, 2026-08-05 --- the abstraction bounds the LLM step, it does not replace it.** The row's earlier proposal (recognition-for-mechanism made tractable by an **authored pattern vocabulary with behavior attached**) is rejected on the grounds that it compiles fictional physics into a deterministic structural context. That is the wrong tool --- we are not doing physics simulation, and it is not what the fiction is about anyway --- and deriving a snare's result from first principles on every interaction is a fool's errand regardless. **Most player-assembled contraptions will require an LLM step to reason about consequences; the design goal is to give that step the right information so it is bounded and manageable.**

Description and mechanism therefore separate by **stakes, not machinery**: recognizing a place setting is a pleasant first pass over cogitation with no causally complicated consequences, and stays pure projection under AB-9's guardrail; recognizing a snare *functionally*, and reasoning about its function in the abstract, is **essential**. This is a cleaner allocation than the previous framing, and it lets AB-9's no-creation rule stand rather than straining it --- description never mints, mechanism mints because something must reason over the result.

**Coyote named as the existing first-iteration attempt at exactly this** --- flawed and ad hoc by its own author's description, and worth reading before designing. The generalizable core (annotate at mint time; closed structural vocabulary with open narrative content; graded aptness; multi-hop structured parse; internal-only reasoning handoff) and the specific ad-hoc parts (no assembly-level object at all, room-scoped rather than contraption-scoped context, boundedness that comes from a tiny world and a closed genre rather than from structure) are written up in [Coyote as prior art](#coyote-as-prior-art-bounded-llm-reasoning-that-is-not-bounded-structurally). Two rows came out of it: **AB-19** re-aimed at "what must an abstraction carry to bound an LLM step," and **AB-22** on annotation timing and vocabulary --- including the observation that member affinities are a cheap filter for *whether an arrangement is worth recognizing at all*, which attacks AB-18's unbounded search without needing a pattern library.

**Scheduling substrate and situational meaning, 2026-08-05 (two findings from one exchange).** First, `mtw.ephemera.thinking` was named as the intended home for deferred recognition --- its future possibility of work "queued for consideration as time allows, but foregrounded by necessity if the results suddenly become needed" is exactly what tier 2 requires. Verification found the **persistence and vocabulary shipped and the execution model absent** (no `claimed` producer, no claimer, every job bootstrapped and completed in one synchronous invocation), plus one free win: `Meta::Result`'s `nonCollidingPutItem` first-write-wins already satisfies AB-18's convergence requirement. Detail: [`thinking` as the scheduling substrate](#thinking-as-the-scheduling-substrate-shipped-ledger-unbuilt-queue).

Second, and more consequential: **AB-22's vocabulary question was posed statically and should not have been.** "Is the vocabulary general, or authored per world?" has no good answer because relevant meaning is **situational and varies within a world** --- ambushed by goblins, meaning is combat-relative; dining at the Faerie Court, the place setting's causal complexity becomes very real. Coyote shortcuts this legitimately, as an early iteration should, because its genre supplies a constant interpretive context. Two consequences: mint-time annotation can only be a partial answer (durable base plus situational overlay), and the member-affinity *filter* is itself context-dependent. Recorded as **AB-23**, with **AB-24** for the proposed low-immediacy scheduled task that watches for frame shifts and parameterizes recognition. Note the second deliberately-deferred axis this conversation has landed on: the render cache's `n` / mark-state axis is **built and unpopulated** (`markState` pinned to `[]`, `SITUATION#DEFAULT` only, per iteration 10), and situational *meaning* is that same axis raised from rendering to interpretation.

**Part connections corrected, 2026-08-05 --- adjacency-mediated, not sequenced (AB-26, AB-27).** This plan had twice claimed extent parts are "ordered along a path." Wrong, and wrong in a way already diagnosed one layer up: it imposes a **scalar where a graph belongs**, the same error AB-13 records for levels. A sequence only looks right because a rope is linear; a net spanning four rooms, wiring from a generator to a powered door, and two ropes knotted together all branch. The correct shape is *"the clifftop rope end connects to the shaft section **by way of** the 'into the abyss' exit"* --- a graph whose edges are labeled by the spatial relation that licenses them. Branching is then free, the edge **explains** the adjacency instead of asserting it, narration takes direction and wording from the exit (as the shipped move compiler already does with `exitName`), and `pull` becomes graph propagation rather than an index walk. Area edge kinds beyond Exit were **already** documented as able to carry non-traversable spatial facts ("north of" without a door), which is exactly what a rope through a floor-hole needs --- a third anticipated axis this design lands on.

Two rules proposed with it: connections are **explicit but constrained** (adjacency *licenses* an edge, never derives one --- two ropes in adjacent rooms are not connected), and an edge must reference **world truth** rather than `projectRoomExits`' perspective-filtered output, or a rope's physical shape would vary by observer. **AB-27** then notes the encouraging corollary: parts sharing a host can use the *existing* intra-host relational vocabulary, so the new edge kind is needed **exactly and only where `applyStepSequenceCore` already throws** --- a sign of a real joint rather than a parallel mechanism.

**Rule (ii) corrected within the hour, and the correction matters more than the fix.** It was first written as "the **authored Exit** edge," which collapsed **three** independent things into one: *world truth* (the real constraint), *Exit* (one kind of spatial relation), and *authored* (one provenance). The licensing relation may be **any kind** --- an Exit edge hard-coding a connection affordance, **or** an adjacency/nesting relation, such as going over the rail of a boat and ending up in the bay --- and **any provenance**, authored **or improvised**, since an improvised relation is world truth the moment it is minted. Only the *projection* is excluded.

Three consequences followed. **(a)** The boat case is the deferred containment operator arriving: `In`/`inside` was explicitly excluded from relational v1 (BD-2) and parked for "a future nested container operator," and a rope over the rail needs exactly that relation to license it. **(b)** Nesting does **double duty** --- it structures the containment tree *and* licenses a boundary-crossing connection --- so AB-25 and AB-26 share a substrate, though not identically, since an Exit gives adjacency with no containment either way. **(c)** Improvised provenance has a concrete gap: an improvised area-level adjacency needs an **ephemera-side** Area graph, which does not exist because `EphemeraAreaId` does not exist --- the same missing piece P1 requires, so one gap with two consumers. Once it exists, mixing provenances is cheap, because participation-stack merge already does exactly that. And **AB-28** captures the failure mode neither framing had named: licensing relations are **mutable**, so a repaired floor-hole strands the rope threaded through it --- probably content rather than drift.

**AB-28 recognized as an instance of a shipped pattern, 2026-08-05 --- and it deflates from "new problem" to "existing discipline, extended."** The mutable-licence question is the same shape as the boundary-edge rule already in production: *a mutation that would sever a relationship is not legal until the severing is made explicit.* Assert-and-throw with no silent-strip fallback, `boundaryEdgeOutcomes` classification, expansion-classifies-compiler-sequences, dissolves ordered ahead of the mutation. The three outcomes guessed for the rope-through-a-sealed-hole case turned out to **be** the existing verdict channels --- `error`, `dissolve`, `defer` --- rather than needing new ones. A convergence worth noting: `defer` already exists as the verdict meaning *needs LLM judgment*, which is exactly the bounded LLM step AB-19 argues contraption consequences require. The classification vocabulary anticipated it.

**AB-29 carries what does not carry over**, and its bifurcation is the substantive finding: **play-driven** licence removal has an operation to block or transform (so the discipline applies and the conflict is content), while an **asset-withdrawn** licence has no operation to make illegal at all --- nobody did anything --- which is the **eviction ladder's** exact situation one layer over, and wants trim-and-repair rather than legality. **First falsification case run, 2026-08-05 --- and it worked, which is the meta-finding.** [C5, the chain mounted at both ends](#c5-the-chain-mounted-at-both-ends), was raised in conversation as a substitute for rope state (iv) on the grounds that re-coiling changes co-location and load-bearing **simultaneously** and therefore cannot distinguish them. Worth recording in that shape: the failure was **collapsing a disjunction**, which is a fourth entry in this plan's own error catalogue alongside scalar-where-a-graph-belongs (AB-13, AB-26) and instance-mistaken-for-category (AB-12, AB-26's rule (ii)).

**Trigger 2's replacement run the same day, 2026-08-05 --- part-mediated reference, and it passed conditionally.** The proposal: in any room where "pull the rope" makes sense a **part** of the rope is present, that part takes the identification-grounding because it is what is locally there, it carries a **parent index** to the whole, and the whole then accumulates its parts' state across rooms to evaluate the pull. **Better than the alternative rather than merely adequate:** the other way to keep the whole referrable was to surface Area-hosted objects in room affordances, which would make compose reach up the containment tree and pull non-present things into every catalog. **PQ-1 settled 2026-08-06, and the answer avoided the trade the question appeared to force.** I had framed the fork as *stored once in the whole* (cheap writes, expensive room reads) versus *split halves* (the `repairObjectPlacementDrift` drift class). **The proposal is a third thing: split into halves holding *complementary* facts** --- an **egress list** on the interior graph (port -> outbound host) and an **ingress list** of port numbers on the exterior node. Neither half restates the other, so the hot read stays local: a room answers *what is here and how is it connected* without opening the object, and opening it is required only to learn *which part* a port lands on. **Detail costs a hop; presence and attachment do not** --- position 2 with the zero-hop case actually zero.

**Two defaults came with it, and the first is the stronger half of the whole port design.** *Failed addressing coarsens rather than dangles:* an edge to `OBJECT#BAG:4` with no legal port 4 resolves identically to an edge to `OBJECT#BAG`, per-hop. **That is a different answer to PQ-6/PQ-7 than an index gives** --- an index *prevents* dangling references, coarsening makes them *benign*, and having both is why the residual risk is now small. *Second:* a port with no exterior edge is a pure presence indicator, which is better than the optional-endpoint field I had proposed because **the absence of an edge is the signal** --- no second record shape.

**Resolved: the interior egress list is authoritative, and the ingress list is accepted as a denormalization inside truth that can drift.** Authority follows from what the halves are for --- the egress list must be complete for plan-time footprint computation, and it is the side whose lifecycle invalidates ports. The ingress list is fully derivable, so it is a materialized view living in `positionGraph` rather than in cache; **that was chosen over putting it in `positionCache` because the room's local read is too hot to depend on cache warmth**, and the precedent is direct (`Meta` placement is already a dual write with "`positionGraph` wins" and a repair sweep).

**Accepting drift creates an obligation, tracked rather than waved at: PQ-11.** The conflict rule follows from authority; the **repair story is open and nothing does this today.** One asymmetry worth exploiting: the ingress list is derivable from the egress lists of objects present, so repair is a **recompute rather than a merge** --- materially easier than `repairObjectPlacementDrift`'s. **And AB-35's scope needed clarifying**: its never-a-second-truth rule was written for `positionCache`, while this is derived data placed deliberately *inside* `positionGraph`. Not a violation, but the plan now has two derived-data sites under different rules, so the general principle was stated --- *derived data may live in truth when the read path demands it, provided a conflict rule and a repair story both exist.*

**One inference blocked before it could be drawn.** The coarsening default is **read-side only** and does not license relaxing BD-33/35's write-side assert-and-throw. Robust reads make the write check *feel* unnecessary; they do not make it so, because a silently-coarsening world accumulates imprecision nobody notices --- the silent-wrong-answer class this plan keeps choosing against. Smaller instance of the same: coarsening turns "pull this strap" into "pull the whole bag," correctly most of the time and silently always.

**C9 --- coiling the rope, 2026-08-06.** The rope's fourth state, owed since Phase 0 was written, and the first corpus entry that is a **worked operation** rather than a falsification case. Its result is what it does *not* require: hauling a three-room rope in rewrites port egress and touches **no edges at all**. The `part` links and sibling links are invariant. **Structure and position are independent**, which today's model cannot express --- relocating a segment means moving a node between graphs, and same-host-only relational edges force dissolve-and-recreate for any segment carrying a relation.

**The scope of "no edge changes" is the presence-only case, and the fiction endorses the limit.** A span tied to a tree in the room being left leaves that room referencing a port that no longer exits there --- which is `boundaryEdgeOutcomes`' existing carry / dissolve / defer / error verdict, and reads exactly right: *you cannot haul in a rope tied to a tree next door without untying it.* **Machinery and fiction agreeing on where the hard case is is the strongest signal available that a joint is real.**

**Clause 5 has nothing left to do, if multi-host + ports wins.** No whole-migration occurs, because the whole is present wherever its ports say. The queued concurrent-migration and thrash cases would target machinery that does not exist --- **the third queued test set to dissolve rather than run**, which is now frequent enough to be worth naming as a pattern: *this design keeps answering questions by removing the thing that raised them.* Encouraging, and also a reason to check that the removed thing was not load-bearing elsewhere.

**Two rows cleared, and the second needs its credit stated carefully.** **AB-17**'s hosting criterion becomes a one-line read --- "all ports share an egress host" --- and its reabsorption criterion gains a second mechanical clause (*does any port carry an exterior edge*). **AB-15's naming seam dissolves outright:** the room's node **is** `OBJECT#ROPE`, so the rope is present by name everywhere it passes and none of that row's three candidates is needed. **But the seam was created by single-hosting the whole**, so its dissolution is evidence about clause 1b, **not** about AB-15's own question, which is untouched. Recording that distinction matters more than the win --- a row whose *instance* dissolves is not a row that has been answered.

**Two questions the case raised that the scheme does not state. PQ-12:** the far end goes `C -> B` rather than `C -> A` because the rope is continuous, so **relocation follows adjacency** --- and if that is an invariant, AB-26's adjacency licensing appears on the **write** side, restricting where a part may move rather than merely licensing a connection. That is a stronger use of the same relation and should not be assumed to follow from the read-side version. **PQ-13:** the implied membership rule (*present iff a port egresses here*) asks whether a plain lantern carries a presence port. Uniformity makes a simple object the **degenerate case rather than a special case** --- one port, zero hops, the same argument that rescued position 2 --- at the price of ceremony on the commonest object in the game, on the hottest read path.

**PQ-1 reversed 2026-08-06, one exchange after it was settled --- the ingress list belongs in `positionCache`, not in `positionGraph`.** The settled answer had rejected the cache branch because "the room's local read is too hot to depend on cache warmth." **That defended a need the room does not have:** its own edges already carry what a coarse read wants, since `boulder -TiedTo-> ROPE:4` names the rope and "a rope is tied to the boulder" renders without any ingress list. The list's unique contribution is *which* ports are live locally, presence-only ones included --- a where-along fact, and **detail costing a hop was the whole deal.** The thing put into truth to protect the hot path was not on it.

**The general form, which is the durable part: judge a denormalization by what it saves.** The ingress list is **weak** --- per record it surfaces information from **one** item, saving one read of an object already known present (**1:1**). Membership is **strong** --- it saves scanning every object in the world (**N:1**). Strong belongs in truth; weak belongs in a read-heavy index. **That line also fixes what would otherwise have been a bad inference from C9's rule:** *present iff a port egresses here* is a **consistency invariant** between the egress list and membership, **not** a derivation a reader performs --- if membership were derived too, answering "what is here" would mean scanning the world.

**And there is a principled reading rather than merely a tidier one: ingress-in-truth was position 3 growing back.** Materialising a lookup answer into truth so the common case need not traverse, while the uncommon case traverses, is *two rules where one would do*. That is the shape this plan already caught itself in once by accident --- **second occurrence of the same instinct producing the same defect**, which is worth more than either instance.

**Three consequences, one of them a whole question removed.** **PQ-11 is retired the day it was opened** --- no denormalization in truth means no drift and no repair story owed; the one observation worth keeping (a derived list is repaired by **recompute, not merge**) moved to AB-35 as a property to design its contract around. **AB-35 goes back to one derived-data site with one clean rule**, reversing the two-sites note added hours earlier, and gains the ingress list as its first named consumer. **PQ-13's cost objection loses its hot-path half** --- with ports living only in the object's own graph, a plain lantern's presence port costs a room read nothing, so what survives is storage and write-path ceremony, not read cost.

**One refinement on "literally one place," because it locates the transaction footprint.** Relocation *within* the spread is **one record** --- rewrite the port's egress host. Crossing a boundary is **two**, since a room's port count going 0 -> 1 or 1 -> 0 changes membership, which stays in `positionGraph`. Hauling a rope through the middle of its own span is nearly free; only its endpoints cost.

**The reversal also removed the ugliest corner of the branch it replaced.** With the ingress list in truth, a stale entry was a **phantom** --- a port listed on a node the object no longer exits to --- and the coarsening default said nothing about that direction. In cache it cannot happen, and coarsening covers the remaining case (a room's graph cannot tell whether `ROPE:4` is live, so it resolves to `ROPE`). **The two decisions now support each other, which they did not before.**

**Edge kinds across a port need not match --- 2026-08-06, and it reframes what a port is.** The proposition that they should was an **artifact of reasoning from a rope**: `TiedTo` outside and `TiedTo` inside agree only because homogeneous extent makes a part the same kind of thing as its whole, so the port looks like an identity function relaying one relation inward. **A heterogeneous case breaks it at once** --- a power cord `ThreadsInto` a flashlight from the room's side, while inside, port 1 is `SolderedTo` the battery case. Both true, neither a copy. **So a port is a scale boundary at which the description changes, not a relay.**

**That withdraws the "kind lives inside only" leaning, and for the same reason the ingress-list question resolved: the facts are complementary, not redundant.** With legitimately different kinds there is no shared claim to disagree about, so each side stores its own and the two-writer worry never arises. No new mechanism is needed --- the exterior edge already carries `HostRelationalEdgeKind`.

**And coarsening turns out to be lossless at the level being read, which is stronger than the tolerance it was granted.** `PowerCord -ThreadsInto-> FLASHLIGHT:1` coarsens to `PowerCord -ThreadsInto-> FLASHLIGHT` and still reads *"a power cord threads into the flashlight."* **Because the exterior kind was never about the interior part**, coarsening drops only the interior fact --- the detail, by construction. Under the matching-kinds assumption coarsening looked like graceful degradation; under heterogeneous kinds it is not degradation at all.

**No compatibility matrix, and the argument is AB-19's rather than a new one.** Policing whether *threads into* may pair with *resting on* is the compile-fictional-physics-into-deterministic-structure move that row already rejected, at a smaller scale. **Recorded on AB-19 as a use of its rejection in a place it was not written for** --- worth flagging because the next few applications will also look like small, reasonable validation tables.

**Two rules added.** *The port is the unit of lifecycle, not either edge* --- independent kinds let the halves diverge under dissolution (a port soldered to a battery case but exiting nowhere), so releasing the port takes both. And *do not "optimize" the rope*: someone will read rope data, see the same kind twice, and remove the "redundancy." **It is coincidentally equal.** Build nothing that assumes matching and nothing that assumes differing.

**Answered 2026-08-05, and it was the question that dissolved:** whether born-spanning things (a river, a cable run) mean changeable granularity is a property some things have rather than a lifecycle everything passes through. **It is a property some things have, and that turns out to cost nothing** --- under H1 a river is simply an extent whose parts never co-locate, so the hosting rule gives the same answer forever and no lifecycle is required. The question looked significant only while "born spanning" was being treated as a category; once the wall was separated out (AB-30), the remainder was ordinary. Kept here rather than deleted because the *shape* of the mistake is the reusable part.

**AB-16's strongest argument withdrawn as circular, 2026-08-06.** The row claimed containment and extent *disagree about what is present* --- a claim it called "the most load-bearing property the position model has." Challenged and it did not hold. The containment half was stated against the shipped host-bound model; the extent half against the [drop-clause-1b candidate](#candidate-drop-clause-1b-and-the-whole-chain-with-it), explicitly not adopted --- so the two halves were never in the same frame. Worse, [C8](#c8-the-flashlight-that-vanished-when-someone-looked-at-it) is what scoped that candidate to extent-only in the first place, and the row then cited the resulting asymmetry as evidence *from C8*. **The finding was a consequence of a scoping decision made two paragraphs earlier.** An earlier hedge ("a second premise to test") had already sensed something wrong but mis-diagnosed it as a conditional on AB-34, which preserved the claim instead of retiring it.

**The failure mode is new and worth naming, because the three before it were a different shape.** Those were costs mis-assigned to a proposal that in fact belonged to its comparator --- an accounting error. This one is *evidence generated by my own scoping decision and read back as a finding*. It is harder to catch because the row cited a **corpus case**, and corpus cases are the part of this plan that is supposed to be grounded. **The check it implies:** when a corpus case is cited as evidence for a distinction, verify the case was not itself *shaped* by that distinction. C7 and C8 were both written after AB-16 existed.

**What survives is a cardinality reading** --- extent wholes have needed to span hosts, containment wholes so far have not --- and it is under two live challenges recorded on the row: whether it reflects anything underlying rather than the sample of examples considered, and whether it would fork any code even if true.

**AB-16 retired 2026-08-06, the same day its strongest argument was withdrawn.** Once the positional claim went, the row was examined generally and **nothing survived in the position model.** The **cardinality** reading (extent spans hosts, containment does not) turned out to be a size confound --- every extent example chosen was long, every containment example hand-sized --- and it is refuted from inside this plan: the **coiled rope in a backpack**, the granularity insight the whole design turns on, is extent that does not span. A **wagon in a doorway with a barrel in its bed** closes the converse. Spanning-ness and containment are independent axes; "both" is the ordinary case, not the edge case.

**The identity-scheme residue was a third error of the same day, and a new kind.** I claimed extent parts are identified by *where* (`ROPE:1`) and containment parts by *what* (`BATTERY`) --- **reading a port number as the name of the part behind it.** The plan's own two worked examples were already symmetric (`OBJECT#RopeEnd1`, `OBJECT#BatteryCase`); I wrote them that way and then read an asymmetry into them. **This is the second time ports have been misread**, after the reusable-interface reading corrected earlier the same day, and both propagated into assessments first. The durable fix is a negative statement the section lacked: [What a port number is not](#what-a-port-number-is-not-added-2026-08-06). A construct that has produced two errors of the same family needs its boundaries written down, not just its purpose.

**What retirement cost, which is less than it looks.** AB-16 appears in eight corpus annotation lines and three interaction rows, and none of them lose anything: the tags stay as *description*, and the interactions were carrying claims the tag was never entitled to make. **AB-16 -> AB-8, AB-11** was already void on independent grounds (AB-26 killed its ordering premise), so retirement only finished a demolition already underway --- weak evidence the row had been decorative for a while.

**The one real survivor left the document.** Separability semantics --- cutting a rope makes two ropes, pulling a battery does not make two flashlights --- is a fact about what a *divide* operation yields, so it is manipulation vocabulary. Handed to the object-manipulation ladder's [Deferred, not rung-sized](../actions/AGENT.objectManipulationIterations.planning.md#deferred-not-rung-sized) list as **future planning gated on this initiative**, per the user's framing: once the abstraction fractal is designed it should be clear *how* separation and merger can happen, and only then is there a planning process worth designing. Recorded there rather than here because this plan should not hold work it will never do.
